# Push Week — Combined Stress Test

This file exists for one reason: you asked to be pushed to the limit, and
timed single-section drills in isolation don't do that — real exam days
do, because fatigue compounds across hours, not across one 20-minute
drill. This is a single sitting, back-to-back, phone away, no pausing
between sections except the exact breaks listed. Do not start this until
you have logged at least one baseline score per exam in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md` — running this cold, with no baseline to compare
against, tells you "it was hard," which you already know, instead of
"here specifically is where fatigue cost you points," which is the only
useful output of this exercise.

## Before you start

- Block 3 hours, uninterrupted, same room you'd realistically sit a real
  test in (desk, not bed; no music).
- Print or open `GRE/verbal_vocab_set2.md`, `GRE/quant_drills_set2.md`,
  `IELTS/writing_task2_set2.md`, and `TOEFL/writing_tasks.md` — you will
  move between them with no re-reading of instructions mid-session.
- Have a real timer (phone timer is fine; phone itself goes in another
  room once the timer's set).

## The session (total ~2h50m working time + 20m breaks)

| Order | Task | Source | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GRE Verbal Set 2 — word→meaning, meaning→word, full 30 words | `GRE/verbal_vocab_set2.md` | 15 min |
| 2 | GRE Quant Set 2, all 12 questions | `GRE/quant_drills_set2.md` | 18 min |
| — | **Break** | | 5 min |
| 3 | IELTS Writing Task 2, Set 2, one prompt, cold (don't reread the prompt bank first — open to any one prompt and go) | `IELTS/writing_task2_set2.md` | 35 min |
| 4 | IELTS Speaking: 3 cue cards back to back, recorded, no retakes | `IELTS/speaking_cue_cards.md` | 15 min |
| — | **Break** | | 10 min |
| 5 | TOEFL Independent Writing | `TOEFL/writing_tasks.md` | 30 min |
| 6 | TOEFL Integrated Writing (read+listen+write) | `TOEFL/writing_tasks.md` | 20 min |
| — | **Break** | | 5 min |
| 7 | TOEFL Reading passage, full timed | `TOEFL/reading_practice_01.md` | 18 min |
| 8 | GRE Reading Comprehension passage, full timed, done LAST (deliberately — this is where fatigue shows up most) | `GRE/reading_comprehension_practice.md` | 10 min |

Do not skip the order. Task 8 landing last, after ~2.5 hours of
output, is the point — your real exam day will stack a weak section at
the end of your energy curve too, and you need to know what that does to
you specifically, not in theory.

## Immediately after (do not delay — do this before you eat, shower, or
check your phone)

1. Self-grade every section using its own rubric, honestly, while it's
   fresh.
2. For the GRE Reading Comprehension passage (task 8): compare your
   accuracy and pacing against how you'd expect to perform on that same
   passage type fresh (first thing in a session). If accuracy dropped or
   you ran over time, that gap is your fatigue tax — write the number
   down.
3. Log every section's score/band AND the time taken in
   `PROGRESS_LOG.md`'s weekly mock table, with "Push Week" as the
   session label so it's identifiable later.
4. Write one sentence: "the section that fell apart hardest under
   fatigue was ___, specifically because ___." Not "I got tired" — what
   specifically degraded (slower reading, sloppier grammar, guessing
   instead of eliminating, losing track of the question).

## Cadence

Run this once every 2 weeks, not more often — this is a stress test, not
a daily drill, and running it too frequently just trains you to survive
this specific sequence rather than generalizing to real exam-day fatigue.
The STUDY_PLAN.md weekly mocks are your regular cadence; this supplements
them in Weeks 5 onward, and again as a dress rehearsal in Week 9.
