# TOEFL Writing — Drill Set

## Task 1: Integrated Writing (20 minutes, 150–225 words)

**Protocol:** Read the passage (3 min), then read the "lecture transcript"
exactly once as if you heard it (you don't get to re-listen on the real
test, so don't re-read it more than once here either), then write for the
remaining time. Your essay must explain how the lecture **challenges or
casts doubt on** specific points in the reading — a summary of both
without connecting them scores low regardless of grammar.

---

### Set 1

**Reading passage:**

> In recent years, several companies have proposed replacing human-staffed
> customer service call centers with AI voice agents. Proponents argue
> this shift offers three major advantages. First, AI agents are available
> twenty-four hours a day without the costs associated with night-shift
> staffing or overtime pay. Second, AI systems can instantly access a
> company's entire knowledge base, theoretically providing more accurate
> and consistent information than a human agent who may not recall every
> policy detail. Third, AI agents can handle a theoretically unlimited
> number of simultaneous calls, eliminating the hold times that frustrate
> customers during peak periods.

**Lecture transcript (simulated professor, read once):**

> "It sounds great on paper, but let's look at what's actually happened
> where companies have tried this at scale. First, the 24-hour
> availability argument ignores that most customer complaints requiring
> calls happen during business hours anyway — the overnight cost savings
> turn out to be minimal in practice, while customers calling with urgent
> problems at 2 a.m. often need judgment calls an AI can't make safely.
> Second, 'accurate and consistent' assumes the knowledge base itself is
> accurate — in several documented cases, AI agents confidently gave
> customers outdated policy information because the company's internal
> documentation hadn't been updated, and unlike a human, the AI expressed
> no uncertainty about it. Third, while AI can handle more simultaneous
> calls, satisfaction surveys show customers abandon AI interactions at
> much higher rates specifically when their issue is unusual or emotionally
> charged — exactly the calls that most need to get through, not the
> routine ones."

**Your task:** Summarize the lecture's points and explain how they cast
doubt on specific claims made in the reading passage.

### Set 2

**Reading passage:**

> A popular theory in urban planning holds that building more highways
> through congested cities will reduce traffic by giving drivers more
> road capacity. Advocates point to three supporting arguments. First,
> additional lanes directly increase the volume of vehicles a road can
> carry at any given time. Second, new highways can redirect traffic away
> from overloaded surface streets, reducing congestion in residential
> neighborhoods. Third, improved highway access is associated with
> increased economic activity, as goods and workers can move more
> efficiently between regions.

**Lecture transcript:**

> "This is actually one of the most extensively studied — and most
> consistently contradicted — ideas in transportation research, a pattern
> called induced demand. First, yes, more lanes add capacity initially,
> but studies going back decades, including a well-known analysis of U.S.
> metropolitan areas, show that within a few years traffic volume rises to
> fill the new capacity almost exactly, because easier driving conditions
> encourage more trips, longer commutes, and sprawl — congestion returns to
> roughly the same level. Second, the claim about redirecting traffic from
> residential streets overlooks that new highways often increase total
> trips originating from those neighborhoods, since residents now find
> longer commutes more tolerable — so local congestion doesn't necessarily
> fall. Third, the economic activity argument confuses correlation with
> causation: regions investing heavily in highways were often already
> growing for unrelated reasons, like industry shifts, and some of the
> fastest-growing metro areas in recent decades actually invested more in
> transit than new highway capacity."

**Your task:** Summarize the lecture's points and explain how they cast
doubt on specific claims made in the reading passage.

## Task 2: Academic Discussion (10 minutes, ~100 words minimum)

**Protocol:** 10 minutes total, aim for 100–150 words. You must respond
to the *specific* content of the student posts, not just restate the
professor's prompt in your own words.

> **Professor:** Our next discussion topic is about work. Some people
> argue that companies should move entirely to a four-day work week with
> no reduction in pay, claiming it increases productivity and well-being.
> Others think this would harm productivity and competitiveness. What is
> your view?
>
> **Student A (Maria):** I think a four-day week makes sense because
> burnout is a real cost to companies too — tired employees make more
> mistakes and quit more often, so the "lost" day might be recovered
> through fewer errors and less turnover.
>
> **Student B (David):** I'm skeptical. Some jobs, like healthcare or
> manufacturing, can't just compress into four days without hiring more
> staff, which costs money that productivity gains might not cover. It
> might work for office jobs but not universally.

**Your task:** Write a response that engages directly with at least one
specific point Maria or David made (agree, push back, or add a new angle
tied to what they said), and contributes your own reasoned position.

## Self-audit after each task

- Integrated: did you name the specific point from the reading that each
  lecture point challenges, rather than just summarizing the lecture
  alone?
- Integrated: did you avoid inserting your own opinion? (This task is
  scored on accurate reporting of the relationship between the two
  sources, not on what you personally think.)
- Discussion: did you quote or closely paraphrase something a specific
  classmate said before responding to it?
- Both: check word count against the minimum — underlength responses are
  capped regardless of quality.
