# TOEFL Integrated Writing — Hard Tier (full-length, dense source material)

**Unlock condition:** You should already be comfortable identifying all 3
reading points and matching all 3 listening counterpoints on the
Integrated Writing task in `writing_tasks.md`. If you're still missing a
point or mismatching which counterpoint attacks which reading claim, this
set will just reinforce sloppy note-taking — fix that on the base set
first.

**Protocol:** 3 minutes reading, then cover the reading passage entirely
(no glancing back — the real test removes it from view during the
listening). Then read the "listening" once at normal pace, no re-reads,
no pausing mid-sentence even if you want to. Then 20 minutes to write,
150–225 words, scored on **accurately representing both sources and their
relationship**, not on your own opinion.

## Task (hard tier: 3 reading points, denser academic register)

**Reading passage (3 minutes):**

> **The Convergence Hypothesis in Economic Development**
>
> The convergence hypothesis predicts that poorer economies will tend to
> grow faster than wealthier ones, eventually closing the per-capita
> income gap between them, for three main reasons. First, developing
> economies can adopt existing technologies developed elsewhere rather
> than bearing the cost of original research, allowing them to achieve
> productivity gains more cheaply and quickly than the countries that
> pioneered those technologies. Second, capital tends to flow toward
> economies where it is scarce, since returns on investment are
> typically higher where capital is limited and labor is abundant;
> this inflow accelerates industrial development in poorer nations.
> Third, poorer countries often have greater latent human capital in the
> form of underemployed or underutilized labor, and even modest
> investment in education and infrastructure can unlock substantial
> productivity gains that wealthier economies, having already captured
> most of such gains, cannot replicate.

**Listening (simulated lecture excerpt, read once):**

> "So convergence sounds clean in theory, but the empirical record is a
> lot messier. Take the technology-adoption argument. Adopting existing
> technology isn't free — it requires institutions, skilled labor, and
> infrastructure that many developing economies simply don't have, so
> the 'catch-up' advantage often doesn't materialize in practice. On the
> capital point: yes, capital should flow to where returns are highest,
> but in reality it often doesn't, because investors price in political
> instability, weak property rights, and currency risk — so capital
> frequently flows the opposite direction, from poor countries to rich
> ones, which economists call the 'Lucas paradox.' And on human capital:
> underemployed labor only becomes productive with investment in
> education and infrastructure, but that investment requires the very
> capital and stable institutions that, as we just said, are often
> missing precisely in the countries that need convergence most. So in
> practice, you get a lot of countries stuck in what some researchers
> call a 'poverty trap' instead of converging at all."

**Your task:** Summarize the points made in the listening passage,
explaining how they cast doubt on the specific points made in the reading
passage.

---

## Self-grading checklist for this tier

- Did you address all **three** reading claims (technology adoption,
  capital flows, human capital) with their matching counterpoint, or did
  you conflate two into one paragraph and lose a point's worth of
  content?
- Did you accurately name the mechanism in each counterpoint (e.g., the
  "Lucas paradox" specifically reverses the *direction* of capital flow,
  it doesn't just say "capital flow is complicated")? Vague paraphrase of
  a specific mechanism costs points even if the general gist is right.
- Did you avoid inserting your own opinion on whether convergence theory
  is correct? This task is scored purely on accurate synthesis of the two
  sources — your view is irrelevant and dilutes the response.
- Word count: 150–225. Under 150 almost always means you dropped a point;
  over 225 usually means you're padding with restatement.

## Why this tier is harder than the base set

The base integrated writing task gives you source material where each
counterpoint maps cleanly 1:1 to a reading claim, in the same order. This
one still maps 1:1, but the listening uses **different terminology and
named concepts** ("Lucas paradox," "poverty trap") that aren't in the
reading — exactly like the real test increasingly does at the harder end.
You have to recognize that a differently-worded idea is attacking the
same claim, not treat unfamiliar vocabulary as a sign you missed
something.
