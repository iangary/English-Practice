# TOEFL iBT

## Format cheat sheet (current iBT format)

| Section | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | ~35 min | 2 passages, ~10 questions each |
| Listening | ~36 min | Lectures + conversations with questions |
| Speaking | ~16 min | 4 tasks: 1 independent, 3 integrated (read/listen then speak) |
| Writing | ~29 min | 1 Integrated task (read + listen + write, 20 min), 1 Academic Discussion task (10 min) |

Scored 0–30 per section, 0–120 total. Unlike IELTS bands, TOEFL scores are
granular — small, consistent improvements compound more visibly than in
IELTS's half-band system, which rewards grinding.

## Where people plateau

- **Reading:** running out of time on the last passage because they
  re-read too much on the first. Pacing discipline matters more than raw
  vocabulary past a certain point.
- **Listening:** note-taking that's too detailed (trying to write every
  word) instead of capturing structure (who/what/why/how it connects).
- **Speaking:** the biggest score suppressor is usually *not* grammar —
  it's insufficient content development in 45–60 seconds. Silence or
  circling back to the same point reads as a lower score than a few
  grammar slips delivered with full, organized content.
- **Writing:** the Academic Discussion task (replacing the old
  independent essay) is graded partly on whether you engage with the
  *specific* classmates' posts, not just the general topic — a generic
  pre-written paragraph dropped in will be penalized.
