# TOEFL Speaking — Hard Mode (28+ Section Target)

**Prerequisite:** Record and self-score at least 3 sessions from
`speaking_tasks.md` first. This file assumes you can already fill the
response time without excessive pausing; it drills precision and score
ceiling, not basic fluency.

**Rules for this set:**
- Full official timing, no extra prep seconds "just this once."
- Record every response. You will transcribe your own filler words
  ("um," "like," "you know," false starts) after listening back — do not
  skip this because it's tedious; it is the single highest-leverage
  10 minutes in TOEFL Speaking prep.
- A response that hits the time limit by trailing off mid-sentence scores
  lower than a shorter, complete response. Practice building in a
  one-sentence "wrap-up" you can deploy if you're running short on time.

## Task 1 — Independent (15s prep / 45s response)

1. Some people prefer to make decisions quickly, while others prefer to
   take a long time and think carefully before making a decision. Which
   approach do you think is better, and why?
2. Describe a rule (at school, work, or home) that you think is
   particularly important. Explain why you think this rule matters.

## Task 2 — Integrated: Campus announcement + conversation (reading 45s,
listening ~60s imagined, prep 30s, response 60s)

**Reading passage (45 seconds):**

> **Notice: Changes to Library Late-Fee Policy**
> Beginning next semester, the university library will replace its
> current per-day late fee with a flat $5 fee for any book returned up
> to two weeks late, after which the fee doubles for each additional
> week. The library states this change is intended to reduce the
> administrative burden of tracking daily fees and to make the penalty
> structure easier for students to understand in advance.

**Listening (imagine two students discussing this; construct their
likely positions yourself for this drill — one supportive, one
skeptical, each giving one specific reason), then respond:**

Prompt: The man expresses his opinion about the policy described in the
announcement. State his opinion and explain the reasons he gives for
holding that opinion.

*(Hard-mode constraint: since you're generating the "listening" content
yourself for self-study, write out both students' positions in under 90
seconds before recording your spoken response — this forces the same
rapid-synthesis skill the real integrated task demands, just with an
extra writing rep layered in.)*

## Task 3 — Integrated: Academic reading + lecture (reading 45s, prep
30s, response 60s)

**Reading passage (45 seconds):**

> **Sunk Cost Fallacy**
> The sunk cost fallacy describes the tendency to continue investing in
> a decision based on the cumulative prior investment (time, money, or
> effort) rather than on a rational evaluation of future costs and
> benefits. Economists consider this irrational because past costs
> cannot be recovered regardless of the choice made going forward.

**Lecture (construct a professor's example illustrating this concept —
e.g., continuing to watch a bad movie "because we already paid for the
ticket" or a company continuing to fund a failing project "because of
how much has already been spent" — write a 4-5 sentence lecture excerpt
yourself, then respond):**

Prompt: Using the example from the lecture, explain the concept of the
sunk cost fallacy.

## Task 4 — Independent academic (lecture only, prep 20s, response 60s)

Construct (write, then read aloud as if it were a professor's lecture) a
4-6 sentence explanation of one concept from your own field of study or
general knowledge — economics, biology, psychology, history — that has a
clear structure: define the concept, then give one concrete example.
Then, without looking at your own text, summarize it aloud in 60 seconds.
This drills the specific TOEFL Task 4 skill of reconstructing content
from memory under time pressure, not reading comprehension.

## Scoring protocol (harsher than Set 1)

For each response, score 0-4 on:
- **Delivery:** Is pacing natural with minimal fillers? (Count them —
  more than 3 filler words in a 45-60s response is a concrete ceiling at
  a "fair" rating, not "good.")
- **Language use:** Range of grammar/vocabulary, not just correctness.
- **Topic development:** Complete idea with a clear progression, not a
  list of disconnected points.

A response needs all three dimensions at 3+ to count as 26+-level work.
One dimension at 2 caps the whole response regardless of the other two —
TOEFL raters do not average generously. Log per-task scores and your
single most common filler word in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
