## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The company's ______ financial reporting unraveled once auditors
   looked past the surface numbers.
2. After the scandal, the senator's political career entered a long
   period of ______, resurfacing only years later.
3. His ______ praise for every decision the board made struck several
   members as insincere flattery rather than honest support.
4. The volcano had seemed ______ for a generation before the recent
   activity resumed.
5. Her lawyer accused the opposing counsel of using ______ tactics to
   push the trial past the statute of limitations.

### Answer key
1. mendacious (or "venal," if the clue is read as about bribery rather
   than dishonest reporting — the sentence supports mendacious best)
2. abeyance / desuetude (abeyance fits "resurfacing," desuetude implies
   permanent disuse — abeyance is the better fit here)
3. fulsome / obsequious
4. quiescent
5. dilatory

## Why this set is harder than Set 1

These words show up less often in casual reading, so your brain has fewer
existing hooks to hang them on — expect Set 2 to take noticeably longer
to stick than Set 1 did. That's the point: GRE Verbal's top percentile is
gated almost entirely on exactly this tier of vocabulary. If Set 1 felt
easy, Set 2 is where the real test starts.
