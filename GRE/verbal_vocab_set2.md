# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Prerequisite:** Do not start this set until you can do Set 1
(`verbal_vocab.md`) both directions, under 3 seconds, twice on different
days. This set assumes Set 1 is solid and does not re-teach that
foundation — it builds on it with lower-frequency but still
GRE-common words, including several that are frequently confused with
each other (that confusion *is* the drill).

**Drill protocol:** Same as Set 1 — word → meaning, then meaning → word.
Additionally, for every pair marked "⚠ confusable," write one sentence
using each word correctly, back to back, before marking either as known.

## ⚠ Confusable pairs — the actual trap at this level

These are not "hard words." They're words test-writers pick *because*
they sound or feel similar to something else, so a confident guess is
often a confident wrong answer.

1. **Diffident vs. Confident** — opposites, but "diffident" sounds like
   it should mean "indifferent." It means shy/lacking confidence, not
   "lacking interest."
2. **Reticent vs. Reluctant** — close but not identical: "reticent" is
   specifically about reluctance *to speak*; using it for general
   reluctance ("reticent to go to the gym") is a common error the exam
   can test by contrast with a word that fits general reluctance.
3. **Mendacious vs. Mendicant** — "mendacious" = lying; "mendicant" =
   a beggar. One letter apart, completely unrelated meanings.
4. **Nebulous vs. Nubile** — unrelated words that are sometimes
   misremembered as similar; "nebulous" = vague, has nothing to do with
   youth or marriageability.
5. **Fecund vs. Fetid** — "fecund" = fertile/productive (positive);
   "fetid" = foul-smelling (negative). Mixing these up flips the
   sentence's polarity entirely.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. His ______ manner at the negotiation — flattering, agreeing with
   everything — made the other side trust him less, not more.
2. The sudden resignation of three board members was widely read as a
   ______ of deeper financial trouble.
3. After the leaked memo, the CEO's claim that the layoffs were
   "temporary" became completely ______.
4. She was normally ______ in meetings, but today she spoke up
   immediately and at length.
5. The guide's ______ small talk with every stranger on the trip made
   the whole group feel instantly welcome.

### Answer key
1. obsequious
2. harbinger
3. untenable
4. reticent (or loquacious — if the contrast is "today she spoke up,"
   the baseline must be the quiet one; reticent is correct)
5. gregarious / xenial (xenial is more precise for "toward strangers";
   log which you chose and why)

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Set 1 drilled logical-signal detection. This set adds a second failure
mode: picking a word that's *thematically* right but *polarity* wrong
(fecund/fetid), or a word that *sounds* right but means something
adjacent (diffident/confident, reticent/reluctant). Before selecting an
answer, check not just "does this fit the logic" but "am I certain this
isn't the word's near-twin."
