# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Prerequisite:** don't start this set until you can do both directions
of Set 1 (`verbal_vocab.md`) in under 3 seconds, twice in a row, on
different days. Stacking new vocab on top of shaky old vocab is how
people end up "knowing" 300 words shallowly instead of 100 words cold —
the latter scores higher under time pressure.

**Drill protocol:** identical to Set 1 — word → meaning, then meaning →
word, cover the opposite side each time.

| Word | Part of speech | Definition | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aberration | n. | something that deviates from the normal/expected | The sudden drop in sales was dismissed as a seasonal **aberration**, not a trend. |
| Bellicose | adj. | eager to fight; aggressively hostile | The senator's **bellicose** rhetoric alarmed allies hoping for a diplomatic resolution. |
| Circumspect | adj. | cautious; considering all consequences before acting | After the earlier scandal, the board became far more **circumspect** about approving new ventures. |
| Diffident | adj. | lacking confidence; shy | Despite his expertise, he gave a surprisingly **diffident** answer when asked a direct question. |
| Ephemeral | adj. | lasting a very short time | Internet fame is often **ephemeral**, fading within weeks of its peak. |
| Fecund | adj. | highly fertile or productive | The valley's **fecund** soil supported three harvests a year. |
| Garner | v. | to gather or collect, especially approval/support | The proposal **garnered** widespread support after the revised draft addressed early concerns. |
| Harangue | n./v. | a lengthy, aggressive lecture or tirade | The coach's halftime **harangue** was less about strategy than venting frustration. |
| Impecunious | adj. | having little or no money | The **impecunious** artist relied on friends for meals during his early career. |
| Juxtaposition | n. | the fact of placing things side by side for contrast | The film's **juxtaposition** of wealth and poverty in adjacent scenes underscored its theme. |
| Lachrymose | adj. | tearful; given to weeping | The film's **lachrymose** final act felt manipulative rather than earned. |
| Mellifluous | adj. | sweet-sounding, flowing pleasantly | Her **mellifluous** voice made even the dry technical content easy to listen to. |
| Nadir | n. | the lowest point | Ticket sales hit their **nadir** during the pandemic's first year. |
| Obstreperous | adj. | noisy and difficult to control | The **obstreperous** crowd ignored repeated requests to quiet down. |
| Placate | v. | to make someone less angry; to appease | The airline offered vouchers to **placate** passengers after the lengthy delay. |
| Querulous | adj. | complaining in a whining manner | His **querulous** tone undermined what was otherwise a reasonable complaint. |
| Reticent | adj. | reluctant to speak; reserved | She remained **reticent** about her role in the decision, deflecting every question. |
| Sagacious | adj. | having or showing keen judgment; wise | The **sagacious** investor avoided the bubble that ruined her less cautious peers. |
| Torpor | n. | a state of physical/mental inactivity; lethargy | A kind of **torpor** settled over the office in the hours after the holiday party. |
| Untenable | adj. | not able to be defended or maintained | Once the data was released, his original position became **untenable**. |
| Vindicate | v. | to clear of blame; to prove correct | New evidence ultimately **vindicated** the engineer's original warnings about the design flaw. |
| Wary | adj. | cautious about possible danger or problems | Investors remained **wary** despite the market's apparent recovery. |
| Xenophobic | adj. | having a fear/dislike of foreigners | The op-ed criticized the policy as thinly veiled **xenophobic** rhetoric. |
| Yoke | v./n. | to join or link together, often restrictively | The two proposals were **yoked** together, forcing legislators to accept both or neither. |
| Zealous | adj. | having great energy in pursuit of a goal | The **zealous** new recruit volunteered for every assignment in her first week. |
| Acrimonious | adj. | bitter and angry, especially in tone | The merger talks turned **acrimonious** once compensation came up. |
| Bombastic | adj. | high-sounding but with little meaning; pompous | Critics mocked the CEO's **bombastic** keynote as all slogans and no substance. |
| Convoluted | adj. | extremely complex and difficult to follow | The contract's **convoluted** clauses seemed designed to confuse rather than clarify. |
| Deride | v. | to express contempt for; to mock | Early reviewers **derided** the design as impractical, though it later became an industry standard. |
| Exacerbate | v. | to make a problem worse | Cutting the maintenance budget only **exacerbated** the equipment failures. |

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The negotiations grew increasingly ______ as both sides refused to
   compromise on price.
2. Her ______ judgment made her the one colleague everyone trusted with
   difficult calls.
3. The CEO's ______ promises about "revolutionizing the industry" turned
   out to be pure marketing.
4. Years later, the whistleblower was finally ______ when internal
   documents confirmed every claim she had made.
5. The committee tried to ______ angry residents by offering a public
   meeting, but it only highlighted how little had actually changed.

### Answer key
1. acrimonious
2. sagacious
3. bombastic
4. vindicated
5. placate

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Sentence Equivalence requires picking **two** answers that both produce
a logically and semantically equivalent sentence — not just two
synonyms of each other in isolation. Several words above (circumspect/
wary, reticent/diffident, acrimonious/bellicose) are close but not
interchangeable in every context; drill the fill-in-the-blank sentences
above by testing whether a *near*-synonym would also fit, and notice
when it wouldn't. That's the actual skill SE tests.
