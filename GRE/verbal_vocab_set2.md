# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Only start this set once Set 1 (`verbal_vocab.md`) is at mastery —
both directions, under 3 seconds, twice on different days.** This set is
deliberately a notch harder: lower-frequency on GRE word lists, more
abstract definitions, and several words that are easy to confuse with a
near-neighbor (watch the "Confusable pairs" section at the bottom —
these are where real test-takers lose points at the 165+ level).

Same drill protocol as Set 1: word → meaning, then meaning → word. Mark
"known" in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` only under the same 3-second, twice,
different-days standard.

## Confusable pairs — drill these explicitly, don't skip

These are the pairs that cost points at the 165+ level because both
words are "known" in isolation but swapped under time pressure:

- **Venal** (corruptible, open to bribery) vs. **Venial** (a minor,
  forgivable sin/fault) — completely unrelated meanings despite looking
  almost identical. A "venal error" and a "venial error" mean opposite
  things.
- **Officious** (overbearing about enforcing rules) vs. **Official**
  (authorized, formal) — officious is near-always negative; official is
  neutral.
- **Fecund** (fertile/productive) vs. **Fecal** — no shared meaning at
  all, but test-takers who've never seen "fecund" sometimes guess wrong
  purely from the visual similarity; don't let shape substitute for
  recall.
- **Diffidence** (shyness, lack of self-confidence) vs. **Indifference**
  (lack of interest/concern) — a diffident person often cares a great
  deal but lacks confidence to show it; conflating the two flips the
  emotional register of a sentence.
- **Inchoate** (just beginning, not fully formed) vs. **Inchoic** (not a
  real word — a trap option on some practice sets) — if you see a
  near-miss word choice that "sounds right," that's exactly the testable
  moment; the GRE will not offer a made-up word as the correct answer.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The negotiator refused to ______ on even the smallest point, which
   ultimately doomed the talks.
2. His prose was so ______ that readers often lost the actual argument
   under the weight of the language.
3. The official's willingness to accept bribes made him notoriously
   ______ among contractors seeking permits.
4. Her ______ answers to the board's direct questions only deepened
   their suspicion that something was being hidden.
5. The intern's ______ plan needed months more work before it could be
   presented to anyone outside the team.

### Answer key

1. obdurate
2. turgid
3. venal
4. equivocal / equivocating (adjective form of equivocate — context
   supports either; if you wrote "mendacious," log it and compare: lying
   outright is different from merely dodging the question)
5. inchoate

## Why this set specifically

Set 1 covers words that show up constantly across GRE practice
materials — you'll see them again and again regardless of source. Set 2
leans into words that appear less often per passage but disproportionately
show up as **wrong-answer traps** precisely because they're less
familiar: test-writers use them to make an incorrect choice "feel"
sophisticated. Knowing them cold removes an entire category of guessing.
