# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Prerequisite:** You should already be at "known" on all 30 words in
`verbal_vocab.md` (both directions, under 3 seconds, on two different
days) before starting this set. If you're not, you're building on sand —
go back and finish Set 1 first. No shortcuts.

**Drill protocol:** Same as Set 1 — word→meaning, then meaning→word, then
the fill-in-the-blank drill below. These 30 are lower-frequency and more
abstract than Set 1; expect them to take longer to stick.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. Despite the storm's calm center, the surrounding seas remained anything
   but ______, tossing the ship violently.
2. The senator's ______ apology, full of self-congratulation, convinced no one.
3. After years of ______ wandering between cities, she finally settled down.
4. The report's conclusions were ______ — argued from a clear bias rather
   than a neutral reading of the data.
5. He faced the committee's harsh questions with remarkable ______, never
   once raising his voice.

### Answer key
1. quiescent (trick: the word describes *inactivity*, so the blank — describing
   violent seas — needs its opposite; if you picked "quiescent" itself,
   re-read the sentence's logic, not just the vocabulary)
2. fulsome
3. peripatetic
4. tendentious
5. equanimity

## Why Set 2 is harder than it looks

These words cluster in near-synonym pairs the test exploits on Sentence
Equivalence (double-answer) questions: **mercurial vs. capricious**
(Set 1) — both mean "changeable," but mercurial leans toward *mood*,
capricious toward *decisions/behavior*. **Querulous vs. truculent**
(Set 1) — querulous complains, truculent threatens. Confusing these
costs you the "exactly matching" answer, which is the entire game on SE.
Drill the pairs across both sets together, not in isolation.
