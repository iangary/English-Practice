# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words, Escalation Tier)

**Only start this set once Set 1 (`verbal_vocab.md`) words are genuinely
automatic — both directions, under 3 seconds, on two separate days. This
set skews harder: more abstract words, more words with easily-confused
near-synonyms (the actual source of most missed Sentence Equivalence
questions at the 165+ level is picking a *close* synonym instead of the
*exact* one — these words are chosen to train that distinction).

**Drill protocol:** same as Set 1 — word → meaning, then meaning → word,
mark "known" in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` only after two clean passes on
different days.

## Near-synonym traps (the actual point of this set)

GRE Sentence Equivalence at the higher score bands rarely tests "do you
know a rare word" — it tests whether you can tell apart words that
*seem* interchangeable but aren't. Drill these pairs specifically:

- **Ingenuous vs. ingenious** — opposite-ish meanings (naive vs. clever).
  Mixing these up is the single most common Set 2 error; drill this pair
  alone for an extra minute.
- **Mollify / Placate vs. Exculpate** — the first two mean "calm an angry
  person"; exculpate means "clear of blame" — someone can be mollified
  without being exculpated, and vice versa.
- **Diffident vs. Contrite** — diffident is about confidence (shy),
  contrite is about remorse (sorry). A diffident person isn't necessarily
  sorry about anything.
- **Sagacious vs. Perspicacious** — close, both mean "showing good
  judgment/insight," genuinely near-synonyms (a legitimate SE pair) —
  contrast this with the pairs above where the words only *sound* similar
  in register but differ in actual meaning.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. Despite his reputation for toughness, the coach's post-game comments
   were surprisingly ______, careful not to criticize any specific player.
2. The scandal might have ended her career, but the leaked memo
   eventually ______ her entirely.
3. His ______ nature made him agree with the CEO's plan before she'd even
   finished explaining it.
4. The two rival factions remained ______, unable to agree on even
   procedural matters.
5. Years of chronic insomnia had left her in a state of constant
   ______, unable to focus on even routine tasks.

### Answer key
1. anodyne
2. exculpated
3. obsequious / sycophantic (obsequious fits best as the adjective form)
4. fractious
5. lassitude

## Why this matters at the 165+ level

Below V160, most missed questions are pure vocabulary gaps — you simply
haven't seen the word. Above V160, missed questions increasingly come
from *confusing* two words you both technically know, especially pairs
that share a Latin root but diverged in meaning (ingenuous/ingenious) or
pairs that overlap in connotation but not denotation (mollify/exculpate).
When you miss a question from this set, always log **which specific pair
you confused**, not just "missed a vocab word" — the fix is targeted
pair-drilling, not more flashcards.
