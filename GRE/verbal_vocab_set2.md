# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Prerequisite:** Set 1 (`verbal_vocab.md`) should already be at
"known" status in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` before starting this set — these
words are pulled from a slightly lower-frequency band and mixing them
in too early just slows down mastery of the words that show up more
often. Same drill protocol as Set 1: word → meaning, then meaning →
word, both directions under 3 seconds, on two separate days, before
marking "known."

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. Despite his wealth today, he was famously ______ in his youth,
   sometimes unable to afford a proper meal.
2. The committee chose to ______ its original proposal entirely rather
   than attempt to salvage an unworkable plan.
3. Her ______ response to the setback — calm, measured, unbothered —
   impressed even her harshest critics.
4. The CEO's ______ press conference, full of grand claims and little
   substance, convinced few analysts.
5. He was known to surround himself with ______ , people who agreed
   with every decision rather than offering honest feedback.

### Answer key
1. impecunious 2. jettison 3. equanimity (also acceptable: equanimous
   framing, though "equanimity" is the noun used here) 4. bombastic
5. sycophants

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Set 2 leans toward words that frequently appear as **distractors** in
Sentence Equivalence — words that are *almost* synonyms of a Set 1 word
but differ in connotation (e.g., "perfunctory" vs. "laconic" — both
involve doing/saying little, but one implies minimal effort/care and
the other implies brevity of speech specifically; mixing these up is a
common near-miss at the V160+ level, not a sign you don't know enough
words).
