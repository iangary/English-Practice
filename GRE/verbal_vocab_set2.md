# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Drill protocol:** Same as Set 1 — word → meaning first (cover the right
side), then meaning → word (cover the left side, read only the
definition, produce the word). Only mark a word "known" in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md` once you can do both directions in under 3 seconds
each, twice in a row, on different days. Do not start this set until
Set 1 (`verbal_vocab.md`) words are consistently fast — stacking new
vocabulary on shaky foundations just produces fluent-sounding guessing.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. His ______ response neither confirmed nor denied the allegations.
2. Despite the mounting evidence against him, the senator remained
   ______, refusing to reconsider his vote.
3. The manager offered a full refund to ______ the irate customer.
4. The professor was known for his ______ reviews, which focused more on
   mockery than substance.
5. The committee's ______ delays meant the proposal wasn't voted on for
   another six months.

### Answer key
1. equivocating
2. obdurate
3. placate / mollify
4. vitriolic
5. dilatory

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Several words here cluster in near-synonym pairs that GRE Sentence
Equivalence loves to test precisely because they're *almost*
interchangeable but not quite: **placate/mollify** (both "calm someone
down") vs. **assuage** (usually calms a feeling, not a person) vs.
**capitulate** (surrender, not merely calm down). Confusing "calms a
person" with "calms a feeling" is a specific, recurring trap — when you
drill these in `text_completion_practice.md`-style sets, write down which
near-synonym a word is often confused with, not just its definition.
