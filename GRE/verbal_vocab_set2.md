## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The judge's ruling ultimately ______ the defendant after new DNA
   evidence surfaced.
2. Her ______ remarks about the merger made it impossible to tell which
   side she actually supported.
3. The two proposals were so ______ that no simple compromise was
   possible.
4. After the scandal, the firm's executives became noticeably more
   ______ in public statements.
5. The manager's ______ apology — delivered in under ten seconds — only
   made employees angrier.

### Answer key
1. vindicated / exculpated (either fits; "exculpated" is more precise for
   "cleared of a specific crime")
2. equivocating
3. disparate
4. circumspect
5. perfunctory

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Several words above form common **contrast pairs** the GRE exploits
directly: *gregarious* vs. *stolid*, *jubilant* vs. *lugubrious*,
*obstinate* vs. *placate-able*. When a Sentence Equivalence question
gives you a blank with a clear "despite/although" signal, scan your two
correct answers for words that are near-synonyms of *each other* first —
that's the actual mechanic of SE, not just "which two words fit the
sentence in isolation."
