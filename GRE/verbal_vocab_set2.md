# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words, harder tier)

Set 1 was the on-ramp. These 30 are the words that actually separate a
160 from a 167+ on Verbal — lower-frequency, more easily confused with a
near-neighbor, and more likely to show up as a wrong-answer trap rather
than the credited answer. Same drill protocol as Set 1: word → meaning,
then meaning → word, mark "known" in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` only after two
clean passes on different days.

## Confusable pairs (this is where Verbal actually bites)

These sound or look similar to a related word and are common GRE traps.
Drill the contrast, not just the single definition.

1. **Fulminate** (denounce loudly) vs. **Ruminate** (think deeply/ponder) —
   opposite registers: one is outward and hostile, the other inward and calm.
2. **Jejune** (naive/simplistic) vs. **Jaded** (worn out/cynical from
   overexposure) — near-opposite connotations despite similar "feel."
3. **Chary** (cautious/sparing) vs. **Wary** (distrustful, suspicious) —
   overlapping but chary leans toward reluctance to give/spend, wary
   leans toward suspicion of danger.
4. **Gainsay** (deny/contradict) vs. **Gainsaid** being mistaken for
   "said in gain" — it has nothing to do with profit; memorize it as a
   single fused unit meaning "speak against."
5. **Effete** (depleted of vigor) vs. **Elite** (superior group) — these
   get confused on sound alone; effete is never a compliment.

## Drill: fill in the blank

1. The junior analyst's report was dismissed by the board as ______ —
   thin on evidence and naive about market realities.
2. Rather than contest the merger terms publicly, the smaller firm chose
   to ______, signing the agreement within a week.
3. His ______ complaints about the office thermostat made him the
   target of eye-rolls at every single meeting.
4. The think tank's position became ______ the moment its funding
   sources were disclosed to the press.
5. Years of travel for the job left her thoroughly ______, more
   comfortable in airports than in her own apartment.

### Answer key
1. jejune 2. acquiesce 3. querulous 4. untenable 5. peripatetic

## Why Set 2 matters more than it looks like it should

Below ~155 Verbal, missed questions are mostly Set-1-tier vocabulary
gaps. Above ~160, missed questions are overwhelmingly this tier: words
you've technically "seen" but can't produce fast enough under time
pressure, or confusable pairs you silently swap. If you're scoring
below 85% on this set after two review passes, that's your actual
verbal ceiling right now — not quant, not reading speed. Log it as its
own row in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`'s recurring mistake tracker if the same
word trips you twice.
