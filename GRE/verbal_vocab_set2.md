# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (30 words)

**Same drill protocol as Set 1:** word → meaning, then meaning → word,
both directions under 3 seconds, on two different days, before marking
a word "known" in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`. Don't start Set 2 as your "new 30
for today" until Set 1 is fully mastered — stacking unlearned sets on
top of each other is how vocabulary drilling quietly turns into
vocabulary skimming.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. His ______ apology, delivered in under ten seconds and without eye
   contact, satisfied no one.
2. Rather than commit to a clear answer, the minister chose to
   ______ whenever reporters raised the topic.
3. The empire's ______ came in the third century, after which its
   influence steadily declined.
4. Her colleagues found her ______ nature frustrating — she rarely
   shared an opinion unless directly pressed.
5. The merger was placed in ______ until the antitrust review
   concluded.

### Answer key
1. perfunctory
2. equivocate
3. zenith
4. diffident (reticent also acceptable in context)
5. abeyance

## Why this matters for Sentence Equivalence specifically

Sentence Equivalence requires picking **two** words that both complete
the sentence *and* produce the same meaning — so near-synonym pairs
from this set (equivocate/hedge-type pairs, obstinate/stubborn-type
pairs) are exactly the kind of trap-and-answer pairing the real test
uses. When you drill this set, deliberately pair each word here with a
synonym from Set 1 or from your own reading and quiz yourself: "do
these two produce the same sentence meaning, or just a similar one?"
That distinction is the entire skill.
