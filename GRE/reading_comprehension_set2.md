# GRE Reading Comprehension — Practice Passage, Set 2 (Harder)

**Timer: 7 minutes for this passage and its 6 questions — one minute
less than Set 1's passage, because this passage is shorter but denser;
the difficulty here is argument structure, not vocabulary.**

Don't start this until you're consistently scoring 5–6/6 on
`reading_comprehension_practice.md`. This passage has two nested
arguments (a claim, then a complication, then a qualification of the
complication) — tracking all three levels under time pressure is the
actual skill being tested.

## Passage

A longstanding assumption in cognitive science holds that expertise
develops through a roughly linear accumulation of deliberate practice:
the more hours a person spends in focused, feedback-driven repetition of
a skill, the more expert they become, with elite performance requiring
on the order of ten thousand hours of such practice. This framework has
proven influential well beyond psychology, shaping how parents structure
children's extracurricular time and how organizations design training
programs.

Recent meta-analyses complicate this picture considerably, however, by
finding that deliberate practice accounts for a highly variable share of
performance differences across domains — substantial in some (certain
games and music), markedly smaller in others (education, and especially
professions involving rapid environmental change, such as emergency
medicine). One proposed explanation is that domains differ in how
"stable" their rules are: chess and classical piano involve fixed
structures that reward repetition of the same patterns, whereas
professions dealing with variable, high-stakes, real-world circumstances
reward something closer to adaptable judgment, which deliberate practice
in the narrow sense may not cultivate as efficiently.

Critics of this stability-based explanation, however, point out that it
risks becoming unfalsifiable: any domain where practice fails to predict
performance can simply be redescribed, after the fact, as "unstable,"
with no independent way to verify that the domain's rules are in fact
less fixed, as opposed to practice merely having been poorly designed or
measured in the relevant studies. A more cautious reading of the
meta-analytic evidence, these critics suggest, is simply that
researchers have not yet isolated which qualities of practice (rather
than domain stability per se) actually transfer to real-world,
variable-environment performance — a gap in measurement, not necessarily
evidence that effortful practice stops mattering once rules become less
fixed.

## Questions

**1.** The primary purpose of the passage is to

(A) refute the ten-thousand-hour framework entirely
(B) present a complication to a popular theory and then a critique of
    one explanation for that complication
(C) argue that deliberate practice is irrelevant in unstable domains
(D) compare chess and emergency medicine as research subjects
(E) propose a new, superior theory of expertise

**2.** According to the passage, the "stability-based explanation" claims
that deliberate practice is less predictive of performance in domains
where

(A) feedback is delayed rather than immediate
(B) the practice itself is poorly designed
(C) the underlying rules or circumstances are more variable
(D) performers lack sufficient motivation
(E) researchers have not conducted meta-analyses

**3.** The critics mentioned in the third paragraph would most likely
agree with which of the following statements?

(A) The ten-thousand-hour framework has been fully vindicated by recent research.
(B) Domain "stability" cannot currently be measured independently of the
    outcome it is meant to explain.
(C) Deliberate practice is equally predictive in all domains once
    measured correctly.
(D) Emergency medicine is a more stable domain than chess.
(E) Meta-analyses are an invalid method for studying expertise.

**4.** Select the sentence in the passage that identifies a specific
risk the critics see in the stability-based explanation. (Select the
sentence, not a question option — this drills the "select-in-passage"
GRE question type; identify which sentence you'd click.)

**5.** It can be inferred from the passage that the "more cautious
reading" offered in the final paragraph differs from the stability-based
explanation primarily in that it

(A) denies that any differences exist across domains
(B) attributes the inconsistent findings to a measurement gap rather
    than to an inherent property of the domains themselves
(C) claims deliberate practice never matters in real-world professions
(D) agrees entirely with the original ten-thousand-hour framework
(E) focuses exclusively on chess and music as case studies

**6. (Select all that apply)** Which of the following, if true, would
support the critics' concern about unfalsifiability?

(A) A study finds a new domain previously labeled "stable" in which
    extensive deliberate practice fails to predict performance, and
    researchers subsequently reclassify it as "unstable" with no
    independent measure of its rule-variability.
(B) A study develops an independent, verifiable measure of domain
    stability and confirms it correlates with practice's predictive power.
(C) A researcher argues a domain is unstable solely because practice
    didn't predict performance there, without other evidence of instability.

---

## Answer key and reasoning

**1. Answer: (B).** The passage's structure is exactly this: paragraph 1
states the popular theory, paragraph 2 complicates it with meta-analytic
findings and proposes an explanation (stability), paragraph 3 critiques
that explanation. (A) overstates — the passage complicates, not refutes,
the framework. (C) overstates the critics' claim in the opposite
direction.

**2. Answer: (C).** Directly stated: "professions dealing with variable,
high-stakes, real-world circumstances reward something closer to
adaptable judgment."

**3. Answer: (B).** The critics' core objection is that the
stability-based explanation "risks becoming unfalsifiable" because
there's "no independent way to verify" domain stability — exactly (B).
(D) reverses the passage's actual examples.

**4. Answer: the sentence beginning "Critics of this stability-based
explanation, however, point out that it risks becoming unfalsifiable..."**
through "...as opposed to practice merely having been poorly designed or
measured in the relevant studies." That full sentence is the one that
names the specific risk (unfalsifiability via after-the-fact
redescription).

**5. Answer: (B).** The final paragraph explicitly frames the issue as
"a gap in measurement, not necessarily evidence that effortful practice
stops mattering" — a measurement-gap explanation, contrasted with the
stability explanation's claim about an inherent property of the domain.

**6. Answer: (A) and (C).** Both describe exactly the unfalsifiable
pattern the critics warn about — labeling a domain "unstable" *because*
practice failed to predict performance there, with no independent
evidence. (B) describes the opposite: an independent, verifiable
measure, which would actually *resolve* the critics' concern rather than
support it.
