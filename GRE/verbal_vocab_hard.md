# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 2 (Hard Tier, 30 words)

**Unlock condition:** Don't start this set until you can do both directions
(word→meaning, meaning→word) on *all 30* words in `verbal_vocab.md` in
under 3 seconds each, twice in a row, on different days. If you haven't
cleared Set 1, drilling this set is just collecting words you'll confuse
with each other under time pressure — go back and actually finish Set 1.

These are lower-frequency than Set 1 but disproportionately common in the
top 10% of difficulty Text Completion and Sentence Equivalence questions —
the ones separating a 162 from a 168+.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. Far from being a radical manifesto, the essay was criticized as
   ______, offering only safe platitudes.
2. The senator's ______ outburst on the floor did nothing to change a
   single vote.
3. Once a thriving center of trade, the port town fell into ______ as
   shipping routes shifted elsewhere.
4. The prosecutor accused the witness of ______, pointing out at least
   three contradictions in her testimony.
5. His argument sounded rigorous at first, but on closer inspection it
   was pure ______ — clever wordplay with no real substance.

### Answer key
1. anodyne / jejune (either fits — "anodyne" if emphasizing blandness,
   "jejune" if emphasizing simplistic thinking)
2. fulminating
3. desuetude
4. mendacity (note: noun form — if the blank needs an adjective, use
   "mendacious")
5. sophistry

## Why this tier matters

At the 162+ level, GRE Verbal stops rewarding raw word count and starts
punishing **near-miss confusion**: mixing up words that are both
"negative but different" (e.g., *obstreperous* vs. *contumacious* — one is
about noise/unruliness, the other specifically about defying authority).
When you self-grade, don't just mark right/wrong — mark *which other word
on this page you almost picked instead*, and drill that pair specifically.
