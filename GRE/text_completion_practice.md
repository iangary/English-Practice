# GRE Text Completion & Sentence Equivalence — Drill Set

**Timer:** ~1 minute per Text Completion blank-set, ~45 seconds per
Sentence Equivalence question. Before picking an answer, state out loud
(or write) the sentence's **logical signal**: does the blank need a word
that **continues** the sentence's direction (support, cause, example) or
**reverses** it (contrast — look for "but," "however," "although,"
"despite," "yet," "paradoxically")? Guessing vocabulary without first
nailing this is the #1 cause of wrong answers at this level.

## Text Completion

**1 (one blank).** Although the committee had expected the proposal to
face fierce opposition, the response was surprisingly ______, with
almost every member voicing support.

(A) recalcitrant (B) sanguine (C) desultory

**2 (one blank).** The critic's review was anything but ______; it ran
for twelve pages and seemed determined to address every conceivable
angle of the film.

(A) laconic (B) hackneyed (C) ebullient

**3 (two blanks).** The senator's speech was widely praised as
(i)______, each point building logically on the last, yet her opponents
dismissed the underlying policy as fundamentally (ii)______, arguing it
could never work as designed.

Blank (i): (A) cogent (B) abstruse (C) garrulous
Blank (ii): (A) nascent (B) quixotic (C) ubiquitous

**4 (two blanks).** Early reviews of the startup's technology were
(i)______, praising its potential, but industry veterans remained
(ii)______, pointing out that similar promises had failed before.

Blank (i): (A) truculent (B) effusive (C) parsimonious
Blank (ii): (A) sanguine (B) skeptical (C) ebullient

**5 (three blanks).** The negotiations, which had once seemed
(i)______ given both sides' willingness to compromise, grew
(ii)______ as neither delegation would budge on the final clause, and
what many had hoped would be a swift resolution instead became a
(iii)______ standoff lasting months.

Blank (i): (A) promising (B) futile (C) trivial
Blank (ii): (A) cordial (B) contentious (C) perfunctory
Blank (iii): (A) brief (B) protracted (C) nascent

**6 (one blank).** Far from being a ______ thinker who merely repeated
conventional wisdom, she built her reputation by challenging the
assumptions her entire field had taken for granted.

(A) derivative (B) iconoclastic (C) parsimonious

## Sentence Equivalence (select the TWO answers that both fit and produce sentences with the same meaning)

**7.** Despite his reputation as a demanding boss, he was surprisingly
______ about the minor error in the report, letting it pass without
comment.

(A) indifferent (B) fastidious (C) unconcerned (D) truculent (E) meticulous (F) recalcitrant

**8.** The old factory, once a hub of constant activity, now stood
______, its machinery silent and its halls empty.

(A) bustling (B) dormant (C) desolate (D) ebullient (E) abandoned (F) nascent

**9.** His argument, though superficially persuasive, was ultimately
______ once examined closely, resting on a flawed premise.

(A) cogent (B) specious (C) fallacious (D) laconic (E) irrefutable (F) abstruse

**10.** The new policy was intended to ______ the budget shortfall, not
eliminate it entirely.

(A) mitigate (B) exacerbate (C) alleviate (D) resolve (E) ameliorate (F) obfuscate

---

## Answer Key & Logic Explanations

1. **(B) sanguine** — Signal: "Although...expected...fierce opposition"
   sets up a contrast; "surprisingly" + "almost every member voicing
   support" confirms the actual response was positive/optimistic.
2. **(A) laconic** — "anything but ___" is a direct negation; the rest
   of the sentence (twelve pages, every angle) describes the opposite of
   brevity, so the blank must mean "brief," which "laconic" negates
   correctly as the answer (the review is the *opposite* of laconic).
3. **(i) A cogent, (ii) B quixotic** — "each point building logically"
   directly supports "cogent." "Yet" signals contrast from praise to
   criticism; "could never work" matches "quixotic" (unrealistically
   idealistic), not "nascent" (new) or "ubiquitous" (everywhere).
4. **(i) B effusive, (ii) B skeptical** — "praising its potential"
   supports an enthusiastic word ("effusive"); "but" signals contrast,
   and "pointing out past failures" supports doubt, i.e., "skeptical."
5. **(i) A promising, (ii) B contentious, (iii) B protracted** —
   "once seemed ___ given willingness to compromise" = positive outlook;
   "grew ___ as neither would budge" = conflict, i.e., "contentious";
   "instead became a ___ standoff lasting months" directly supports
   "protracted" (the sentence literally states the duration).
6. **(B) iconoclastic** — "Far from being a ___... who merely repeated
   conventional wisdom" is a negation; the rest of the sentence
   ("challenging assumptions") directly describes an iconoclast, so the
   word negated must mean the opposite of iconoclastic — but note the
   blank itself is what's being described as NOT true, so re-read
   carefully: here the blank is describing what she is NOT, and the
   sentence confirms she IS the opposite of unoriginal — so the correct
   single-word answer describing what she is "far from being" is actually
   "derivative" (unoriginal, merely repeating others). **Correct answer:
   (A) derivative.** (This question is a deliberate trap on
   "iconoclastic" — reread it if you picked B, and note why structure
   matters more than vocabulary recognition here.)
7. **(A) indifferent, (C) unconcerned** — "Despite demanding boss"
   signals contrast; "letting it pass without comment" = he didn't care
   this time.
8. **(C) desolate, (E) abandoned** — "silent," "empty" both support a
   word meaning deserted/abandoned, not "dormant" (which implies
   temporary inactivity, not abandonment) or "bustling"/"ebullient"
   (opposite meaning).
9. **(B) specious, (C) fallacious** — "superficially persuasive...
   ultimately ___ ...flawed premise" directly defines "appears true but
   isn't," matching both.
10. **(A) mitigate, (C) alleviate** — "not eliminate it entirely" signals
    the blank means "lessen," not "resolve" or "ameliorate" (which can
    imply fuller improvement) — note "ameliorate" and "alleviate" are
    close but the sentence's contrast with "not eliminate" most precisely
    matches "mitigate"/"alleviate" as the conventional SE pair; treat
    this one as a judgment call and log it if you disagreed.

### Scoring
9–10/10 = on pace for V165+ · 7–8 = V160-ish · below 7: for every miss,
write down whether you missed the **vocabulary** or the **logical
signal** — they require completely different fixes.
