# GRE Analytical Writing — Drill Set

**Timer: 30 minutes, hard stop, for the Issue task below. The Argument
task format (analyzing flaws in a given argument) is no longer part of
the current scored General Test but is included here as bonus practice,
since it directly sharpens the logical-flaw-spotting skill AWA Issue
essays also reward.**

## What separates a 6.0 from a 4.0 (it's not "better writing")

A 4.0 essay states a position and gives generic support. A 6.0 essay:

1. **Takes a nuanced position**, not a flat yes/no — e.g., "this is true
   under conditions X but not Y," rather than just picking a side.
2. **Explicitly anticipates the strongest counterargument** and responds
   to it, rather than ignoring it or only mentioning weak counterarguments
   that are easy to dismiss.
3. **Uses specific, developed examples** (a real or plausible detailed
   scenario), not a one-sentence generic reference ("for example, in
   business, this happens often").
4. **Has visible structure** a reader can follow without re-reading —
   clear topic sentences, not just correct grammar.

## Issue Task prompts (rotate; each is a generic-style claim + instructions)

**1.** "The most effective leaders are those who prioritize long-term
consequences over short-term popularity." Write a response in which you
discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree with the statement,
and explain your reasoning, considering ways in which the statement
might or might not hold true.

**2.** "Funding for scientific research should be determined primarily by
practical, real-world applications rather than by the pursuit of
knowledge for its own sake." Discuss the extent to which you agree or
disagree, considering the implications of acting on this statement.

**3.** "A society that does not preserve its cultural traditions loses an
essential part of its identity." Write a response discussing the extent
to which you agree or disagree, addressing the most compelling reasons
and/or examples that could be used to challenge your position.

**4.** "Technology has done more to isolate people than to connect them."
Discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree, and explain your
reasoning.

### 10-minute outline method (do this before writing, every time)

1. (2 min) Write your nuanced thesis in one sentence — it should name
   the condition(s) under which the statement is true/false, not just
   "agree" or "disagree."
2. (3 min) List your 2 strongest supporting points, each with a specific
   example in mind (not written out yet — just enough detail to expand).
3. (2 min) Write the single strongest counterargument to your own
   thesis, and one sentence on why it doesn't overturn your position.
4. (3 min) Order paragraphs: intro w/ thesis → point 1 → point 2 →
   counterargument + rebuttal → conclusion. Then start writing — the
   remaining ~20 minutes should be pure execution, not more planning.

## Argument Task prompts (bonus — flaw-spotting drill, 20 minutes)

Identify the logical flaws in the argument below (don't argue your own
opinion on the topic — critique the *reasoning* as given).

> "Last year, the city of Rivertown installed bright new streetlights
> throughout its downtown area, and in the following six months, reported
> crime in that area dropped by 15%. Clearly, other cities struggling
> with downtown crime should install similar streetlighting, as it has
> been proven to reduce crime significantly."

**What you should identify (self-check after writing):**
- Correlation vs. causation — were there other changes in that period
  (increased police presence, seasonal effects, a new business
  improvement district) that could also explain the drop?
- Sample size / generalizability — one city's six-month result may not
  generalize to other cities with different crime drivers.
- Undefined terms — "crime" isn't broken down by type; a 15% drop in one
  category (e.g., vandalism) might coexist with no change in others
  (e.g., assault), which matters for whether lighting is really the
  active ingredient.
- Missing comparison — was there a comparable area without new lighting
  to serve as a control, the way the IELTS UHI passage's Phoenix/Singapore
  comparison or the TOEFL induced-demand lecture used controlled
  comparisons to actually test a causal claim?

## Self-audit after each essay

- Can you point to the exact sentence containing your nuanced thesis?
- Did you name a real counterargument, not a straw man you immediately
  demolish?
- Did each body paragraph have one specific, developed example?
- Word count: 400–600 words is typical for a strong response in 30
  minutes — well under ~350 usually signals underdeveloped examples.

Log a self-estimated 0–6 score and your single biggest gap in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`.

## Advanced tier: compressed-time Issue prompts (20 minutes, not 30)

**Why:** a 6.0 under 30 minutes with planning time you've gotten
comfortable with is not the same skill as a 6.0 under real pressure. Cut
the outline step to 6 minutes total (3 min thesis + 2 strongest points,
2 min counterargument, no separate ordering step — order as you write) and
give yourself 20 minutes total. If your structure collapses under the
cut, you were leaning on extra planning time as a crutch rather than
having the structure internalized — that's exactly the gap this drill is
built to expose.

**5.** "Institutions should prioritize preserving their founding
principles over adapting those principles to changing circumstances."
Write a response discussing the extent to which you agree or disagree,
considering ways in which the statement might or might not hold true.

**6.** "The widespread availability of information has made people more
informed, but not necessarily wiser." Discuss the extent to which you
agree or disagree with this claim, and explain your reasoning.

**7.** "Competition is a more powerful motivator of excellence than
collaboration." Write a response in which you discuss the extent to
which you agree or disagree, considering the most compelling reasons
and/or examples that could be used to challenge your position.

### Self-audit specific to the compressed version
- Did cutting planning time cost you your nuanced thesis (did you revert
  to a flat yes/no under pressure), or did it only cost you polish in the
  final paragraph? These require very different fixes — the first means
  drill the thesis-writing step in isolation until it's automatic; the
  second just means you need more reps at this pace.
- Compare this essay's word count to your untimed baseline. A drop of
  more than ~100 words usually means you spent too long mid-essay
  second-guessing your structure instead of committing to the outline and
  executing.
