# GRE Verbal — High-Frequency Vocabulary, Set 1 (30 words)

**Drill protocol:** Go word → meaning first (cover the right side). Then
go meaning → word (cover the left side, read only the definition, produce
the word). Only mark a word "known" in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` once you can do
both directions in under 3 seconds each, twice in a row, on different days.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The CEO's ______ explanation seemed to answer the question while
   actually avoiding it entirely.
2. After years of research, the ______ field of quantum computing is
   finally attracting serious investment.
3. Her ______ one-word answers made the interview feel like pulling teeth.
4. The new regulations aim to ______ the environmental damage caused by
   the factory.
5. Despite the criticism, he remained stubbornly ______, refusing to
   change the proposal at all.

### Answer key
1. obfuscating / specious (context supports either — "obfuscating" fits
   best if emphasizing deliberate unclarity)
2. nascent
3. laconic
4. mitigate
5. recalcitrant

## Why this matters for Text Completion specifically

GRE Text Completion rarely fails you on "do you know this word" in
isolation — it fails you on whether you correctly identified the
sentence's **logical direction** (same-direction clue vs. contrast clue)
before you even got to picking the word. Drill the logic in
`text_completion_practice.md` using these words once you've learned them.

# Set 2 — Push past high-frequency (30 words)

Set 1 is the common tier — the words that show up often enough that
most serious test-takers already half-know them. This set is the tier
that actually separates a 165 from a 170: less frequent, easier to
blank on under time pressure, and more likely to appear as a wrong-answer
trap precisely because they're half-familiar. Same protocol: both
directions, don't mark "known" until you've nailed it cold twice on
different days.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. The committee's response to the complaint was widely seen as
   ______, a box-checking exercise rather than a real investigation.
2. Even after being proven right, she remained ______, declining to
   gloat over colleagues who had doubted her.
3. His case for innocence was built on a ______ chain of
   circumstantial evidence that fell apart under cross-examination.
4. The new hire's ______ flattery of every manager in the building
   fooled no one and earned him no favors either.
5. Reporters found the minister's answers ______ — long on confident
   tone, short on specifics anyone could verify.

### Answer key
1. perfunctory
2. magnanimous
3. tenuous
4. obsequious / unctuous
5. mendacious (accept "evasive" in spirit, but the word drilled here is mendacious)

## Why this tier matters more than it looks like it should

On the real test, Set 1-level words rarely cost you the question by
themselves — you either know "ubiquitous" or you don't, and most
test-takers do. Set 2-level words are exactly where a 162 Verbal scorer
and a 168 Verbal scorer diverge: both can reason out the sentence's
logic perfectly, but only one of them actually has the word in reach
when it's time to pick an answer under a 1-minute clock. Treat a blank
here as diagnostic, not embarrassing — it's telling you precisely where
your ceiling currently sits.
