# The Gauntlet — Day 1 Reset Mock

A single timed sitting that forces a real, current baseline across all
three exams at once. You don't get to pick your best section and call it
representative — you do all of it, back to back, under the clock, like
test day actually works.

**Total time: ~3 hours. Phone in another room. One 10-minute break only,
placed where marked — not where you feel like it.**

Do this before touching anything else in this repo. If `PROGRESS_LOG.md`
still has zero rows after today, nothing else here matters.

## Rules

- Real timer, visible, counting down — not up.
- No pausing mid-section. If you get interrupted, the attempt is voided;
  you restart that section from zero, not from where you stopped.
- No dictionary, no translator, no second tab. Exactly what the real test
  allows and nothing more.
- Write down your start time and end time for every section before you
  begin. Drift between "planned" and "actual" start time is itself data —
  log it.

## Immediately after — no delay, no "I'll log it later"

Scores decay from memory within hours; this is explicitly called out in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md` and it is still true today. Before you do anything else:

1. Self-grade every section against that file's own rubric/answer key.
   Be harsh — a fudged score here corrupts every later comparison.
2. Fill in the **Baseline (Week 0)** table in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` with
   today's date, one row per section above.
3. Write one sentence per section on *why* you lost points — not "need to
   review more." Name the specific failure (e.g. "ran out of time on
   Q4 of Quant because I didn't skip and return," "Task 2 conclusion was
   generic boilerplate, scored me down on Task Response").
4. Pick your two worst sections by score. Those are your Week 1 priority
   per the reset protocol at the top of `STUDY_PLAN.md` — not the ones
   that felt worst, the ones that scored worst. Those are frequently
   different, and the gap between them is itself worth a line in the log.
