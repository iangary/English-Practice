# The Gauntlet — Cross-Exam Stress Session

This is not a drill, it's a stress test, and it only exists because the
10-week plan's Weeks 5–6 instruction to "combine skills... context-
switching under fatigue is what the real test day actually demands" was
being followed in spirit but not nearly hard enough. Run this once your
Week 0 baseline is logged and you're into Week 3+ — not before, since you
need working technique in each skill before testing it under compound
fatigue.

**Rule zero: this is one sitting, no pausing, phone in another room,
start to finish. If you stop partway through, it doesn't count as a
Gauntlet — log it as whatever individual drill you got through instead.**

## The sequence (~2 hours 10 minutes, back to back, in this order)

1. **GRE Verbal vocab, Set 2, mixed review drill** (5 min) —
   `GRE/verbal_vocab.md`, the combined Set 1 + Set 2 shuffled list. This
   is the warm-up; it should feel almost too easy if your vocab is solid.
2. **GRE Text Completion, Set 2** (10 min, strict timer) —
   `GRE/text_completion_practice.md`.
3. **GRE Reading Comprehension, Passage 2** (7 min, strict timer) —
   `GRE/reading_comprehension_practice.md`.
4. **IELTS Writing Task 2, one advanced-tier prompt, full 40 minutes** —
   `IELTS/writing_task2.md`, prompts 11–14. No break before or after.
5. **— 5 minute water break, no phone, no scrolling —**
6. **GRE Quant, Set 2, all 10 questions** (15 min, strict timer) —
   `GRE/quant_drills.md`.
7. **TOEFL Reading, Passage 2** (16 min, strict timer) —
   `TOEFL/reading_practice_02.md`.
8. **IELTS Speaking: one advanced cue card + rapid-fire Part 3**
   (record it, ~8 min total) — `IELTS/speaking_cue_cards.md`.
9. **TOEFL Speaking: Task 3 or Task 4 advanced set** (record it, ~3 min
   total including prep) — `TOEFL/speaking_tasks.md`.
10. **GRE AWA, one compressed-time Issue prompt, 20 minutes** —
    `GRE/awa_prompts.md`, prompts 5–7.

## Why this specific order

The sequence deliberately alternates verbal-analytical work (GRE
Verbal/Quant) with sustained-production work (essays, speaking) rather
than grouping by exam. Real exam-day fatigue doesn't arrive as "tired of
GRE" — it arrives as general cognitive fatigue that then gets tested
differently depending on what task you're thrown into next, which is
exactly the scenario most practice plans never actually simulate because
people practice one exam, in isolation, on a good night's focus.

## Scoring the Gauntlet

Don't just score each section in isolation — that's what your regular
drills already do. Score these three things specifically, and log all
three in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` under a new dated "Gauntlet" entry:

1. **Degradation curve:** compare your GRE Quant score here (item 6,
   roughly 90 minutes in) against your normal isolated Quant Set 2 score.
   A drop of more than ~20% signals a real stamina gap, not a knowledge
   gap — the fix is running more Gauntlets, not more quant drilling.
2. **Task-switching cost:** did moving from a 40-minute essay directly
   into timed quant (item 4 → 6) cost you noticeably more than moving
   between two similar task types? If the switch itself is the problem,
   practice the *transition*, not just the destination skill.
3. **The weak link:** which single item in the sequence had the sharpest
   score drop relative to your isolated baseline for that same drill?
   That is your actual exam-day risk, more than your weakest skill in
   isolation — a skill that's merely mediocre but holds steady under
   fatigue is a smaller risk than a skill that's strong in isolation but
   collapses under compound pressure.

## Standing rule

Run the Gauntlet every other week starting Week 3, timed to land the
week before each weekly full-length mock in the 10-week plan, not on the
same day as it. Two maximum-fatigue sessions in one week teaches you
nothing new the second time — it just adds risk of burnout without added
signal.
