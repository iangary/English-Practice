# The Gauntlet — Full Cross-Exam Stress Session

A single sitting, ~2 hours, no breaks except where noted. This is not a
normal study session — it exists to simulate the one thing no individual
drill can: sustained performance across different skills and exams while
already fatigued. Context-switching under fatigue is what real test-week
prep (and, for some of you, real exam day if you're sitting more than one
of these) actually demands, and it's the thing people are worst at the
first time they try it.

**Rules:**
- Phone away, single sitting, no pausing the overall clock once you start
  (you may take the specific short breaks noted between sections, nothing
  more).
- Do not "warm up" beforehand. The whole point is testing performance
  without a warm-up, because the real exam doesn't give you one either.
- Grade everything honestly and log all of it in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`
  immediately after — not tomorrow, not "later tonight."

**When to run this:** Not every week. This belongs in Weeks 5–6 (per
`STUDY_PLAN.md`'s "combine skills under fatigue" guidance) and again in
Weeks 8–9, not as a weekly habit — run it too often and you're just
practicing exhaustion instead of the underlying skills.

## The session

| Order | Segment | Source | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GRE Verbal vocab, both directions, Set 1 + Set 2 | `GRE/verbal_vocab.md` | 15 min |
| 2 | GRE Quant Drill Set 2 (harder, tighter clock) | `GRE/quant_drills.md` | 18 min |
| — | *2-minute break only* | | 2 min |
| 3 | GRE Text Completion, one full set | `GRE/text_completion_practice.md` | 10 min |
| 4 | IELTS Writing Task 2, one Set 2 (abstract) prompt | `IELTS/writing_task2.md` | 35 min |
| — | *5-minute break only* | | 5 min |
| 5 | TOEFL Speaking, all 4 Set 2 tasks back to back, recorded | `TOEFL/speaking_tasks.md` | ~12 min |
| 6 | TOEFL Integrated or Independent Writing (pick one you haven't done recently) | `TOEFL/writing_tasks.md` | 20–30 min |

Total: roughly 2 hours including the two mandated short breaks.

## Why this exact order

- Vocab first, while fresh — it's pure recall and benefits least from
  adrenaline, most from a clear head.
- Quant immediately after — still fresh enough for precise calculation,
  but now you've already spent mental energy, which is the point: you
  need to know your quant accuracy *doesn't* fall off a cliff after 15
  minutes of unrelated work, because on the real GRE it can't.
- Writing (IELTS) comes after the first real fatigue sets in — this is
  deliberately the hardest spot in the session, because sustaining a
  coherent, well-organized argument while tired is exactly what a timed
  Task 2 on actual exam day will demand after hours of other sections.
- Speaking last — by this point your filler words and pacing problems
  under fatigue will be at their most honest and revealing. Don't let
  yourself "phone in" these recordings because you're tired; that's
  exactly the data point this session exists to produce.

## Post-session audit (do this before you do anything else)

1. Score every component against its own rubric (see each file).
2. For each component, note not just the score but whether it dropped
   relative to your normal (non-fatigued) performance on that same drill
   type, and by how much.
3. Identify the single component with the largest fatigue-related drop.
   That's your actual bottleneck under exam conditions — not necessarily
   the skill you're weakest at in isolation.
4. Log the full session — every score, the fatigue-drop finding, and one
   concrete fix — in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`'s weekly mock log.

## A note if your `PROGRESS_LOG.md` is still blank

If you haven't logged a single baseline score yet, do not run the
Gauntlet. It will only produce numbers you can't contextualize, because
you'll have no non-fatigued baseline to compare them against. Go do
Week 0 of `STUDY_PLAN.md` first — one timed mock per exam's weakest
section, logged — and come back to this once you have real numbers to
compare against.
