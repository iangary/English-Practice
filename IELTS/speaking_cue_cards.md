# IELTS Speaking — Drill Set

**Protocol:** Record audio every time. Playing it back is non-negotiable —
you cannot hear your own hesitation, filler words, or flat intonation
while you're busy producing it in real time.

## Part 1 (intro/familiar topics) — 4–5 minutes

Answer each in 20–30 seconds, no more. Band 7+ Part 1 answers are concise
and natural, not mini-essays.

- Do you work or are you a student? Why did you choose that?
- What do you usually do in your free time?
- Is there a type of weather you particularly like or dislike? Why?
- How do you usually travel to places you need to go?
- Did you enjoy studying history when you were at school?

## Part 2 (cue card) — 1 min prep, 2 min speak

Set a timer for exactly 1 minute to plan using bullet points only (no full
sentences), then speak for 2 full minutes without stopping. Running out
of things to say before 2 minutes is the most common Band 6 ceiling — it
means you need more concrete detail, not more opinions.

> **Describe a skill you would like to learn.**
> You should say:
> - what the skill is
> - how you would learn it
> - how long it might take to learn
> and explain why you want to learn this skill.

> **Describe a piece of technology you find useful.**
> You should say:
> - what it is
> - how often you use it
> - what you use it for
> and explain why it is important to you.

> **Describe a journey that didn't go as planned.**
> You should say:
> - where you were going
> - what happened
> - how you dealt with the situation
> and explain how you felt about it afterwards.

## Part 3 (discussion) — 4–5 minutes, abstract follow-ups

Use these immediately after a Part 2 card above to simulate the real
transition. Band 7+ here requires justified opinions with real examples,
not just restated claims.

- Do you think skills learned as an adult differ from skills learned as a
  child? In what way?
- Some people say technology is making people less sociable. Do you
  agree?
- How has the way people travel changed in your country over the last
  few decades?
- Do you think governments should invest more in public transport than
  in roads? Why?

## Self-audit after each recording

1. Count filler words ("um," "like," "you know") per minute. Under 3/min
   is solid; over 6/min needs direct attention — slow down instead of
   filling the gap.
2. Did you use at least one piece of less common but natural vocabulary
   (not a memorized "impressive" phrase shoved in awkwardly)?
3. Did your pronunciation of multi-syllable words stay clear at your
   natural (fast) speaking speed, not only when you slowed down?
4. Rate fluency, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation 0–9 separately —
   your overall Speaking band is their average, and one weak pillar drags
   the whole score down more than people expect.

## Advanced tier: abstract cue cards + rapid-fire Part 3

These cue cards are deliberately harder to fill 2 minutes on because
they're more abstract than "describe a thing you own" — which is exactly
what separates Band 7 from Band 8+ speakers: can you generate specific,
concrete detail even when the topic itself doesn't hand it to you.

> **Describe a time you changed your opinion about something.**
> You should say:
> - what the opinion was
> - what made you change your mind
> - how long the change took
> and explain how you feel about the original opinion now.

> **Describe a rule (at school, at work, or in your family) that you
> think is unnecessary.**
> You should say:
> - what the rule is
> - who enforces it
> - what would happen if it were removed
> and explain why you think it is unnecessary.

> **Describe a time you had to make a decision without enough
> information.**
> You should say:
> - what the decision was
> - why you didn't have enough information
> - what you decided
> and explain whether you think it was the right decision in hindsight.

### Rapid-fire Part 3 (15 seconds to start speaking, no extra think time)

Use immediately after any cue card above. The real exam doesn't give you
a quiet moment to compose a perfect answer — the examiner asks and you
respond. Set a 15-second cap before you must start talking (thinking
"out loud" into a rephrase of the question is a legitimate strategy, not
stalling):

- Do people in your country generally find it easy or hard to admit when
  they're wrong? Why do you think that is?
- Is it better for organizations to have many rules or to trust people's
  judgment? Why?
- How reliable do you think human intuition is compared to data when
  making important decisions?
- Do you think society rewards people who stick to their original
  opinions, or people who are willing to change their minds? Why?

### Self-audit specific to this tier
- Did the abstractness of the cue card cause you to speak in generalities
  ("sometimes people change their minds about things") instead of
  committing to one specific, personal example? That drift toward
  vagueness under difficulty is the single biggest tell of a Band 6–7
  ceiling on Part 2.
- On rapid-fire Part 3: did the 15-second cap cause more filler words than
  usual? If yes, your fluency isn't actually automatic yet — it was
  relying on think time you won't reliably get in the real exam.
