# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 40 minutes, hard stop. 250 words minimum (no upper
limit, but padding past ~300 costs you time you need for accuracy). Write
by hand or in a plain text file with no spellcheck/grammar-check running —
the real exam gives you neither.

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Response** — Did you directly answer every part of the question?
   A partial answer caps you at Band 6 no matter how good the English is.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Clear paragraphing (intro, 2–3 body, conclusion);
   logical progression; linking words used naturally, not mechanically
   ("Furthermore" in every paragraph is a Band 6 tell, not a Band 8 one).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range + precision. Circle every word you weren't
   100% sure was correct. More than 2–3 circles in a 250-word essay = this
   is your bottleneck.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Mix of simple and complex sentences.
   Count your errors per 100 words. Over 3 errors/100 words is Band 6
   territory regardless of sentence complexity.

Your overall Task 2 band is roughly the average of the four, rounded down
if Task Response is weak (a brilliantly written answer to the wrong
question is still a weak answer).

## Prompts (do one per session, rotate types)

**Opinion (agree/disagree):**
1. Some people believe that unpaid community service should be a
   compulsory part of high school education. To what extent do you agree
   or disagree?
2. Many argue that university education should be free for all citizens,
   regardless of their financial background. Discuss both views and give
   your own opinion.
3. In many countries, the gap between the salaries of the highest and
   lowest paid employees is increasing. What problems does this cause, and
   what solutions can you suggest?

**Discuss both views:**
4. Some people think that children should begin learning a foreign
   language as soon as they start school, while others believe it is
   better to wait until secondary school. Discuss both views and give your
   opinion.
5. Some believe that the main purpose of science is to make life easier;
   others believe that science creates more problems than it solves.
   Discuss both views and give your opinion.

**Problem/solution:**
6. As cities grow larger, so does the volume of traffic. What problems
   does this cause, and what measures could be taken to reduce traffic
   congestion?
7. An increasing number of people are choosing to work from home rather
   than commute to an office. What are the causes of this trend, and what
   effects does it have on individuals and society?

**Two-part question:**
8. Many museums charge for admission while others are free. Do you think
   this is a positive or negative development?
9. Some people think governments should spend more money on the arts.
   Others believe this money should be spent in other ways. Which do you
   agree with?

**Advantages/disadvantages:**
10. Increasing numbers of people are choosing cosmetic surgery to change
    their appearance. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this
    trend?

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Underline your thesis in the intro. Can you find it in under 5 seconds?
  If not, rewrite it to be one unambiguous sentence.
- Check each body paragraph has exactly one main idea, one explanation,
  and one specific example (not a vague generalization dressed as one).
- Count linking words. If you used the same one twice, replace the
  second instance.
- Read your conclusion alone, without the essay above it. Does it
  summarize your actual argument, or just restate the question?

Log the band estimate and the single biggest flaw in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`.

## Round 2 — Band 8+ caliber prompts (harder on purpose)

**Gate before you start:** these prompts are written to punish a
memorized five-paragraph template. Each one has a trap built in — a
question with more than two parts, an abstract concept that's easy to
discuss vaguely, or a premise that invites a lazy, one-sided answer. If
you've only ever scored your Round 1 essays against the rubric above and
haven't logged at least 3 of them in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`, do that first —
these won't diagnose anything new if your basics aren't logged yet.

**What makes these harder, specifically:**
- Several have **three implicit questions**, not two — miss one and Task
  Response caps you at Band 6 regardless of how the rest reads.
- "Discuss both views" prompts here pair genuinely strong arguments on
  each side, so a token one-sentence nod to the opposing view will read
  as obviously unbalanced to an examiner.
- Topics are chosen to tempt generic "in today's society" filler —
  specific, concrete examples are mandatory here, not optional polish.

**Prompts:**

11. Some people believe that technology companies should be held legally
    responsible for the mental health effects of the products they
    design, while others believe individuals alone are responsible for
    their own technology use. Discuss both views and give your own
    opinion. Additionally, suggest one practical step that could reduce
    harm regardless of where responsibility ultimately lies.
12. In many countries, young people are increasingly choosing short-term
    gig work over traditional long-term employment. What are the causes
    of this shift, and is it, on balance, a positive or negative
    development for society?
13. Some argue that a government's primary duty is to maximize economic
    growth, while others believe its primary duty is to minimize
    inequality, even at some cost to growth. To what extent do you agree
    or disagree that these two goals are fundamentally in conflict?
14. Space exploration programs consume billions of dollars that some
    argue would be better spent addressing problems on Earth, such as
    poverty and climate change. To what extent do you agree with this
    view?
15. It has been suggested that international travel does more to
    increase mutual understanding between cultures than years of formal
    education about other countries. To what extent do you agree or
    disagree?

**Harder self-audit for this round (in addition to the checklist above):**

- Count the distinct questions/sub-tasks in the prompt before you write a
  single word. Write each one down. Check off each one in your conclusion
  by name — if you can't point to the sentence addressing each sub-task,
  you missed it, not just under-developed it.
- For "discuss both views" prompts, write the single strongest argument
  for the side you *disagree* with before you write anything else. If you
  can't produce a genuinely strong one, you don't understand the issue
  well enough yet to write a Band 8 answer on it — that's useful
  information, not a reason to skip the drill.
- Find every sentence that could be dropped into a completely different
  essay on a completely different topic without editing a single word.
  Those sentences are generic filler; replace them with something
  specific to this exact prompt.

Log these under a separate "Round 2" note in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` so you can
track whether your band estimate on harder prompts is converging with
Round 1, or still dropping under the added pressure.
