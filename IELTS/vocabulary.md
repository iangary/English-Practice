# IELTS Academic Vocabulary & Band-8 Phrasing Upgrades

Memorizing "impressive" words is a Band 6 strategy and it shows — examiners
are trained to spot vocabulary that doesn't fit naturally. The actual
Band 8 skill is **precision**: picking the word that most exactly fits the
idea, and using topic-appropriate collocations correctly.

## Common Band 6 → Band 8 upgrades

| Band 6 phrase | Band 8 upgrade | Why it's better |
|---|---|---|
| "a lot of people think" | "it is widely believed that" / "a growing number of people argue" | More precise about the claim's strength and spread |
| "this is a big problem" | "this poses a significant challenge" / "this has far-reaching consequences" | Specifies *how* it's a problem |
| "good for the economy" | "beneficial to economic growth" / "boosts productivity" | Names the specific mechanism, not just a vague positive |
| "I think that..." (repeated) | "In my view... / It seems to me that... / I would argue that..." (varied) | Shows range without changing meaning |
| "very important" | "crucial" / "pivotal" / "indispensable" — but only if degree actually matches | Don't inflate — "crucial" for something merely "useful" is an accuracy error, not a vocabulary win |
| "make better" | "enhance" / "improve" / "optimize" (pick based on what's actually being changed) | Precision — "optimize" implies a process already working, being refined |

## Topic-based collocations worth owning (drill both directions)

**Education:** compulsory education, academic performance, higher education,
vocational training, a steep learning curve, rote learning, extracurricular
activities.

**Environment:** carbon footprint, renewable energy sources, environmental
degradation, biodiversity loss, greenhouse gas emissions, sustainable
development, to mitigate the effects of.

**Economy/Work:** economic downturn, disposable income, job security,
income inequality, to outsource labor, a competitive job market, work-life
balance.

**Health:** a sedentary lifestyle, mental well-being, a balanced diet,
preventive healthcare, life expectancy, an aging population, to curb the
spread of (disease).

**Technology:** cutting-edge technology, to streamline a process,
data privacy concerns, artificial intelligence, to automate repetitive
tasks, the digital divide.

## Drill: fill in the blank (cover the answer key below)

1. Many governments are investing in ______ energy sources to reduce
   dependence on fossil fuels.
2. The rising cost of housing has widened ______ inequality in many
   cities.
3. Critics argue that standardized testing encourages ______ learning
   rather than genuine understanding.
4. An ______ population places increasing strain on healthcare systems.
5. Remote work has prompted many employees to reconsider their
   ______ balance.

### Answer key
1. renewable 2. income 3. rote 4. aging 5. work-life

## Self-check rule

Before using a "big" word in a Writing Task 2 essay or Speaking answer,
ask: *could I define this word correctly if asked?* If not, don't use it —
a misused advanced word costs you more in Lexical Resource / Grammatical
Accuracy than a correctly used simple one ever would.

## Advanced tier: Band 8.5–9 precision upgrades

At Band 8+, the remaining gap is almost never vocabulary range — it's
**hedging precision**: matching your language to exactly how confident
your claim actually is. Band 7 writers tend to either overclaim
("this proves...") or hedge everything identically ("maybe...",
"perhaps..." repeated). Band 9 writers vary hedge strength deliberately.

| Confidence level | Phrasing | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Near-certain | "It is almost universally accepted that..." / "There is little doubt that..." | Claims with strong, broad evidence |
| Strong but arguable | "There is a compelling case that..." / "The evidence strongly suggests..." | Your main thesis points |
| Genuinely contested | "Opinion remains sharply divided on whether..." / "This remains a matter of ongoing debate" | Acknowledging a real counterargument |
| Speculative | "It is conceivable that..." / "One might reasonably speculate that..." | Forward-looking claims about effects that haven't fully played out |

## Precision pairs (words people use interchangeably but shouldn't)

- **"affect" vs. "effect"** — verb vs. noun, a Band 6 error that persists
  into Band 7 writing more than any other single mistake.
- **"less" vs. "fewer"** — fewer for countable nouns ("fewer jobs"), less
  for uncountable ("less unemployment").
- **"number of" vs. "amount of"** — number for countable, amount for
  uncountable — same rule as less/fewer, commonly missed in the same essay
  that gets it right elsewhere.
- **"imply" vs. "infer"** — a writer/speaker implies; a reader/listener
  infers. Mixing these up in an essay arguing about interpretation of data
  is a precision error examiners are specifically trained to catch.
- **"economic" vs. "economical"** — economic = relating to the economy;
  economical = cost-efficient. "an economic decision" vs. "an economical
  decision" mean genuinely different things.

## Drill: precision rewrite

Rewrite each sentence to match the stated confidence level, without
changing its core claim.

1. (Make this near-certain, not hedged) "Maybe social media has some
   effect on how teenagers interact with each other."
2. (Make this appropriately speculative, not overclaimed) "Remote work
   will completely eliminate traditional offices within a decade."
3. (Fix the precision pair error) "The amount of students taking online
   courses has risen sharply."

### Suggested rewrites
1. "It is now widely accepted that social media has a significant effect
   on how teenagers interact with each other."
2. "It is conceivable that remote work will substantially reduce reliance
   on traditional offices over the coming decade, though a complete
   elimination seems unlikely."
3. "The **number** of students taking online courses has risen sharply."
