# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Band 9 Benchmarking (Hard Mode)

**Prerequisite:** Self-grade at least 4 essays from `writing_task2.md`
honestly before starting this file. Benchmarking against Band 9 without
a stable Band 6-7 baseline just teaches you to imitate surface features
without the control underneath them.

## Why most self-graded "Band 8" essays are actually Band 6.5-7

The single most common self-grading error is scoring Task Response and
Coherence generously because the essay "sounds sophisticated," while
ignoring that:
- A thesis that only addresses part of a two-part question caps the
  whole essay at Band 6, regardless of vocabulary.
- Linking words used *mechanically* ("Firstly... Secondly... Moreover...
  In conclusion...") read as Band 6 structure wearing Band 8 vocabulary.
- Band 9 Lexical Resource is not rare words — it's *natural* collocation
  and zero errors. One wrong preposition or unnatural phrase ("make a
  discussion about" instead of "discuss") is a harder ceiling than most
  people assume.

## Band 9 model — annotated

**Prompt:** Some people believe that unpaid community service should be a
compulsory part of high school education. To what extent do you agree or
disagree?

> Making community service a mandatory component of secondary education
> is, on balance, a policy I support, though its success depends heavily
> on how thoughtfully it is implemented.
>
> [**Why this is Band 9, not Band 7:** The thesis takes a clear position
> ("I support") *and* immediately qualifies it with a condition
> ("depends on implementation") — this single sentence previews the
> entire essay's structure without listing topics mechanically. A Band 7
> version would write "I agree with this statement for several reasons,"
> which states a position but gives no analytical preview.]
>
> Proponents of compulsory service point to its capacity to build civic
> awareness in a generation increasingly insulated from direct contact
> with social need. A teenager who spends a semester assisting at a food
> bank does not merely learn that poverty exists in the abstract; she
> encounters the people affected by it, which tends to produce a more
> durable sense of responsibility than a classroom lecture on inequality
> ever could.
>
> [**Why Band 9:** "A teenager who spends a semester... she encounters"
> — specific, singular, plausible example, not a vague "many students
> would learn important lessons." The sentence varies in length and
> clause structure without strain.]
>
> Critics, however, raise a legitimate concern: service performed under
> compulsion is not service in any meaningful sense, and schools risk
> producing resentment rather than civic virtue if students experience
> the requirement as punitive box-ticking rather than genuine
> engagement. This objection is strongest where schools treat the
> requirement as an administrative hurdle — a signed form, a logged
> hour count — rather than as a program with any pedagogical design
> behind it.
>
> [**Why Band 9:** This paragraph doesn't just "discuss the other view"
> as a formality; it identifies *the specific mechanism* by which the
> policy could fail (administrative box-ticking) rather than a generic
> "some people disagree."]
>
> On balance, the objection is an argument for better implementation,
> not against the principle itself. A well-designed program — one that
> gives students some choice over placement, connects the work
> explicitly to a reflective component, and is assessed on engagement
> rather than hours alone — addresses the critics' concern directly
> rather than conceding the whole policy to it.
>
> [**Why Band 9:** The conclusion doesn't restate the question. It
> resolves the tension between the two views with a specific, falsifiable
> claim about what "good implementation" would concretely look like.]

## Harder self-grading protocol

For your next 3 essays, before assigning a band score, answer these in
writing:
1. Could a stranger summarize your thesis in one sentence after reading
   only your introduction? If it takes them two sentences or a guess,
   your thesis isn't Band 8+ yet.
2. Find the single most sophisticated sentence in your essay. Now find
   the single weakest one. Is the gap between them more than one band's
   worth of quality? If yes, your score is the *weak* sentence's level,
   not the average — IELTS examiners weight consistency.
3. Count instances of hedging language used for genuine nuance
   ("although," "to the extent that," "insofar as") versus hedging used
   to avoid committing to a claim ("it could be argued," "some might
   say," used more than once). The second kind caps Task Response.

Log the result as a new row in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`: `IELTS WT2 Band9-Audit`
with the specific gap identified, not just a number.
