# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. This task
is **pure description** — no opinion, no speculation about causes unless
the data itself shows a cause, and no recommendations. The single most
common Band 5 ceiling is writing a Task 2-style opinion paragraph by
habit; Task 1 does not want your view on whether a trend is good or bad.

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you write a clear **overview** (1–2 sentences
   identifying the 2–3 most significant overall trends/features, with no
   specific numbers) separate from the detail paragraphs? An answer with
   great detail but no overview is capped at Band 5, regardless of accuracy.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (by category, by time period,
   or by magnitude — pick one organizing principle and stay consistent),
   not a sentence-by-sentence recitation of every number in the order given.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Variety in describing change/comparison (see
   vocabulary bank below). Repeating "increased" five times in 150 words is
   a Band 6 tell.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data's time
   frame (past simple for historical data, present simple for a static
   diagram/map "now", passive voice for process description), accurate use
   of comparatives/superlatives.

## Core mistake to eliminate first

**Do not describe every single data point.** A Band 8 response selects
the 3–5 most significant features (the highest, the lowest, the biggest
change, a notable exception to the overall pattern) and groups the rest.
Trying to mention every number in 150–200 words produces a cramped list,
not an analysis.

## Vocabulary bank — describing change (drill both directions)

**Upward:** rise, increase, climb, grow, surge, soar, jump, pick up
**Downward:** fall, decline, decrease, drop, plummet, dip, slump
**No change:** remain stable/constant, level off, plateau, stagnate
**Fluctuation:** fluctuate, vary, go up and down
**Extremes:** peak (at), reach a high/low of, bottom out
**Degree adverbs:** sharply, dramatically, steadily, gradually, slightly,
marginally, significantly
**Comparison:** twice as many as, roughly half of, slightly higher than,
in contrast to, whereas, while

## Prompt 1 — Line graph (describe trends over time)

> The table below shows the percentage of UK households with access to
> selected technologies, 2000–2020.

| Year | Television | Home internet | Smartphone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 98% | 19% | 0% |
| 2005 | 97% | 51% | 3% |
| 2010 | 96% | 73% | 27% |
| 2015 | 95% | 87% | 71% |
| 2020 | 94% | 96% | 92% |

**Task:** Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main
features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.

| Age group | Watching TV/streaming | Exercise | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18–30 | 11 hrs | 4 hrs | 2 hrs |
| 31–50 | 9 hrs | 3 hrs | 3 hrs |
| 51+ | 15 hrs | 2 hrs | 6 hrs |

**Task:** Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main

## Prompt 3 — Two pie charts (compare proportions across two points in time)

> The pie charts below show the proportion of electricity generated by
> different sources in one country in 1990 and in 2020.

**1990 (total: 300 TWh):** Coal 55%, Natural gas 10%, Nuclear 20%,
Hydroelectric 10%, Other renewables 5%

**2020 (total: 340 TWh):** Coal 8%, Natural gas 35%, Nuclear 15%,
Hydroelectric 7%, Other renewables 35%

**Task:** Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main
features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.
(Note: since the totals differ, a feature can be true in percentage terms
but misleading in absolute terms — strong responses sometimes flag this,
e.g., coal's share collapsed, but check whether you want to also note the
overall total only grew modestly, i.e. absolute renewable output rose far
more than the percentage alone suggests.)

## Prompt 4 — Process diagram (describe stages, passive voice required)

> The stages below show how plastic bottles are recycled.

1. Used plastic bottles are collected from household recycling bins and
   public collection points.
2. Bottles are transported to a sorting facility, where they are separated
   by plastic type and color using automated optical scanners.
3. Sorted bottles are shredded into small flakes.
4. The flakes are washed to remove labels, glue, and residue.
5. Clean flakes are melted and extruded into plastic pellets.
6. Pellets are sold to manufacturers, who melt them again to mold new
   plastic products.

**Task:** The diagram shows the process of recycling plastic bottles.
Summarize the information by describing the main stages of the process.
Write at least 150 words. **Grammar note:** this is one of the only Task 1
types where near-exclusive use of the passive voice ("bottles are
collected," "flakes are washed") is correct and expected, since the actor
performing each stage is usually unnamed or unimportant.

## Prompt 5 — Maps (describe change, past vs. present tense)

> The maps below show the town of Millbrook in 1990 and in 2020.

**1990:** A small town center with a market square, surrounded by
farmland to the north and south. A single two-lane road connects it to
the nearby city, 15 km east. A small primary school sits on the west edge
of town.

**2020:** The farmland to the north has been replaced by a residential
housing estate and a new secondary school. The farmland to the south is
now a retail park and a four-lane highway has replaced the old road to
the city. The market square remains, but a shopping mall has been built
adjacent to it. The primary school has been expanded.

**Task:** Summarize the information by describing the key changes shown
in the maps. Write at least 150 words. **Grammar note:** use past simple
for 1990 features, present simple for 2020 features, and present perfect
passive ("has been replaced," "has been built") for the changes
themselves — mixing these up is the most common grammar error on this
question type.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Find your overview sentence(s). Does it mention only the big-picture
  pattern, with zero specific numbers? If it has a number in it, it's
  detail, not overview — move it.
- Count how many individual data points you named. If it's more than
  ~6–8 in a 150–180 word response, you likely listed instead of analyzed.
- For the process/map prompts specifically: check your tense consistency
  across the whole response, not just the first two sentences.
- Did you avoid any opinion language ("I think this is a positive
  development," "this is concerning")? That belongs in Task 2, not here.
