# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. This is
**not an essay** — no opinion, no speculation about causes unless the task
explicitly asks for them. Task 1 tests whether you can accurately
describe data under time pressure, nothing more.

## The #1 mistake that caps scores at Band 6

Writing a list of every number in the data instead of an **overview**. A
Task Achievement score cannot reach Band 7+ without a clear overview
paragraph identifying the 2–3 most significant overall trends or
features. Markers are trained to check for this specifically — a
response with perfect numbers but no overview is capped regardless of
how accurate the detail is.

## Structure template (use every time until it's automatic)

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — restate what the chart/graph
   shows using different words, don't copy the prompt.
2. **Overview** (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 biggest-picture trends, with no
   specific numbers. This is the single highest-value paragraph in Task 1.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — group and describe the data showing the most
   significant trend, using specific figures to support it.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — describe the remaining data, including any
   notable exceptions or comparisons the first paragraph didn't cover.

## Self-grading rubric (0–9 each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Is there a clear overview? Are key features
   covered (not every single number, but the ones that matter)? Is data
   accurate and not invented?
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping of information (by
   category, by time period, by comparison) rather than a flat,
   unstructured list of every data point in order.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of trend/data language: *rose, climbed,
   surged, plateaued, fluctuated, dipped, overtook, remained stable,
   accounted for, a marginal increase, a sharp decline* — not just "went
   up" and "went down" repeated throughout.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data (past
   tense for historical data, present for current/general, future only if
   the chart shows projections); accurate comparatives/superlatives.

## Prompt 1 — Line graph (two-variable trend)

The table below shows the percentage of households with internet access
in three countries, 2000–2020. Summarize the information by selecting and
reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

| Year | Country A | Country B | Country C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 5% | 35% | 20% |
| 2005 | 15% | 55% | 38% |
| 2010 | 35% | 72% | 60% |
| 2015 | 58% | 85% | 79% |
| 2020 | 80% | 91% | 88% |

*Hint for your overview: which country started lowest but grew fastest in
relative terms? Which country shows the most consistent, steady growth
rather than acceleration? By 2020, how close together do all three end up
compared to how far apart they started?*

## Prompt 2 — Bar chart (categorical comparison)

The chart below shows electricity generation by source in two countries
in 2022, in percentage of total output. Summarize the information by
selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where
relevant.

| Source | Country X | Country Y |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 45% | 8% |
| Natural gas | 25% | 20% |
| Nuclear | 5% | 40% |
| Renewables (wind/solar/hydro) | 20% | 30% |
| Other | 5% | 2% |

*Hint for your overview: what is each country's single dominant source,
and how different are the two countries' overall energy profiles — fossil
fuel-heavy vs. low-carbon-heavy? Don't just restate all ten numbers in
order; group by theme (e.g., "fossil fuels combined" vs. "low-carbon
combined") where it produces a clearer comparison.*

## Prompt 3 — Process diagram (no numerical data — describe steps)

The diagram below shows the process of water treatment at a municipal
treatment plant. Summarize the information by describing the main
stages of the process.

> **Stage 1:** Raw water is pumped from the reservoir into a holding tank.
> **Stage 2:** Coagulant chemicals are added, causing small particles to
> clump together into larger masses called floc.
> **Stage 3:** The water passes through a sedimentation tank, where the
> heavier floc settles to the bottom and is removed.
> **Stage 4:** The remaining water is passed through sand and carbon
> filters to remove finer particles and odors.
> **Stage 5:** Chlorine is added to disinfect the water, killing remaining
> bacteria and pathogens.
> **Stage 6:** The treated water is stored in a clean water reservoir
> before being pumped into the public supply network.

*Note: for a process diagram there is no "overview" of trends — instead,
open with one sentence stating how many stages the process has and that
it is a linear/cyclical sequence, then describe stages in order using
sequencing language (first, following this, subsequently, once X has
occurred, finally) rather than just "then... then... then."*

## Model overview (for Prompt 1, to calibrate your own)

> *"Overall, while all three countries saw substantial growth in internet
> access between 2000 and 2020, Country A started from the lowest base
> but closed most of the gap with the other two by the end of the period,
> whereas Country B maintained the lead throughout despite the slowest
> relative rate of increase."*

Notice: no specific numbers in the overview — those belong in the body
paragraphs. This is the most common thing people get backwards.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Find your overview paragraph. Does it contain zero specific numbers? If
  it has numbers, that's a body paragraph detail that snuck in — fix it.
- Count how many individual data points you described vs. grouped. If you
  wrote one sentence per number for a 15-data-point table, you ran out of
  time before reaching the comparison language that actually scores well.
- Check your tense matches what the data actually is (completed past data
  vs. a diagram describing a general/repeatable process).
- Read it back and cross out any sentence that states an opinion, a
  cause, or a prediction not shown in the data — Task 1 penalizes this as
  off-task content, unlike Task 2 where opinion is required.
