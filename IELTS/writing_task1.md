# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop (this is 1/3 of your Writing
time budget — if you're still here at minute 20, you're stealing time from
Task 2, which is worth more). 150 words minimum. No invented numbers that
aren't in the prompt's data — Task 1 is scored on accurate reporting, not
imagination.

## The structure that actually scores (not "describe everything")

1. **Paraphrase the prompt** in 1 sentence (don't copy it verbatim — that's
   a Band 5 tell).
2. **Overview** — 1–2 sentences stating the 2–3 *biggest* trends/features
   without any numbers. This is the single most under-weighted paragraph:
   skipping it caps you at Band 5 regardless of how good the detail
   paragraphs are.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — group of related data, with specific figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — the other group of related data, with specific
   figures.

You are never asked for your opinion on Task 1. An opinion sentence here
is wasted words at best, a Task Achievement penalty at worst.

## Self-grading rubric (0–9 each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Overview present and accurate? Key features
   selected (not every single data point — selecting what matters IS the
   skill)? No unsupported speculation about *why* the trend happened
   (describe what the data shows, not why you think it happened)?
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping of data (by category, by
   time period, by comparison) rather than a flat list in prompt order.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of data-description language: avoid using
   "increased" more than twice: rotate "rose," "climbed," "grew,"
   "surged," "crept up," etc., matched to the actual steepness of change.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data's time
   frame (past for historical data, present simple for processes/maps,
   future only if the prompt explicitly projects forward).

## Prompts (rotate types — each one trains a different skill)

**Line graph (trend over time):**
1. The graph below shows the number of international tourists visiting
   three countries (Spain, Thailand, and Mexico) between 2000 and 2020.
   Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
   and make comparisons where relevant.
   *(Data to use: Spain rises steadily 40M→70M; Thailand rises sharply
   after 2010 from 15M→38M; Mexico stays roughly flat 20M–25M throughout,
   with a dip around 2009.)*

**Bar chart (comparison across categories):**
2. The chart below shows the percentage of household income spent on
   housing, food, transport, and leisure in four countries. Summarize the
   information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make
   comparisons where relevant.
   *(Data to use: Country A spends highest on housing (35%) and lowest on
   leisure (8%); Country B is the inverse — lowest housing (18%), highest
   leisure (22%); Countries C and D are mid-range and similar to each
   other on all four categories.)*

**Pie charts (proportions, often 2 charts to compare):**
3. The two pie charts below show the sources of electricity generation in
   a country in 2000 and in 2020. Summarize the information by selecting
   and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.
   *(Data to use: 2000 — coal 55%, gas 25%, renewables 5%, nuclear 15%.
   2020 — coal 20%, gas 30%, renewables 35%, nuclear 15%.)*

**Process diagram (no time axis — use present simple passive):**
4. The diagram below shows the process of water treatment at a municipal
   plant, from intake to distribution. Summarize the information by
   selecting and reporting the main features.
   *(Stages to use: raw water intake → screening (removes debris) →
   coagulation/sedimentation (removes suspended solids) → filtration →
   chlorination/disinfection → storage → distribution to households.)*

**Map (change over two time points — use past tense, spatial language):**
5. The maps below show the town of Ashcombe in 1990 and in 2020.
   Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
   and make comparisons where relevant.
   *(Changes to use: a small housing estate replaced farmland in the
   north; the town center's market square became a shopping mall; a new
   ring road was built around the town's perimeter; the train station was
   relocated closer to the new housing estate.)*

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Isolate your overview sentence(s). Read them alone — do they tell a
  reader who never sees the chart what the single biggest story is? If
  you'd need the chart to understand your overview, rewrite it.
- Count how many individual numbers you cited. More than ~6–8 in a
  150-word response usually means you're listing instead of selecting.
- Check tense consistency: did you drift into present tense describing
  historical data, or past tense describing a process?
- Did you write even one sentence speculating on *why* (e.g., "this is
  probably because the economy improved")? Cut it — that's an opinion,
  and Task 1 doesn't ask for one.
