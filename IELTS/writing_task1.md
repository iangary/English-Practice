# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. Task 1 is
**not** an essay — no opinion, no conclusion paragraph with your own view.
It is a factual summary of visual data. The single most common score
ceiling here is writing a well-organized description of the *wrong*
things (listing every data point instead of the overall pattern).

## The structure that works under time pressure

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — rephrase the chart's title/
   axes in your own words. Never copy the prompt verbatim.
2. **Overview** (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 most significant overall trends
   or features, with no specific numbers. Examiners weight this
   disproportionately: a report with a strong overview and weak detail
   paragraphs outscores the reverse. If you only have time to perfect one
   part, make it this one.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — group and describe the data that share a pattern
   (e.g., all the categories that rose), citing 2–4 specific figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — the remaining data/contrasting pattern, same
   treatment.

No conclusion paragraph. No "I think." No speculation about *why* the
trend happened unless the data itself states a cause.

## Self-grading rubric (score 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Is there a clear, accurate overview? Are key
   features selected (not an exhaustive data dump)? Are figures accurate?
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (by category, by trend,
   by time period — pick one organizing principle and stick to it).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Variety in describing change/comparison:
   don't repeat "increased" more than twice. Precision in degree
   ("slightly," "dramatically," "marginally") matched to the actual data.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data type
   (past tense for historical data, present for a static/process diagram,
   varies for projections — check the prompt's dates before you start).

## Prompts (rotate chart types — don't only practice your favorite)

**1. Line graph (trend over time):**
The graph below shows the percentage of households with internet access
in four countries between 2000 and 2020.
*(Data: Country A rises steadily 10%→85%; Country B rises fast then
plateaus 15%→60% by 2010, flat after; Country C stays low throughout,
5%→20%; Country D starts highest and grows steadily, 40%→95%.)*

**2. Bar chart (comparison across categories):**
The chart below shows average weekly hours spent on five leisure
activities by people in three age groups (18–30, 31–50, 51+).
*(Data: younger group highest on "gaming" and "socializing," lowest on
"gardening"; older group reverse pattern; middle group roughly even
across all five, with "reading" highest for 51+.)*

**3. Pie charts (proportions, often 2 charts to compare):**
The two pie charts below show the breakdown of a university's funding
sources in 2000 and in 2020.
*(Data: government funding drops from 60% to 30%; tuition fees rise from
20% to 45%; private donations/research grants make up the remainder,
roughly doubling as a combined share.)*

**4. Process diagram (no time element — use present simple/passive):**
The diagram below shows the process of recycling plastic bottles, from
collection to the production of new items.
*(Stages: collection → sorting by type → washing → shredding into flakes
→ melting → forming into pellets → manufacturing new products.)*

**5. Map (describe change between two time periods):**
The maps below show a small coastal town in 1990 and the same town in
2020.
*(Changes: a fishing harbor is replaced by a marina; farmland on the
north side becomes a housing estate; a new road bypasses the town
center; the old train station is converted into a museum.)*

**6. Mixed/combination (table + chart together — harder, time-crunch practice):**
The table shows the number of international students at a university by
region of origin (2010 vs. 2020), and the bar chart shows the same
university's total international enrollment over the same period.
*(Table: Asia 400→1,200; Europe 300→350; Africa 100→500; Americas
200→250. Chart: total enrollment roughly triples, 1,000→3,200, with the
steepest single-year jump between 2015 and 2017.)*

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Read only your overview sentence(s) in isolation. Could someone who
  never saw the chart guess the 2–3 biggest patterns from it alone? If
  not, your overview is too vague or too specific — fix the altitude.
- Count how many individual numbers you cited. More than ~6–8 in a
  150-word report usually means you're describing data points instead of
  patterns.
- Check your organizing principle is consistent: don't group by category
  in paragraph 1 and switch to grouping by time period in paragraph 2
  with no signal to the reader.
- Check tense consistency against the prompt type (process diagrams:
  present passive throughout; historical data: past tense throughout;
  projected/future data: future forms — mixing these is a common Band 6
  tell).
