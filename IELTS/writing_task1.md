# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. Task 1
is an **overview + data report**, not an essay — no opinion, no
conclusion with recommendations. The single most common Band 5–6 trap
is writing a Task 2-style conclusion ("In conclusion, this shows
that...") onto a Task 1 answer. Don't.

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you give a clear **overview** of the
   main trend(s) *before* the details? A report with only detail and
   no overview caps at Band 5, no matter how accurate the numbers are.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping of data (don't just
   describe every data point left-to-right in the order it appears —
   group by trend or category).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of data-description language: avoid
   repeating "increased" more than twice; know synonyms (*rose, climbed,
   grew, surged, crept up*) and their different intensities.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data type
   (past tense for historical data, present for a static diagram/process,
   mixed appropriately for a prediction graph).

## The 4-part structure (use this every time, no exceptions)

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — restate what the
   chart/graph shows, without copying the prompt's exact wording.
2. **Overview** (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 *biggest* trends or
   features, with no specific numbers. This is the single highest-value
   part of the whole task; examiners look for it explicitly.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — detail on the first trend/group, with
   specific figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — detail on the second trend/group, with
   specific figures and comparisons.

## Prompts (do one per session, rotate chart types)

**Line graph:**
1. The line graph below shows the number of international tourists
   (in millions) visiting four countries — Spain, Thailand, Mexico, and
   Turkey — between 2000 and 2020. *(Describe: Spain starts highest at
   ~45M in 2000, rises steadily to ~80M by 2020; Thailand starts lowest
   at ~10M, rises sharply after 2010 to overtake Mexico by 2018,
   reaching ~40M; Mexico rises gradually from ~20M to ~45M; Turkey
   dips sharply around 2016 from ~40M to ~25M before recovering to
   ~50M by 2020.)*

**Bar chart:**
2. The bar chart shows the percentage of households with internet
   access in three countries (Brazil, Nigeria, Germany) in 2005, 2012,
   and 2020. *(Describe: Germany starts near 60%, reaches ~95% by
   2020; Brazil starts near 15%, reaches ~80%; Nigeria starts under
   5%, reaches ~50%, the fastest relative growth of the three despite
   the lowest absolute figure throughout.)*

**Pie charts (comparison):**
3. The two pie charts show the sources of electricity generation in
   a country in 2000 and 2020. *(Describe: coal drops from 55% to 20%;
   natural gas rises from 20% to 30%; renewables (solar + wind
   combined) rise from 3% to 35%; nuclear stays roughly stable at
   ~12–15% across both years.)*

**Table:**
4. The table shows average weekly hours spent on three activities
   (reading, watching television, social media) by age group (13–17,
   25–34, 55–64) in a single year. *(Describe: teens lead social media
   by a wide margin (~18 hrs/week) but trail in reading (~1.5 hrs); the
   55–64 group leads reading (~5 hrs) and TV (~15 hrs) but has
   minimal social media use (~2 hrs); the 25–34 group is roughly
   balanced across all three, highest in none.)*

**Process diagram:**
5. The diagram shows the process of producing bottled olive oil, from
   harvesting to packaging. *(Describe stages in order using present
   simple passive: olives are harvested → washed → crushed into a
   paste → pressed to extract oil → filtered → bottled → labeled and
   packaged. Group into "pre-pressing," "extraction," and "packaging"
   stages for your overview rather than just listing all six steps.)*

**Map (two time periods):**
6. The maps show a small coastal town in 1990 and in 2020.
   *(Describe: the fishing harbor present in 1990 is replaced by a
   marina and waterfront restaurants by 2020; a new residential area
   has been built where farmland used to be; the single road through
   town has been widened and a new bridge added; the old town square
   remains largely unchanged in both maps — note this as a point of
   continuity, which examiners reward as much as noting change.)*

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Find your overview sentence(s). Do they contain **zero** specific
  numbers? (They should — numbers belong in the body, trends belong in
  the overview.)
- Count how many data points you described. If it's every single one
  in the chart, you didn't group/select — Task 1 rewards selecting the
  *significant* features, not transcribing the whole chart.
- Check your tense consistency: did you drift between past and present
  tense for the same time period?
- Make sure you wrote **zero** opinion words ("I think," "in my view,"
  "this is a positive trend") — Task 1 is descriptive only.
