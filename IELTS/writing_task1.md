# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop (Task 1 is 1/3 of your Writing
time but worth the same as Task 2 in the overall Writing band — don't let
it eat into Task 2's 40 minutes). 150 words minimum. No calculator, no
spellcheck. You are **describing and comparing data**, not analyzing why
it happened and not giving your opinion — that's the single most common
Band 5–6 ceiling on this task.

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you give a clear overview of the main
   trend(s) *before* the details? An essay with accurate numbers but no
   overview paragraph is capped at Band 5, full stop — this is the single
   most expensive mistake on Task 1.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (don't describe the chart
   left-to-right/category-by-category if a different grouping tells the
   story better — e.g., group by trend direction, not by legend order).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of data-description language (see below).
   Using "increased" seven times in 150 words is a Band 6 tell.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the chart type
   (past tense for historical data, present for a process/map with no
   dates, "will" only if the chart itself shows a projection).

## The structure that works for every chart type

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — restate what the chart
   shows without copying the prompt's wording verbatim.
2. **Overview** (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 *most significant* features,
   with zero specific numbers. This is what separates Band 7+ from Band
   6: a reader who only reads your overview should understand the whole
   chart's story.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — group of related data, with specific figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — the other group, with specific figures.

No conclusion paragraph — Task 1 doesn't need one, and writing one just
steals time from accuracy.

## Data-description vocabulary (vary these — don't repeat)

| Direction | Verbs | Adverbs (degree) | Nouns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up | rose, increased, climbed, surged, grew | sharply, dramatically, steadily, gradually, slightly | a rise, an increase, a surge, an upward trend |
| Down | fell, declined, dropped, decreased, plummeted | sharply, dramatically, steadily, gradually, slightly | a fall, a decline, a drop, a downward trend |
| No change | remained stable, stayed constant, plateaued, leveled off | — | stability, a plateau |
| Fluctuation | fluctuated, varied | wildly, slightly | fluctuation(s) |
| Extremes | peaked at, reached a low of, bottomed out at | — | a peak, a low point |

**Comparing:** whereas, in contrast, while, compared with, by the same
token, similarly, likewise. **Approximating numbers** (do this — exact
transcription of every figure reads as copying, not analysis): roughly,
approximately, just over/under, around, nearly, almost exactly.

## Prompts (rotate chart types — don't only drill the one you're comfortable with)

**1. Line graph (trend over time).** The line graph below shows the
percentage of households with internet access in three countries
(Country A, Country B, Country C) between 2000 and 2020.

> Data to describe: Country A rose steadily from 10% (2000) to 92%
> (2020), overtaking the others around 2012. Country B rose quickly from
> 5% (2000) to 60% (2010), then plateaued at 62–65% through 2020. Country
> C rose slowly and steadily from 2% (2000) to 45% (2020), never
> overtaking the others.

**2. Bar chart (comparison across categories).** The bar chart shows
average weekly hours spent on four leisure activities (TV, reading,
sport, socializing) by two age groups (18–30 and 60+) in one country.

> Data to describe: 18–30 group: TV 8 hrs, reading 2 hrs, sport 5 hrs,
> socializing 10 hrs. 60+ group: TV 15 hrs, reading 7 hrs, sport 3 hrs,
> socializing 6 hrs.

**3. Pie charts (proportions, often two charts to compare).** The two
pie charts below show the sources of electricity generation in Country X
in 2000 and in 2020.

> Data to describe: 2000 — coal 55%, gas 20%, nuclear 15%, renewables
> 5%, other 5%. 2020 — coal 20%, gas 25%, nuclear 10%, renewables 40%,
> other 5%.

**4. Table (precise figures across multiple variables).** The table
below shows the number of international students (in thousands)
studying in four countries in 2010 and 2020.

> Data: Country W: 120 → 340. Country X: 450 → 510. Country Y: 90 → 85.
> Country Z: 200 → 600.

**5. Process diagram (no numbers — describe steps, present tense,
passive voice heavily favored).** The diagram below shows the process of
water treatment at a municipal treatment plant, from intake to
distribution.

> Stages to describe: 1) Raw water intake from reservoir. 2) Screening
> removes large debris. 3) Coagulation — chemicals added to clump fine
> particles. 4) Sedimentation — clumped particles settle and are removed.
> 5) Filtration through sand/carbon layers. 6) Disinfection with
> chlorine or UV. 7) Storage in clean-water reservoir. 8) Distribution to
> households via pipe network.

**6. Map (two time points or two versions of a location — describe
changes, not geography trivia).** The maps below show the town of
Ashford in 1990 and the same town in 2020.

> Changes to describe: A small residential area in 1990 expanded
> significantly by 2020. A forest in the north was partly cleared and
> replaced by a shopping center. A single-lane road became a four-lane
> highway. A new school and hospital were built in the previously
> undeveloped east side. The river remained unchanged.

**7. Mixed/combination (bar + line together — the hardest type; identify
which data series deserves the overview focus).** The chart below shows
both the number of new car sales (bars) and the average price of a new
car (line) in one country from 2015 to 2022.

> Data: Sales fell steadily from 2.1M (2015) to 1.3M (2022), with a sharp
> drop in 2020. Average price rose steadily from $25k (2015) to $38k
> (2022), with no dip in 2020 — prices kept rising even as sales
> collapsed.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Circle your overview paragraph. Does it contain **zero** specific
  numbers and **all** of the 2–3 biggest-picture trends? If it has a
  number in it, you wrote a body sentence, not an overview.
- Check you did not write a single sentence of opinion, speculation
  about causes, or prediction beyond what the chart itself shows (unless
  the chart is explicitly a projection) — Task 1 is description only.
- Count distinct trend-language words/phrases used. Fewer than 4 distinct
  ones in a 150-word response is a Lexical Resource flag.
- For process/map prompts specifically: check your verb tense is
  consistent (present passive throughout for a process; past throughout
  for two historical map snapshots, not a mix).

Log the band estimate, the chart type, and the single biggest flaw in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`. If "no overview" or "included opinion" is the flaw
twice, that's a recurring-mistake-tracker entry, not a one-off.

### Scoring note

Unlike Task 2, Task 1 rewards **completeness and accuracy over
ambition** — a plain, correctly structured description of all the key
data beats a stylistically impressive essay that omits or misreports a
trend. When in doubt, describe more data points correctly rather than
fewer data points more elaborately.
