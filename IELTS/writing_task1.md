# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. No opinion,
no speculation about causes not shown in the data — Task 1 is purely
descriptive/reportive. The single most common score-capping error is
writing what you *think* explains the data instead of what the data
*shows*.

Since these drills are text, not images, each prompt gives you the
underlying data as a table or list instead of a chart. Your job is
identical to the real test: identify the overview (the 1–2 biggest
takeaways) and the most significant supporting features — don't just
describe every number in order, that's a Band 5 strategy.

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you give a clear overview statement
   (usually its own short paragraph) identifying the main trend(s)
   *before* diving into detail? Essays without an explicit overview
   paragraph cap at Band 5, regardless of how accurate the detail is.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (don't describe the data
   in a random order; group by category, time period, or magnitude).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of data-description language (see below)
   used accurately — not just "increased" and "decreased" repeated.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data type
   (past tense for historical data, present for a process diagram,
   comparatives/superlatives used correctly for comparisons).

## Data-description vocabulary (drill both directions)

**Trends:** rose/climbed/surged (sharp) vs. increased gradually/edged up
(slow); fell/plummeted/dropped sharply vs. declined slightly/tailed off;
fluctuated; remained stable/plateaued; peaked at; reached a low of.

**Comparisons:** significantly higher/lower than; roughly on par with;
more than double/triple; a marginal difference between; by far the
largest/smallest.

**Process diagrams:** first/initially, subsequently, following this,
once X has occurred, the final stage/output.

## Prompt 1 — Line graph (data given as a table)

The table shows the percentage of households with internet access in
three countries, 2000–2020.

| Year | Country A | Country B | Country C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 5% | 20% | 35% |
| 2005 | 15% | 35% | 55% |
| 2010 | 40% | 55% | 70% |
| 2015 | 68% | 72% | 82% |
| 2020 | 85% | 88% | 90% |

**Task:** Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main
features, and make comparisons where relevant.

## Prompt 2 — Bar chart (data given as a table)

The table shows average weekly hours spent on three leisure activities
by age group in one country in 2022.

| Age group | Watching TV | Exercising | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16–24 | 8 hrs | 5 hrs | 2 hrs |
| 25–44 | 10 hrs | 3 hrs | 3 hrs |
| 45–64 | 14 hrs | 2 hrs | 5 hrs |
| 65+ | 20 hrs | 1 hr | 7 hrs |

**Task:** Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main
features, and make comparisons where relevant.

## Prompt 3 — Process diagram (steps given as a list)

The stages of producing bottled drinking water:

1. Water extracted from underground spring source.
2. Water passes through a multi-stage filtration system (removes
   sediment, then carbon filtration for taste/odor).
3. Water is UV-sterilized to eliminate remaining microorganisms.
4. Water is pumped into a holding tank for quality testing.
5. Approved water is pumped into automated bottle-filling machines.
6. Bottles are capped, labeled, and packed into crates.
7. Crates are shipped to distribution warehouses.

**Task:** Describe the process by which bottled water is produced,
using passive voice and sequencing language throughout (a process
diagram has no "owner" performing the action for most steps, so active
voice with a vague subject like "they" or "workers" reads as imprecise).

## Prompt 4 — Map/diagram change (two descriptions given as text)

**Town of Millbrook, 1990:** A small town center with a market square,
surrounded by farmland to the north and south, a single road connecting
to the highway, and a small school on the east side.

**Town of Millbrook, 2020:** The market square remains, but the
farmland to the north has been replaced by a residential housing
development with three new streets. The farmland to the south is now a
shopping center with a large parking lot. The single road to the
highway has been widened to four lanes, and a second road now connects
to the highway from the new housing development. The school has been
expanded with a new sports field.

**Task:** Summarize the information by describing the main changes and
making comparisons between the two time periods, as you would for a
pair of maps.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Is your overview paragraph identifiable as a separate paragraph, and
  does it avoid specific numbers (overview = the shape of the data, not
  the data itself)?
- Did you group related data together rather than listing figures in
  the order they appear in the source table?
- For Prompt 3 (process): did you use passive voice consistently, and
  did you include every stage without adding a stage that wasn't given?
- For Prompt 4 (maps): did you use past tense accurately for 1990 details
  and a mix of past/present perfect for what has changed by 2020?
- Word count: under 150 almost always means an underdeveloped overview
  or missing a data group entirely.
