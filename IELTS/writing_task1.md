# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop (Task 1 + Task 2 share a single
60-minute writing period — if you run over 20 here, Task 2 pays for it).
150 words minimum. No calculator, no spellcheck. This task is graded as
heavily as Task 2 but gets a third of the time, so precision beats
elaboration — you are reporting data, not arguing an opinion. Never give
your own opinion or speculate about causes the data doesn't show; that is
an automatic Task Achievement penalty here (the opposite of Task 2).

## Self-grading rubric (score yourself 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you give a clear overview of the main
   trends/differences (2–3 sentences, no numbers) AND support it with
   accurate, selected data? A report with numbers but no overview caps at
   Band 5 — the overview is not optional, it is the single most
   under-weighted sentence group by candidates who plateau at Band 6.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (don't describe the chart
   left-to-right line by line; group by trend/category instead).
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of trend/comparison language used
   accurately (see vocabulary bank below). Repeating "increased" six times
   in 150 words is a Band 6 ceiling regardless of grammar.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data given
   (past simple for a single past period, present simple for a static
   process/map, mixed past for diagrams showing change over time).

## Standard structure (use every time, no exceptions)

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — restate the chart's subject
   without copying the prompt's exact wording.
2. **Overview** (2–3 sentences, no numbers) — the 1–2 most striking
   trends or differences. This is what an examiner reads first to judge
   Task Achievement; write it before the body paragraphs, not as an
   afterthought.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — the first group of data, with 2–4 specific
   supporting figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — the second group, same treatment.

## Trend & comparison vocabulary bank

- **Upward:** rose, increased, climbed, surged, soared, rocketed
- **Downward:** fell, decreased, declined, dropped, plummeted, slumped
- **No change:** remained stable, levelled off, plateaued, stagnated
- **Fluctuation:** fluctuated, fluctuated wildly, varied
- **Extremes:** peaked at, reached a high/low of, bottomed out at
- **Degree:** slightly, marginally, moderately, substantially, sharply,
  dramatically
- **Comparison:** compared with, in contrast to, whereas, while,
  by a wide margin, roughly double/half, marginally higher/lower than

## Prompts (do one per session, rotate chart types — all four types appear on the real test)

**1. Bar chart — Line (static, one point in time, no trend language needed).**
The table below shows the percentage of households with internet access
in four countries in 2023.

| Country | % of households with internet access |
|---|---|
| South Korea | 97% |
| United Kingdom | 93% |
| Brazil | 81% |
| Nigeria | 36% |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

**2. Line graph — trend over time.**
The graph below shows the number of international tourists (in millions)
visiting three countries between 2000 and 2020.

| Year | Country A | Country B | Country C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | 10 | 25 | 5 |
| 2005 | 15 | 30 | 8 |
| 2010 | 22 | 28 | 15 |
| 2015 | 30 | 20 | 25 |
| 2020 | 12 | 10 | 38 |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

**3. Pie charts — proportions, two points in time.**
The two pie charts below show the sources of electricity generation in a
country in 1990 and in 2020.

1990: Coal 55%, Natural Gas 15%, Hydro 20%, Nuclear 8%, Other renewables 2%
2020: Coal 20%, Natural Gas 25%, Hydro 15%, Nuclear 10%, Other renewables 30%

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

**4. Table — multiple categories, static comparison.**
The table below shows average weekly hours spent on four activities by
three age groups.

| Age group | Exercise | Reading | Socializing (in person) | Screen time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18–30 | 3 | 1 | 6 | 28 |
| 31–50 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 18 |
| 51+ | 5 | 6 | 5 | 10 |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

**5. Process diagram — no trend language, present simple passive voice.**
The diagram below shows the process of producing bottled drinking water,
from source to distribution: (1) water extracted from underground spring
→ (2) transported by pipeline to treatment plant → (3) filtered to remove
sediment → (4) ozone-treated to kill bacteria → (5) bottled and sealed by
automated machinery → (6) labeled → (7) packed into crates → (8)
distributed to retailers by truck.

Summarize the information by describing the stages of the process.

**6. Maps — before/after, past simple for the earlier map, present or past for changes.**
The maps below show a small coastal town in 1980 and the same town in
2020. In 1980, the town had a small fishing harbor, a single row of
houses along the coast road, and farmland inland. By 2020, the harbor
had been converted into a marina for leisure boats, the farmland had
been replaced by a hotel complex and a car park, and a new road bypassed
the original coast road.

Summarize the information by describing the main changes.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Find your overview sentence(s). Does it contain a number? If yes,
  delete the number and rewrite it as a pure comparison — numbers belong
  in the body, not the overview.
- Count how many different trend/comparison words you used. Fewer than 3
  distinct ones in a chart with clear ups and downs = you're under-using
  the vocabulary bank above.
- Check your tense matches the chart type (static table = present simple;
  historical trend = past simple; map before/after = past simple
  throughout, not present for the "after" map).
- Did you include any opinion, cause, or prediction not shown in the
  data? Cross it out — it doesn't earn credit here and can cost Task
  Achievement marks for going off-task.
