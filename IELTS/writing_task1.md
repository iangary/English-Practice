# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. No
spellcheck/grammar-check. This is a **report**, not an essay — you
describe what the data/diagram shows, you never give your opinion on it.

## Why Task 1 is graded differently from Task 2

Same four criteria (Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical
Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy), but **Task Achievement** here
means something specific: you must write a clear **overview** identifying
the main trends/stages/features, and you must select and report the key
data rather than listing every number. A response that lists all the
figures with no overview caps at Band 5, no matter how accurate the
numbers are. A response with a sharp overview but a couple of minor
figure errors can still reach Band 7+.

**Structure that works for every graph/chart/table:**
1. Paraphrase the question (1 sentence) — restate what the
   graph/chart/table shows, in your own words.
2. Overview (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 biggest patterns, *without*
   specific numbers. This is the single highest-value part of the
   response; examiners look for it explicitly.
3. Body paragraph 1 — detail + supporting figures for one group/trend.
4. Body paragraph 2 — detail + supporting figures for the rest.

**For process/map diagrams**, swap the overview for "how many stages
there are and where the process starts/ends" (process) or "the general
nature of the change" (map), and use body paragraphs for sequential
stages or specific changed locations instead of data groups.

## Prompt 1 — Line graph

**The graph below shows household spending on four categories in the
United Kingdom (as a percentage of total household spending) between
1980 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the
main features, and make comparisons where relevant.**

| Year | Food | Housing | Leisure & recreation | Transport |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1980 | 24% | 15% | 9% | 13% |
| 1990 | 20% | 17% | 11% | 14% |
| 2000 | 16% | 19% | 14% | 15% |
| 2010 | 13% | 22% | 15% | 14% |
| 2020 | 11% | 24% | 17% | 12% |

### Model overview (for self-check only — write your own first)

> Overall, spending on food fell steadily and substantially over the
> period, while spending on housing rose to become the largest category
> by 2020, having started as the second smallest. Leisure and recreation
> spending nearly doubled, whereas transport spending remained relatively
> stable throughout, fluctuating only slightly.

Note what this overview does: no numbers, just direction + magnitude
("fell steadily," "nearly doubled," "relatively stable") and one direct
comparison ("became the largest... having started as the second
smallest"). That comparison sentence is what separates Band 7 overviews
from Band 6 ones — most candidates state trends but never compare them
to each other.

## Prompt 2 — Bar chart

**The chart below shows the percentage of electricity generated from
renewable sources in four countries in 2000 and in 2020. Summarise the
information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make
comparisons where relevant.**

| Country | 2000 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | 7% | 44% |
| Brazil | 36% | 46% |
| India | 14% | 21% |
| Canada | 58% | 65% |

Plan your own overview before looking at the hint below.

**Hint (don't skip straight to it):** the most reportable feature isn't
"all countries increased" — it's the difference in *starting points and
rate of increase*: Germany's huge percentage-point jump from a low base
versus Canada's already-high and more modestly increasing base. A strong
overview names both of these groupings instead of describing each country
one by one.

## Prompt 3 — Process diagram

**The diagram below shows the process of recycling plastic bottles.
Summarise the information by describing the main stages of the process.**

Stages (in order):
1. Used plastic bottles are collected from household recycling bins and
   public collection points.
2. Bottles are transported to a sorting facility, where they are
   separated by plastic type and colour, and non-plastic contaminants are
   removed.
3. Sorted bottles are shredded into small flakes.
4. Flakes are washed to remove labels, glue, and residue, then dried.
5. Clean flakes are melted and extruded into plastic pellets.
6. Pellets are sold to manufacturers, who melt them again to mould new
   plastic products.

**Task Achievement reminder for process diagrams:** state the number of
stages and the start/end points in your overview ("The process consists
of six stages, beginning with collection from households and ending with
the production of new plastic items from recycled pellets"), then use
sequencing language (first, once X has occurred, after Y, finally) in the
body — not just "and then... and then."

## Prompt 4 — Maps (before/after)

**The maps below show the town centre of Elmridge in 2000 and the same
area in 2020. Summarise the information by describing the main changes.**

**2000:** A small train station in the north; a car park directly south
of the station; a row of small independent shops along Main Street
(running east–west through the centre); a vacant lot in the southeast
corner; two-lane roads throughout.

**2020:** The train station has been expanded with an additional
platform; the car park has been replaced by a multi-storey shopping
mall; Main Street's independent shops have been replaced by chain stores
and a pedestrianised zone (no vehicle access); the vacant lot now
contains a public park with a pond; the two-lane roads around the centre
have been widened to four lanes, except on the now-pedestrianised Main
Street.

**Task Achievement reminder for maps:** report the *general nature* of
the change in your overview ("the town centre has been significantly
redeveloped and modernised, with increased commercial space and reduced
vehicle access to the centre") before listing specific changed locations.

## Self-grading rubric (score 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Is there a clear, numberless overview
   identifying the 2–3 main features? Did you select key data rather
   than dumping every figure?
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (by trend/stage/location,
   not just chronological number-reading); appropriate linking words for
   comparison/sequencing.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of trend/comparison language beyond
   "increased" and "decreased" (e.g., "rose steadily," "fluctuated,"
   "overtook," "remained static," "a slight dip").
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data given
   (past for historical data, present for a diagram/process with no
   dates), accurate comparative/superlative forms.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Can you point to your overview sentence(s) and confirm they contain
  **zero** specific numbers?
- Did you make at least one direct comparison between two data
  points/groups, not just report each in isolation?
- Count how many numbers you cited in the body — more than 8–10 in a
  150–180 word response usually means you're listing instead of
  selecting.
- Check your tense matches the data (don't describe a 1980–2020 graph
  entirely in the present tense).
