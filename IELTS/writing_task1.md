# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. No ruler,
no calculator, no outline prepared in advance — on exam day the data is
new to you every time, so practice reading it cold. Task 1 is worth the
same as Task 2 in your overall Writing band, but most candidates spend a
third of the time on it. That imbalance is a choice, not a law — fix it
here, not on exam day.

## The one rule that separates Band 6 from Band 7+

**Do not describe every data point.** Task 1 rewards *selecting and
reporting the main features* — a Band 9 answer explicitly identifies the
two or three things that matter (the highest, the lowest, the clearest
trend, the sharpest change, the most significant comparison) and backs
each with supporting figures. An answer that marches through the chart
left to right, mentioning every bar, is a data transcription, not a
report, and caps around Band 5–6 regardless of grammar.

## Self-grading rubric (score 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Did you give a clear overview (1–2 sentences,
   usually right after the intro, stating the main trend/comparison
   *before* any supporting detail)? Overview is the single most-missed
   requirement — omit it and you're capped at Band 5.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping of information (by
   category, by time period, by biggest-to-smallest), not just
   chronological dumping. Paragraphs, not one block.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of reporting/trend language used
   accurately: *rose, climbed, surged, plummeted, fluctuated, stagnated,
   a gradual/sharp/marginal increase/decline*. Repeating "increased" six
   times is a Band 6 ceiling regardless of everything else.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data (past
   for historical data, present for a current snapshot/process/map with
   no date, "will" only for a labeled future projection); accurate
   comparative/superlative forms.

## Required structure (every response, every time)

1. **Paraphrase the question** (1 sentence) — restate what the
   chart/graph/table/process/map shows, in your own words.
2. **Overview** (1–2 sentences) — the 2–3 most significant
   features/trends, with *no numbers yet*.
3. **Body paragraph 1** — group of related data, with specific figures.
4. **Body paragraph 2** — the other group, with specific figures and at
   least one direct comparison between the two groups.

No conclusion, no opinion, no speculation about *why* the data looks this
way (unless a process diagram explicitly asks you to explain a stage) —
Task 1 is description, Task 2 is argument. Mixing them up is a common
Band 6 tell.

## Language bank (use, don't just read)

- **Trend verbs, rising:** rose, increased, climbed, surged, soared,
  shot up, picked up
- **Trend verbs, falling:** fell, declined, dropped, plummeted, slumped,
  tailed off
- **Trend verbs, flat:** remained stable, stagnated, leveled off,
  plateaued, held steady
- **Degree/speed modifiers:** sharply, dramatically, steadily, gradually,
  marginally, slightly, significantly
- **Process language:** is then, after which, once X has been done,
  during this stage, finally
- **Comparison:** by contrast, whereas, in contrast to, more than double,
  roughly half, a marginal difference of

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Find your overview sentence. Does it contain a specific number? If yes,
  delete the number and move it to a body paragraph — overview is trends
  only.
- Count distinct trend verbs used. Fewer than 3 different ones in a
  150-word response is a lexical-range problem, not bad luck.
- Check your tense matches the data type (historical line graph = past;
  dateless process diagram = present passive).
- Did you compare the two data sets directly at least once ("X was more
  than double Y" / "unlike Country A, Country B saw a steady decline")?
  No direct comparison = a Coherence & Cohesion penalty.
