# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. This task
is worth the same as Task 2 in your overall Writing band, but most
candidates under-practice it because it "feels easier" — that
overconfidence is exactly why people plateau at Band 6 here while scoring
higher on Task 2.

## What Task 1 actually tests (and what it doesn't)

It does **not** test whether you can explain every number in the data. It
tests whether you can:
1. Identify and report the **overall trend(s)** (this is the single
   highest-leverage sentence in the whole response — missing it caps you
   at Band 5 regardless of everything else).
2. Select and report the **most significant features**, not every data
   point — Task 1 rewards selection and synthesis, not transcription.
3. Make accurate comparisons using correct grammar for data description
   (comparatives, "whereas," "while," trend verbs + adverbs).
4. Organize the response logically (overview, then grouped detail
   paragraphs — never a sentence-by-sentence narration in data order).

## Self-grading rubric (score 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Is there a clear overview paragraph summarizing
   the 2–3 main trends/features? No overview = capped at Band 5.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (by category, by time
   period, by trend direction) rather than listing data in the order it
   appears in the source.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Varied trend vocabulary (rose/climbed/surged vs.
   fell/declined/plummeted vs. remained stable/fluctuated), used with
   accurate degree (don't call a 2% change a "surge").
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data's time
   frame (past tense for historical data, present for a snapshot,
   present/future for a predicted/process diagram), accurate comparatives.

## Prompts (do one per session, rotate types — describe what you're given in your own words first, then write)

### Type 1: Line graph (trend over time)

**1.** The line graph below shows the percentage of households in three
countries (Japan, Brazil, and Germany) with internet access from 2000 to
2020.

> Data to describe: Japan rose steadily from 30% (2000) to 93% (2020),
> with the steepest increase 2000–2010 (30%→82%) and a plateau 2010–2020
> (82%→93%). Brazil started lowest at 5% (2000), overtook no one until
> 2015, and reached 74% by 2020 with consistent, near-linear growth
> throughout. Germany started highest at 35% (2000), grew steadily to
> 95% by 2012, then plateaued completely through 2020 (95%→96%).

**2.** The graph shows average monthly electricity consumption (in kWh)
for a household in one city across four seasons, compared between 2010
and 2020.

> Data to describe: Winter consumption rose from 850 kWh (2010) to 1100
> kWh (2020). Summer consumption rose more sharply, from 600 kWh (2010)
> to 1250 kWh (2020), overtaking winter as the peak season by 2020.
> Spring and autumn both remained relatively stable (around 500–600 kWh)
> across the decade with only minor fluctuation.

### Type 2: Bar chart (comparison across categories)

**3.** The bar chart shows the percentage of total energy production from
five sources (coal, natural gas, nuclear, wind, solar) in one country in
2000 and 2020.

> Data to describe: Coal fell from 50% (2000) to 15% (2020). Natural gas
> rose from 25% to 30%. Nuclear stayed roughly flat (18%→17%). Wind rose
> from near 0% (2%) to 20%. Solar rose from near 0% (1%) to 18%,
> essentially starting from nothing to become a major source.

### Type 3: Process diagram (no time frame — use present tense passive voice)

**4.** The diagram below shows the process of water treatment at a
municipal plant, from intake to distribution.

> Stages to describe: (1) Raw water intake from a river/reservoir, (2)
> screening to remove large debris, (3) coagulation and sedimentation to
> remove suspended particles, (4) filtration through sand/carbon layers,
> (5) disinfection with chlorine or UV light, (6) storage in a clean-water
> reservoir, (7) distribution to households via pipelines.

### Type 4: Map (describing change between two time points — use past tense)

**5.** The maps show a small coastal town in 1990 and the same town in
2020.

> Changes to describe: a fishing harbor present in 1990 was expanded into
> a marina with tourist shops by 2020; a forested area north of the town
> in 1990 was cleared and replaced with a housing development by 2020; a
> single-lane road connecting the town to the highway in 1990 was widened
> into a dual carriageway by 2020; a small school present in 1990 remained
> unchanged in the same location in 2020.

## The overview sentence — the single habit worth drilling in isolation

Before writing the full response, practice writing **just the overview**
for each prompt above in under 2 minutes. A strong overview:
- Names the 2–3 most significant overall patterns (not every category).
- Contains no specific numbers (numbers belong in the body paragraphs).
- Could stand alone and still tell a reader "what this chart shows" at a
  glance.

**Example overview for Prompt 1:** "Overall, internet access increased
substantially in all three countries over the period, though Germany
started from and maintained the highest level throughout while Brazil,
starting far behind, saw the most consistent year-on-year growth."

## After you write: the honest 5-minute audit

- Find your overview sentence. If it contains a specific number, cut the
  number — overviews state patterns, not data points.
- Count how many individual data points you mentioned. More than ~6–8 in
  a 150-word response usually means you're transcribing, not selecting.
- Check your trend verbs match their actual magnitude (a change of 1–3%
  is not a "surge" or a "plummet").
- Check tense consistency: historical data = past tense throughout; a
  process diagram = present passive throughout — don't mix them.
