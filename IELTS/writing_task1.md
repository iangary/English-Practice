# IELTS Writing Task 1 (Academic) — Drill Set

**Rules every time:** 20 minutes, hard stop. 150 words minimum. No
calculator, no outline typed in advance — you get the data cold, exactly
like test day. This task is worth the same weight toward your Writing band
as Task 2, and it's the one most self-studiers under-practice because it
"feels easier." It isn't — it's a different skill (selection and
comparison, not argument), and sloppy Task 1s quietly cap a lot of
otherwise-Band-8 Writing scores.

## What this task actually tests

You are NOT asked to explain *why* the data looks the way it does, and you
are NOT asked for your opinion. You are graded on whether you can:

1. Identify the **overall trend/pattern** (this one sentence matters more
   than any other sentence in the response).
2. Select and report the **key features** — not every number, the ones
   that matter (highest/lowest, biggest change, notable comparison).
3. Make accurate **comparisons** between data points, using a range of
   comparative language (not just "higher than" repeated five times).
4. Organize it clearly (overview, then grouped detail paragraphs) in
   roughly 150–185 words — padding past ~200 costs Task 2 time for no
   extra credit.

## Self-grading rubric (score 0–9 on each, be harsh)

1. **Task Achievement** — Is there a clear overview statement identifying
   the main trend(s)? (No overview = automatic Band 5 ceiling, regardless
   of how good the rest is.) Are the key features selected, not just a
   list of every number in the data?
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — Logical grouping (by category, by time
   period, by trend direction) rather than describing the data in the
   order it was given. Clear paragraphing.
3. **Lexical Resource** — Range of trend/comparison vocabulary (see bank
   below). Repetition of "increased" five times in 150 words is a Band 6
   tell.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** — Correct tense for the data type
   (past tense for historical data, present for current/no-date data,
   future/modal for projections). Accurate use of comparatives,
   superlatives, and proportion language.

## Vocabulary bank (rotate these — don't default to one pair)

**Upward:** rose, increased, climbed, grew, surged, soared, picked up
**Downward:** fell, declined, dropped, decreased, slumped, plummeted, dipped
**No change:** remained stable, plateaued, leveled off, stayed constant
**Fluctuation:** fluctuated, varied, see-sawed
**Degree:** slightly, marginally, moderately, substantially, dramatically,
sharply, gradually, steadily
**Comparison:** in contrast, whereas, while, compared to, relative to,
more than double, roughly half, a similar proportion of
**Extremes:** peaked at, reached a high of, hit a low of, was at its
lowest/highest

## Prompts — data given as text, you write the description

Rotate task types; don't just drill your favorite one. If you dread maps
or process diagrams, that's the one to do next, not last.

### 1. Line graph (trend over time)

The data shows annual coffee consumption per capita (kg) in three
countries from 1990 to 2020:

| Year | Finland | Brazil | USA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1990 | 11.0 | 4.0 | 3.2 |
| 2000 | 11.5 | 4.8 | 3.5 |
| 2010 | 12.0 | 5.5 | 4.0 |
| 2020 | 12.3 | 6.2 | 4.2 |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

### 2. Bar chart (comparison across categories)

The chart shows the percentage of households with internet access in
four countries in 2005 and 2023:

| Country | 2005 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| South Korea | 78% | 99% |
| Germany | 62% | 96% |
| Brazil | 14% | 81% |
| Nigeria | 1% | 42% |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

### 3. Pie charts (proportion, two time points)

The pie charts show the breakdown of a university's energy consumption
by source in 2000 and 2024:

**2000:** Coal 55%, Natural gas 25%, Hydro 12%, Solar/Wind 2%, Other 6%
**2024:** Coal 10%, Natural gas 30%, Hydro 15%, Solar/Wind 40%, Other 5%

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

### 4. Table (multiple variables, no visual shape to lean on)

The table shows average weekly hours spent on three activities by age
group in one country:

| Age group | Screen time | Exercise | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13–17 | 32 | 3 | 2 |
| 18–30 | 28 | 4 | 1.5 |
| 31–50 | 18 | 5 | 3 |
| 51+ | 14 | 6 | 5 |

Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features,
and make comparisons where relevant.

(Tables are the hardest Task 1 type precisely because there's no
pre-drawn shape — you must impose the organization yourself. Practicing
this one directly builds the "overview first" instinct the others let you
fake.)

### 5. Process diagram (no numbers, sequence instead)

The diagram shows the process of how glass bottles are recycled, in six
stages:

1. Bottles collected from household recycling bins
2. Transported to a sorting facility; sorted by color
3. Crushed into small pieces ("cullet")
4. Cullet washed to remove contaminants
5. Melted in a furnace at ~1,500°C
6. Molded into new bottles and cooled

Summarize the information by describing the main stages, and make
comparisons where relevant.

*(Process-diagram rule: use passive voice heavily — "bottles are
collected," "cullet is melted" — since there's no human subject named,
and use sequencers: first, next, following this, subsequently, finally,
at the final stage.)*

### 6. Maps (before/after, no numbers at all)

The maps show a town center in 1980 and the same area in 2024.

**1980:** A single main road runs north–south; a small market square at
the center; residential housing on both sides; a river to the east with
no bridge.

**2024:** The main road has been widened to four lanes; the market
square has been replaced by a shopping mall; a new bridge crosses the
river to the east, connecting to a new residential district; a park has
been added south of the mall.

Summarize the information by describing the main changes, and make
comparisons where relevant.

## After you write: the honest 10-minute audit

- Underline your overview sentence. Is it in paragraph 2 (right after a
  one-line intro), and does it name the single biggest pattern WITHOUT
  any specific numbers? If your overview has a number in it, it's not an
  overview, it's a detail that escaped.
- Count how many exact data points you copied in verbatim vs. how many
  you grouped ("the two lowest-ranked countries both..."). More than
  ~60% verbatim copying means you're listing, not selecting.
- Check tense consistency — did you drift from past to present mid-
  description for historical data?
- Read it back and count repeated trend words. Replace any word used
  3+ times with a synonym from the bank above.
