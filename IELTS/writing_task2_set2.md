# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Drill Set 2 (Band 7.5–8.5 tier)

Set 1 built the skeleton: thesis, paragraphing, linking. These 10 prompts
are harder on purpose — abstract topics, two-part questions buried
inside one sentence, and themes where a generic template answer visibly
falls apart. **Same rules: 40 minutes, hard stop, no spellcheck.** If
you're consistently hitting Band 7 on Set 1, grade yourself harder here
— these are where Band 8 is actually won or lost.

## What's different about this set

- Several prompts have **two distinct questions** disguised as one
  sentence. Missing either half caps Task Response at Band 6, no matter
  how fluent the prose is — reread each prompt twice before writing.
- Several topics resist a confident, simple position. That's
  deliberate: a nuanced position ("mostly A, but B in specific
  circumstances") scores higher than a flattened one, provided it's
  still clearly stated, not wishy-washy.

## Prompts

**Abstract / values-based:**
1. Some people believe that success in life is primarily the result of
   hard work, while others believe it is mostly a matter of luck and
   circumstances beyond a person's control. Discuss both views and give
   your own opinion.
2. It is sometimes said that the pursuit of happiness has become the
   central goal of modern life, at the expense of other values such as
   duty or achievement. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Hidden two-part questions:**
3. Advances in artificial intelligence are expected to automate a large
   number of jobs within the next few decades. What problems might this
   cause, and how can governments and individuals prepare for this
   change?
4. In many countries, young people are increasingly choosing to delay
   marriage and parenthood. What do you think are the reasons for this
   trend, and is it a positive or negative development for society?

**Policy / trade-off (resist a simple answer):**
5. Some argue that governments should restrict advertising aimed at
   children, while others believe parents alone should be responsible
   for managing their children's exposure to advertising. Discuss both
   views and give your own opinion.
6. As remote work becomes more common, some argue that companies should
   be required to give employees a legal "right to disconnect" outside
   working hours. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this
   policy?

**Environment / long-horizon trade-offs:**
7. Some people believe that individual actions, such as recycling or
   reducing personal consumption, have little real impact on climate
   change compared to action by governments and large corporations.
   To what extent do you agree or disagree?
8. Many countries are investing heavily in renewable energy, even where
   it is currently more expensive than fossil fuels. Do you think this
   is a reasonable use of public money, or should other priorities come
   first?

**Education / two-part:**
9. Some universities now offer most of their courses online rather than
   in person. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this trend
   for students, and should it continue to expand?
10. Standardized testing is used in many countries to determine
    university admission. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of
    relying on standardized tests for this purpose.

## Harder self-audit (apply after the Set 1 checklist)

- **Position precision:** Find the exact sentence stating your
  position. If a stranger read only that sentence, could they predict
  your entire essay's stance? If not, it's buried or too vague.
- **Nuance without hedging:** If your position is "it depends," does
  the essay specify *on what* it depends, with a concrete example per
  condition? "It depends on the situation" with no elaboration reads as
  Band 6 evasion, not Band 8 nuance.
- **Two-part check:** For prompts 3, 4, 9, and 10 specifically — circle
  where you answered the *first* question and where you answered the
  *second*. If you can't find one of the circles, you've written a
  Band-6-capped partial response no matter the prose quality.
- **Idea density per paragraph:** Each body paragraph should survive
  having its topic sentence deleted — if the paragraph is still
  readable as making one clear point, the idea was well-developed. If
  deleting the topic sentence leaves a vague paragraph, you under-
  developed the point and padded instead.

Log the band estimate, which half of a two-part question (if any) you
underweighted, and whether your position was precise or hedged, in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
