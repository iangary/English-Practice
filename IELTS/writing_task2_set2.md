# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Drill Set 2 (Band 7.5+ Standard)

**Prerequisite:** You should have completed at least 4 essays from
`writing_task2.md` (Set 1) and self-graded honestly before starting
this set. If your Set 1 average is below Band 7, go back and fix that
first — these prompts are harder to structure, not easier, and will
mostly expose the same weaknesses at a worse angle.

**What's different about this set:** every prompt below combines two
task types or hides an extra demand inside the wording. Markers
specifically watch for whether a Band 8–9 candidate catches the
compound/hidden instruction that a Band 6 candidate answers only
partially. Read each prompt twice before planning.

**Rules:** same as Set 1 — 40 minutes, hard stop, 250+ words, no
spellcheck.

## Raise-the-bar self-grading rubric

Use this instead of Set 1's rubric once you're consistently scoring
Band 7+. It's stricter on the same four criteria:

1. **Task Response (Band 8 bar):** every part of the question answered
   *with a clear, specific position* — not just "there are advantages
   and disadvantages" without ever stating which side you lean toward
   when the prompt asks for your opinion.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion (Band 8 bar):** a reader should be able to
   predict your next sentence's *function* (not content) from the one
   before it, without needing a linking word to signal it. If every
   transition needs "Furthermore/However/In addition" to hold together,
   the underlying logic isn't doing the work yet.
3. **Lexical Resource (Band 8 bar):** zero words you're not 100% sure
   of. At this level, one wrong collocation ("make an advantage" instead
   of "gain an advantage") in an otherwise strong essay is enough for a
   marker to cap Lexical Resource at 7.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (Band 8 bar):** at least one correctly
   formed complex conditional, one correctly punctuated non-restrictive
   clause, and zero run-ons — count these explicitly after writing.

## Prompts — compound/hidden-demand type

1. Some people believe that the increasing use of robots and artificial
   intelligence in the workplace will benefit society, while others
   believe it will create more problems than it solves for workers. Discuss
   both views and give your own opinion, **and** suggest one measure
   governments could take to address the view you find less convincing.
   *(Hidden demand: this is not a pure "discuss both views" — it also
   requires a specific policy suggestion. Many candidates answer only
   the discuss-both-views half.)*

2. In some countries, average levels of personal debt are increasing.
   What do you think are the causes of this, **and** what problems can
   this trend cause for individuals and for the economy as a whole?
   *(Hidden demand: two separate effect categories — individual AND
   economy-wide — both must be addressed, not merged into one vague
   paragraph.)*

3. Some people think that the best way to reduce crime is to give
   longer prison sentences. Others, however, believe there are better
   alternative ways of reducing crime. Discuss both views and give your
   own opinion, **specifying at least one concrete alternative** rather
   than describing alternatives only in general terms.

4. It is sometimes argued that funding for the arts (music, theatre,
   painting) should come entirely from private sponsorship rather than
   government funding, since this would not cost taxpayers money. To
   what extent do you agree or disagree **with both the claim and its
   stated justification** (i.e., even if you agree funding should shift,
   do you agree the taxpayer-cost argument is the right reason)?
   *(Hidden demand: this prompt asks you to evaluate the reasoning, not
   just the conclusion — a surprisingly common trap in "to what extent"
   prompts.)*

5. Some people think that the government should provide free healthcare
   for all citizens, regardless of their income. To what extent do you
   agree or disagree? Discuss the implications of your view for
   **both** quality of care and the national budget.

## After you write: the Band 8 audit (stricter than Set 1's)

- Find the sentence where you address the *hidden* second demand in
  each prompt above. If you can't point to one specific sentence or
  paragraph, you didn't fully answer the question — this alone caps you
  at Band 6, regardless of language quality.
- Read your essay and delete every linking word one at a time. Does the
  paragraph still make logical sense without it? If yes for all of
  them, your cohesion was coming from the words, not the logic — that's
  a Band 6–7 ceiling, not Band 8.
- Highlight your most complex sentence. Is it complex because it says
  something that genuinely needs that structure, or because you added
  clauses to look sophisticated? Markers can tell the difference.
- Time how long you spent on the conclusion. Under 3 minutes for a
  conclusion that does real synthesis work (not just restating the
  intro) is a skill — if yours reads like a restatement, it probably
  was rushed, not naturally short.
