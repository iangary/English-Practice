# IELTS Writing Task 2 — Drill Set 2 (Band 8–9 calibration)

**The difficulty increase in this set is not the topics — it's the
clock and the standard.** Set 1 gave you 40 minutes and a Band 7 bar.
This set gives you **35 minutes, hard stop**, and the self-grading
standard below is calibrated to Band 8–9, not Band 7. If you've been
averaging Band 7 on Set 1 with 40 minutes, expect this set to feel
noticeably harder even though the prompts aren't more obscure — that's
the point. The real exam doesn't get easier because you're tired on
test day; practice at a harder standard than the real thing demands.

## Raise-the-bar self-grading rubric

Score 0–9 per criterion, but hold yourself to these Band 8 thresholds
specifically (not the general band descriptors — the specific tells):

1. **Task Response (Band 8 bar):** Your position is clear from sentence
   one of the introduction, not just inferable by the conclusion. Every
   body paragraph's topic sentence could stand alone and tell a reader
   exactly what that paragraph argues, with no vague topic sentences
   ("There are many reasons why...").
2. **Coherence & Cohesion (Band 8 bar):** Zero paragraphs where you could
   delete the first sentence without losing the paragraph's logic. Ideas
   build on each other across paragraphs (paragraph 3 should reference or
   contrast with paragraph 2's point, not just sit next to it).
3. **Lexical Resource (Band 8 bar):** At least 3 instances of precise,
   less-common word choice used *correctly and naturally* (not forced in
   to show off — a wrong but fancy word scores worse than a correct
   simple one). Zero repeated content words across the essay.
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (Band 8 bar):** At least 2 different
   complex structures beyond basic subordination (e.g., inversion for
   emphasis, a cleft sentence, a non-restrictive relative clause used
   correctly) with zero errors in them. One error anywhere in a complex
   structure undoes the credit you'd otherwise get for attempting it.

## Prompts — harder because they resist a templated answer

**Opinion, with a trap (both "agree" and "disagree" are easy to argue
badly — the trap is picking a side and then not actually defending it):**
1. Some people believe that modern technology is making people less
   capable of independent thought. To what extent do you agree?
2. It is sometimes argued that competitive sports do more to divide
   people than unite them. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**Discuss both views, where the "balanced" answer is the lazy one:**
3. Some people think that the best way to reduce crime is to give longer
   prison sentences. Others believe there are better alternative methods.
   Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (Trap: a true "both
   sides are valid" conclusion without a clear personal stance caps you
   at Band 6 — Discuss Both Views still requires your own position.)
4. Some argue that it is the responsibility of individuals to protect
   the environment, while others believe governments should bear this
   responsibility. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

**Two-part question with parts that pull in different directions:**
5. In some countries, young people are leaving rural areas to live in
   cities in increasing numbers. What problems does this cause in rural
   areas, and what problems does it cause in the cities they move to?
   (Trap: answering only one city/rural half and treating the other as
   an afterthought is the single most common Task Response failure on
   this question type.)

**Abstract prompt, hardest category to avoid vagueness on:**
6. Some people believe that success in life comes from taking risks,
   while others think careful planning is the key to success. Discuss
   both views and give your own opinion.

## The 35-minute protocol

- **Minutes 0–4:** Plan. Write your thesis as one complete sentence
  before writing anything else. If you can't state it in one sentence
  at minute 4, you don't have a position yet — don't start writing body
  paragraphs without one.
- **Minutes 4–30:** Write. No going back to "fix" the introduction once
  you've moved past it — that's a Set 1 habit; at this level you plan
  well enough the first time that you don't need to.
- **Minutes 30–35:** Proofread only. Check: subject-verb agreement,
  article usage (a/an/the — the single most common Band 6–7 ceiling for
  non-native patterns), and that your conclusion doesn't introduce a new
  idea (a Band 6 tell).

## After writing: the Band 8 gap-check

Band 7 and Band 8 essays often have the same ideas. What separates them:
- Does paragraph 2 explicitly engage with (not just sit beside)
  paragraph 3's point, or do they read as two unrelated mini-essays?
- Is there a single sentence anywhere that a Band 6 writer could not
  have produced — one piece of genuinely precise phrasing, not just
  correct phrasing?
- Would removing your weakest sentence make the essay stronger? If yes,
  you already know what to cut next time — cut it before you submit,
  not after.

Log your self-assessed band **and** the single sentence you'd cut, in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`. The cut sentence matters more than the score — it's
the specific, actionable thing Set 1's log never captured.
