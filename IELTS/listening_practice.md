# IELTS Listening — Drill Set

**Timer: 30 minutes for the 4 sections below + 10 minutes to transfer
answers to a separate answer sheet — do the transfer for real, by hand.
On the real test the transfer time is where people lose easy marks to
sloppy handwriting and misnumbering, so don't skip practicing it.**

You don't have audio here, so simulate the exam honestly: read each
transcript **once, at a steady pace, out loud or in your head at normal
speaking speed**, cover it, and answer the questions from memory/notes
only. Re-reading the transcript before answering defeats the entire
point of the drill — Listening tests capture-on-first-pass, not reading
comprehension.

## Section 3 — Academic discussion (two speakers, multiple choice + matching)

> **Transcript:**
> **Tutor:** "So, you've both chosen to look at urban heat islands for
> your joint presentation. Sarah, what's your angle?"
> **Sarah:** "I want to focus on the vegetation side — how tree cover and
> green roofs measurably lower surface temperatures. There's good data
> from Chicago's green roof program showing a real difference."
> **Tutor:** "And you, David?"
> **David:** "I'm more interested in the materials side — reflective
> pavement coatings, that kind of thing. Though, actually, after reading
> Sarah's sources, I think the strongest version of our argument combines
> both — materials alone don't address humidity effects the way
> vegetation does."
> **Sarah:** "Right, and conversely, vegetation alone is slower to scale
> city-wide than resurfacing roads, which city governments can do faster
> than growing mature trees."
> **Tutor:** "So it sounds like your presentation's thesis is really
> about complementary strategies rather than picking one approach."
> **David:** "Exactly — we were originally going to argue for 'the best
> single solution,' but we've actually changed our minds on that
> framing entirely."
> **Sarah:** "One thing I'm still unsure about is how to handle the cost
> comparison — reflective coatings are cheaper up front but need
> reapplication every few years, while trees cost more initially but the
> cooling benefit compounds over decades."
> **Tutor:** "That's worth a slide on its own — don't bury it in the
> conclusion."

**Questions 11–14 (multiple choice):**

11. What was David and Sarah's original planned thesis?
 (A) Complementary strategies (B) A single best solution (C) Cost comparison only

12. What does David say is a limitation of materials-only approaches?
 (A) They're too expensive (B) They don't address humidity (C) They take too long to install

13. What does Sarah say is a limitation of vegetation-only approaches?
 (A) Slower to scale city-wide (B) No cooling effect (C) Requires reapplication

14. What does the tutor recommend about the cost comparison?
 (A) Leave it out (B) Give it its own slide (C) Put it only in the conclusion

## Section 4 — Academic lecture (sentence completion, higher difficulty)

> **Transcript:**
> "Today I want to talk about migratory bird navigation, specifically the
> magnetoreception hypothesis. For decades, researchers have known that
> many migratory species can detect the Earth's magnetic field, but the
> actual mechanism remained unclear until relatively recently. Two
> competing theories have dominated the field. The first proposes that
> iron-based particles, specifically magnetite, located in the bird's
> beak or inner ear, act as a kind of internal compass needle. The second,
> more recent theory, involves a light-sensitive protein called
> cryptochrome, found in the retina, which is thought to produce a
> chemical reaction sensitive to magnetic fields — meaning birds might, in
> a sense, actually *see* the magnetic field as a visual overlay, rather
> than feel it. Recent experiments have favoured the cryptochrome theory,
> partly because disrupting a bird's vision — even temporarily — impairs
> its magnetic orientation far more reliably than removing the
> magnetite-containing tissue does. However, researchers caution against
> fully discarding the magnetite hypothesis; it's possible both systems
> operate together, with cryptochrome providing general directional
> information and magnetite providing something more like positional, or
> 'map,' information — the bird's sense of where it is, not just which
> way is north."

**Questions 15–20 (sentence completion, NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS each):**

15. The magnetite hypothesis locates the relevant particles in the beak
    or the bird's ________.
16. The cryptochrome protein is found in the bird's ________.
17. Cryptochrome is described as allowing birds to possibly ________ the
    magnetic field rather than feel it.
18. Disrupting a bird's ________ impairs orientation more reliably than
    removing magnetite tissue.
19. Magnetite may provide ________ information rather than directional
    information.
20. The lecturer's overall conclusion is that both systems may
    ________ rather than one replacing the other.
