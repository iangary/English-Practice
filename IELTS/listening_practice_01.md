# IELTS Listening — Drill Set 1

**Honest limitation up front:** real IELTS Listening practice requires
audio — natural pacing, accents, hesitations, and the fact that you only
hear it once are the entire skill being tested. A text file cannot
replace that. Use the official Cambridge IELTS practice test audio (or
the free sample tests on the British Council / IDP websites) for your
real listening reps. What this file *can* still train, with no audio
needed, is the two things that sink scores even when hearing isn't the
problem: **question-type recognition** and **answer-format discipline**.
Drill those here; drill your ears there.

## Protocol for this file

Read each transcript **exactly once**, at normal reading speed, start to
finish, with the questions covered. Only then uncover the questions and
answer from memory — don't scroll back up to "check." That's the one
rule that makes this exercise worth anything: on the real test you get
one pass, period.

## Set 1 — Section 1 style (everyday conversation, form completion)

**Transcript (read once):**

> **Agent:** Thanks for calling Lakeside Community Centre, how can I help?
> **Caller:** Hi, I'd like to sign my daughter up for the after-school art
> class. I saw it advertised on your noticeboard.
> **Agent:** Sure — that's the Tuesday and Thursday sessions, 4 to 5:30
> pm, for ages 8 to 12. Can I get her full name?
> **Caller:** It's Maya Thornbury. T-H-O-R-N-B-U-R-Y.
> **Agent:** Got it. And a contact number?
> **Caller:** 0797 224 6618.
> **Agent:** And the fee is £45 for the ten-week term, but there's a 10%
> discount if you also have a sibling enrolled in any other programme
> here.
> **Caller:** Oh — my son's actually in the Saturday swimming class.
> **Agent:** Perfect, that qualifies, so it'll be £40.50 total. One more
> thing — classes start the week of the 14th, but we need all materials
> fees paid by the 10th, in cash or by card at the front desk, not over
> the phone.
> **Caller:** Got it, I'll come by before the 10th then.
> **Agent:** Great, and just so you know, the room's changed this term —
> it's now Room 4B instead of the usual art room, since that's being
> renovated.

**Questions (form completion — answer in NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS/A NUMBER):**

1. Child's surname: ___________
2. Contact number: ___________
3. Fee after discount: ___________
4. Payment deadline: ___________
5. New room number: ___________

### Answer key
1. Thornbury
2. 0797 224 6618
3. £40.50
4. the 10th (of [this month])
5. 4B / Room 4B

**Common trap check:** did you write "£45" because it was the first
number you heard, instead of the corrected final figure? IELTS Section 1
almost always includes one self-correction or discount like this
specifically to catch listeners who stop processing once they hear the
"obvious" answer.

## Set 2 — Section 3 style (academic discussion, multiple choice)

**Transcript (read once):**

> **Tutor:** So you two are presenting on urban beekeeping next week —
> how's the research going?
> **Student A:** Good, I think. We found that city bee colonies often
> actually outperform rural ones in honey yield, which surprised us.
> **Student B:** Yeah, the theory is it's because cities have more
> diverse flowering plants year-round — gardens, parks, even weeds in
> pavement cracks — compared to rural areas that might be dominated by a
> single crop that only flowers for a few weeks.
> **Tutor:** Interesting. Is that your main argument, then?
> **Student A:** We were going to lead with that, but I think the
> stronger point is actually about pesticide exposure — rural bees near
> farmland get hit much harder by agricultural pesticides than urban
> colonies do.
> **Student B:** I'm not totally sold on making that the main point
> though — the data we found was from only two studies, both in the same
> country. The flower diversity point had more sources behind it.
> **Tutor:** That's a fair concern — for a presentation, I'd actually
> prioritize breadth of evidence over which point sounds more dramatic.
> **Student A:** Okay, fair — let's lead with diversity, then, and
> mention pesticides as a secondary factor worth more research.

**Questions (multiple choice — one answer):**

6. What will the students present as their MAIN argument?
   A. Pesticide exposure differences
   B. Flower diversity in urban areas
   C. Honey yield comparisons
   D. The tutor's personal opinion

7. Why did they decide against leading with the pesticide point?
   A. The tutor said it was factually wrong
   B. It was based on limited evidence (two studies, one country)
   C. Student A refused to research it further
   D. It contradicted the flower diversity finding

### Answer key
6. B — note the discussion *changes direction mid-conversation*; the
   initial lead ("pesticide exposure") is explicitly overturned near the
   end. This is the single most common Section 3 trap: answering based
   on what was said first instead of tracking the conversation to its
   actual conclusion.
7. B

## Why this matters even without audio

Both traps above — the self-correction in Set 1 and the opinion reversal
in Set 3 — are the two most frequent reasons strong listeners still lose
points: they lock in an answer the moment they hear *a* plausible number
or claim, instead of continuing to track whether it gets revised. Train
yourself to hold every answer as provisional until the speaker moves to
the next topic. Then go do the real thing with real audio.
