# IELTS Listening — Drill Set 1 (Section 1 & Section 4 style)

**Protocol — this matters more than usual:** On the real test, audio
plays **exactly once**, and you read the questions *before* the audio
starts (you get preview time between sections). Simulate this honestly:

1. Read the questions for the section first (you get ~30–45 seconds
   on the real test — time yourself).
2. Read the transcript below **one time only, at normal reading
   speed, start to finish, without pausing or re-reading any line.**
   Do not peek ahead to the questions mid-transcript — note answers as
   you pass the relevant point, exactly as you would while listening.
3. Stop. Do not re-read the transcript before finalizing your answers.
   This single-pass discipline is the actual skill being tested — IELTS
   Listening scores are suppressed far more often by "I'll catch it on
   the second listen" habits (which don't exist on test day) than by
   not knowing the vocabulary.

### Answer Key

1. Harrington 2. Off-Peak (membership) 3. 14th (of next month) /
the 14th 4. 0797 224 3310 5. Priya Nair 6. WELCOME20

**Common traps to notice on review:** Q2 requires catching that the
caller changes their mind mid-sentence (asks about standard/annual,
then specifically requests "Off-Peak" instead) — IELTS Section 1
routinely includes a correction or change of mind, and the *final*
stated answer is always the one that counts, never the first one
mentioned.

## Section 4 — Note completion (academic lecture monologue)

**Questions 7–13:** Complete the notes below. Write **ONE WORD ONLY**
for each answer.

### Lecture notes: Coral Reef Bleaching

- Coral bleaching occurs when coral expels the __(7)__ living in its
  tissue.
- These organisms normally provide coral with up to __(8)__ percent of
  its energy.
- The main trigger for bleaching is a rise in sea __(9)__, even a small
  one.
- Bleached coral is not dead, but is more vulnerable to __(10)__.
- The 2016 event discussed affected a large section of the __(11)__
  Barrier Reef.
- Recovery is possible if conditions __(12)__ within a few weeks.
- The lecturer's main concern is the growing __(13)__ of bleaching
  events, leaving less time for recovery between them.

### Transcript (read once)

> "Today I want to talk about coral bleaching, which is one of the
> clearest visible signs of ocean stress linked to climate change. So,
> corals aren't actually plants — they're animals — but they live in a
> close partnership with tiny algae called zooxanthellae, which live
> inside the coral's tissue. This relationship is what gives coral its
> vivid color, and crucially, these algae photosynthesize and pass a
> huge amount of the energy they produce directly to the coral — up to
> ninety percent of the coral's energy needs come from this
> partnership. Bleaching happens when the coral becomes stressed,
> usually by a rise in sea temperature of just one or two degrees
> sustained over several weeks, and in response, the coral expels the
> algae living in its tissue. Without the algae, the coral turns
> white — hence 'bleaching' — and loses its main energy source. Now,
> I want to be clear: bleached coral is not necessarily dead coral. It's
> a stressed, starving coral, which makes it significantly more
> vulnerable to disease, but if temperatures drop back to normal within
> a few weeks, the coral can reabsorb algae and recover. The event I
> want to focus on today is the mass bleaching that affected a large
> stretch of Australia's Great Barrier Reef in 2016 — one of the most
> severe on record. What concerns researchers most isn't a single severe
> event like that one, though — it's the increasing frequency of these
> events. Historically, reefs had roughly a decade between severe
> bleaching events to recover. Now some reefs are experiencing them only
> a few years apart, which doesn't leave enough time for the ecosystem
> to bounce back before the next shock hits."

### Answer Key

7. algae 8. ninety 9. temperature 10. disease 11. Great
12. drop / normalize (accept "drop") 13. frequency

### Scoring

11–13/13 = Band 8+ pace · 8–10 = Band 7 · 6–7 = Band 6 · below 6: check
whether your misses are concentrated in the back half of the transcript
(a sign you're still processing earlier sentences and falling behind —
drill shorter note-taking, not full sentences) or scattered throughout
(a sign of vocabulary gaps — review the topic-based collocations in
`vocabulary.md`).
