# IELTS Listening — Drill Set (Section 1 + Section 3 style)

**You cannot self-administer real audio from a text file — don't let that
become an excuse to skip Listening entirely, which is what most
self-studiers do and then get blindsided by it on test day.** This drill
uses the transcript-reading method below to train the two skills that
actually determine your Listening band: **predicting answer type before
you hear it**, and **not losing your place when the speaker paraphrases**.
It is a supplement, not a replacement — pair it with real IELTS Listening
audio (Cambridge IELTS 15–19 practice tests or the British Council's free
practice tests) at least twice a week.

## How to run this drill (read the method before you touch the transcript)

1. Read the **questions only** first. For each blank, predict: what type
   of word fills it (number? name? single word? max two words?). This is
   the single highest-leverage Listening skill — real exam answers are
   almost always exactly the word type the question format demands.
2. Cover the transcript. Have someone read it aloud to you at a normal
   pace (or use a text-to-speech app) ONCE, no pausing, no rereading —
   that's the actual exam condition.
3. Fill in what you caught. Do not peek.
4. THEN reveal the transcript and check. For every miss, find the exact
   sentence and note *why* you missed it (distractor before the real
   answer? paraphrase you didn't catch? spelling?).

If you have no one to read it aloud and no TTS tool, read it once
yourself at a brisk, steady pace without stopping or backtracking — it's
weaker than audio but still trains prediction and paraphrase-tracking.

## Section 3 — Multiple choice + matching (academic discussion)

**Questions 9–14.** Choose the correct letter, A, B, or C.

**9.** The students are mainly meeting to discuss:
A. choosing a topic for their group presentation
B. dividing up research tasks for an existing topic
C. their disagreement over last week's lecture

**10.** Priya thinks the topic of "urban green spaces" is:
A. too broad for a 15-minute presentation
B. already covered by another group
C. the strongest option they've discussed

**11.** What does the tutor's feedback (mentioned by Marcus) warn against?
A. using too many statistics
B. presenting only one side of an argument
C. exceeding the time limit

**12.** Who will research the case study on Singapore?
A. Priya
B. Marcus
C. both of them together

**13.** The group decides their presentation will NOT include:
A. a cost-benefit analysis
B. a historical comparison
C. audience questions at the end

**14.** What is their next concrete action after this conversation?
A. email the tutor for approval
B. book a room to rehearse
C. split up to start individual research

PRIYA: So, for the group presentation — I still think urban green spaces

## Scoring and what to do with your misses

5–6/6 per section = Band 8+ pace · 4/6 = Band 7 pace · below 4 = Band 6
or lower.

Sort every miss into exactly one bucket, in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`:
- **Prediction failure** — you didn't know what word type to listen for,
  so you heard the right word and didn't recognize it as the answer.
- **Paraphrase failure** — the audio said something different from the
  question's wording and you were listening for the question's exact
  words instead of the meaning.
- **Distractor failure** — the speaker said something that sounded right
  first, then corrected or contradicted it, and you wrote down the first
  thing (this is Section 3's signature trap — see Q10/Q13 above).

These three failure modes need different fixes — don't lump them into
"I need to listen more carefully."
