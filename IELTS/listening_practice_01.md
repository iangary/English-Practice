# IELTS Listening — Practice Set 1 (All 4 Section Types)

**Timer: 30 minutes for the recording simulation below + 10 minutes to
transfer answers. Real IELTS Listening plays audio once — do not re-read
any script section more than once before answering.** If you're doing
this with a partner, have them read each script aloud at natural
conversational speed while you take notes; if alone, read each script
exactly once, cover it, then answer from memory + notes.

Real IELTS Listening has 4 recordings, 40 questions total. This set
gives you one shortened recording per section type (18 questions) so you
can drill all four formats without a 30-minute time commitment every
session — do a full 40-question mock from an official source at least
once before any exam-condition week in the study plan.

## Section 1: Everyday conversation (Form completion)

**Context:** A customer calls a storage rental company to book a unit.

> **Agent:** Thanks for calling SafeSpace Storage, how can I help?
> **Customer:** Hi, I'd like to book a storage unit, starting from the
> first of next month.
> **Agent:** Sure. Can I get your full name?
> **Customer:** It's Daniela Marchetti — that's M-A-R-C-H-E-T-T-I.
> **Agent:** Great, and a contact number?
> **Customer:** 0477 219 346.
> **Agent:** And what size unit are you after? We've got small, 1x1
> metres, up to extra-large at 4x3.
> **Customer:** Probably a medium — I've got some furniture from a
> one-bedroom flat.
> **Agent:** No problem. Medium is 2x2 metres, and that runs at
> thirty-eight dollars a week. We also require a refundable deposit of
> one hundred dollars on booking.
> **Customer:** That's fine. Is there a discount for a longer booking?
> **Agent:** Yes — if you book for six months upfront you get a 10%
> discount on the weekly rate.
> **Customer:** I'll do that then. Oh, and is the facility accessible on
> weekends?
> **Agent:** Yes, access hours are 7am to 7pm, seven days a week, except
> public holidays.

## Section 2: Monologue (Map/notice — tour guide introduction)

**Context:** A guide welcomes visitors at the start of a museum tour.

> "Welcome everyone to the City History Museum. Before we start, a quick
> orientation. We're standing in the main entrance hall. If you go
> straight ahead, you'll reach the Natural History wing — that's
> currently closed for renovation, so skip that today. Instead, take the
> staircase on your left, which leads up to the Colonial Era exhibit on
> the first floor. Next to that, past the central atrium, is the Modern
> Art gallery — that one requires a separate ticket, which isn't
> included in today's group pass. On the ground floor, behind the gift
> shop, you'll find the café, which stays open until the museum closes
> at 6pm. Restrooms are located just past the information desk, to the
> right as you came in. We'll begin the guided portion in the Colonial
> Era exhibit in five minutes, so feel free to use the facilities now."

**Questions 6–9: Choose the correct answer.**

6. The Natural History wing is currently:
   A) the starting point of the tour
   B) closed for renovation
   C) requiring a separate ticket

7. To reach the Colonial Era exhibit, visitors should:
   A) go straight ahead from the entrance
   B) take the staircase on the left
   C) walk past the gift shop

8. Which exhibit requires an extra ticket not included in the group pass?
   A) Colonial Era
   B) Natural History
   C) Modern Art

9. Where are the restrooms located?
   A) behind the gift shop
   B) near the information desk
   C) inside the café

## Section 3: Academic discussion (Student + tutor)

**Context:** A student discusses a research proposal with her supervisor.

> **Tutor:** So, Mei, you wanted to talk about narrowing down your
> dissertation topic on urban transport?
> **Mei:** Yes — I was originally planning to cover public transport
> usage across the whole city, but I think that's too broad for the word
> count.
> **Tutor:** I'd agree. What are you thinking of narrowing it to?
> **Mei:** I was considering focusing just on cycling infrastructure, since
> that's something I'm personally interested in.
> **Tutor:** That's a reasonable angle, but be careful — there's already a
> huge amount of literature on cycling infrastructure in Northern European
> cities specifically. If you go that route, you'd need a genuinely new
> angle, maybe a comparison with a city that hasn't been studied as much.
> **Mei:** That's actually a good point. What about looking at how cycling
> infrastructure investment correlates with reported commuter stress
> levels? I don't think that's been done much.
> **Tutor:** Now that's more promising — it combines an under-researched
> angle with data you can actually collect through a survey. I'd recommend
> you also look at the methodology used in Chen's 2019 study — not on
> cycling, but the survey design on commuter wellbeing is a good template.
> **Mei:** I'll track that down. Should my survey be city-specific or try
> to cover multiple cities?
> **Tutor:** For a dissertation at this level, pick one city and go deep
> rather than spreading thin across several — depth is what gets rewarded
> at this stage, breadth is for a later, bigger project.

**Questions 10–13: Do the following statements agree with the
information given?**

Write TRUE, FALSE, or NOT GIVEN.

10. Mei's original topic covered the whole city's public transport usage.
11. The tutor says cycling infrastructure in Northern Europe is
    under-researched.
12. The tutor recommends Chen's 2019 study specifically for its findings
    on cycling infrastructure.
13. The tutor advises Mei to study multiple cities for better data
    coverage.

## Section 4: Academic lecture (Monologue, note completion)

**Context:** A lecture excerpt on the history of the printing press.

> "Today we're looking at the spread of movable-type printing in Europe.
> Gutenberg's press, developed around 1440 in Mainz, is often credited as
> the starting point, though movable type had already existed in East
> Asia for centuries beforehand — what made Gutenberg's version
> transformative in Europe specifically was the combination with an oil-
> based ink and a metal alloy durable enough for mass reuse of type
> pieces. Within fifty years of Gutenberg's press, printing had spread to
> over two hundred and fifty cities across Europe, which historians now
> consider one of the fastest technology diffusions of the pre-industrial
> era. The economic effect was significant: book prices fell by roughly
> two-thirds within a single generation, which in turn drove a sharp rise
> in literacy rates, particularly in urban trading centers where
> merchants had practical incentives to read contracts and records
> themselves rather than relying on scribes. One underappreciated
> consequence was standardization: before print, every hand-copied
> manuscript introduced small variations, but printing allowed, for the
> first time, thousands of identical copies of the same text — which
> some historians argue was a necessary precondition for the standardized
> spelling and grammar that later national languages adopted."

**Questions 14–18: Complete the notes. Write NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS for
each answer.**

- Gutenberg's press developed in Mainz, around the year __(14)__.
- Movable type existed earlier in __(15)__, but Gutenberg's version
  combined it with oil-based ink and a durable metal __(16)__.
- Within 50 years, printing reached over 250 European __(17)__.
- Book prices fell by about two-thirds within one __(18)__.

### Scoring
17–18/18 = Band 9 · 15–16 = Band 8 · 13–14 = Band 7 · 11–12 = Band 6.
Below 11: your problem is very likely **note-taking under time
pressure**, not listening comprehension in the abstract — on the second
pass, read the script at full natural speed out loud and check whether
you missed answers because you were still writing the previous one.

## Common failure modes to self-diagnose

- **Spelling slips on names/numbers (Section 1):** these are marked
  wrong even if you clearly "heard" them correctly — practice writing
  spelled-out letters and numbers at speed, not just recognizing them.
- **Losing your place in a monologue (Section 2/4):** practice tracking
  a numbered list of locations/points as they're mentioned, not after.
- **Paraphrase blindness (Section 3):** the recording will almost never
  use the exact word from the question — "under-researched" in the
  question may be spoken as "there's not much literature on." Train
  yourself to listen for meaning matches, not word matches.
