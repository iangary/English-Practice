# IELTS Listening — Drill Set 01

**You have no audio file, so simulate exam conditions properly instead of
reading the transcript as text (reading it defeats the entire point — you
need the skill of extracting information *while it's spoken and gone*):**

1. Use a phone/computer text-to-speech voice (or a friend) to read each
   section's script aloud, at a natural, uninterrupted pace, **once only**
   — no pausing, no rewinding. That "once only" constraint is the actual
   exam condition you're training for.
2. You read the questions below beforehand (you get this time on the real
   test too — roughly 30–45 seconds per set), then listen and answer
   in real time, writing as it plays.
3. Do not look at the transcript until you've attempted every question.

**Timing:** ~8 minutes of audio total across both sections below, plus the
real exam's 10-minute transfer time at the end (practice that transfer
step too — copying answers to an answer sheet under time pressure is
itself a skill people lose points on).

## Section 1 — Everyday conversation (Questions 1–5)

**Context:** A form-completion task. Listen for the information needed to
complete the gaps. Numbers, spellings, and dates are the most commonly
missed details in Section 1 — they're said quickly and only once.

**Script (read aloud once):**

> **Receptionist:** Good morning, Riverside Community Center, how can I
> help?
> **Caller:** Hi, I'd like to sign up for the pottery class advertised on
> your website.
> **Receptionist:** Sure, I can take your details. Can I get your full
> name first?
> **Caller:** It's Marianne Castellano — that's C-A-S-T-E-L-L-A-N-O.
> **Receptionist:** Got it. And a contact number?
> **Caller:** 0171 445 9823.
> **Receptionist:** Thanks. The pottery class runs on Thursday evenings,
> 6 to 8 PM, starting on the 14th of March. The fee is 85 pounds for the
> eight-week term, but there's a 10-pound discount if you pay the full
> amount before the first session rather than paying weekly.
> **Caller:** Great, I'll pay in full then. Do I need to bring my own
> materials?
> **Receptionist:** No, clay and tools are provided, but we do ask
> everyone to bring their own apron.

**Questions:**

1. Caller's surname: ____________________
2. Contact number: ____________________
3. Class start date: ____________________
4. Full discounted fee if paid upfront: £____________________
5. Item the caller must bring personally: ____________________

2. 0171 445 9823
3. 14th of March (14 March)

## Section 3 — Academic discussion between two students and a tutor
(Questions 6–10)

**Context:** Multiple speakers, academic topic — this section's main
difficulty is tracking *who* holds *which* opinion, since questions often
test whose view is whose, not just the content.

**Script (read aloud once):**

> **Tutor:** So you two were going to tell me about your progress on the
> renewable energy group project. Where are things at?
> **Student A (Priya):** We've decided to focus the case study on offshore
> wind rather than solar, mainly because the data available for our
> region is much more complete for wind.
> **Student B (Tom):** I actually pushed for solar at first, since I
> thought the installation cost trend was the more interesting story, but
> Priya's right that we just don't have enough regional data points to
> make a strong argument.
> **Tutor:** That's a fair reason to pivot. What's your main research
> question going to be?
> **Priya:** We want to look at whether community-owned wind projects
> produce different economic outcomes for local residents compared to
> corporately-owned ones.
> **Tom:** My worry is that "economic outcomes" is quite broad — we might
> need to narrow it to just job creation, or just local energy price
> effects, or we'll run out of word count trying to cover both.
> **Tutor:** I'd agree with Tom there. Pick one outcome measure and go
> deep rather than broad.
> **Priya:** Okay — let's commit to local energy price effects, then,
> since that's the data we already have partial access to.
> **Tom:** Agreed. And for the presentation, I'll handle the data analysis
> section since that's more my strength, and Priya can take the policy
> background section.

**Questions:**

6. Which energy source did the group ultimately choose to study?
   ____________________
7. Why did they reject the alternative energy source? ____________________
8. Whose idea was it originally to study the rejected energy source?
   ____________________
9. What specific economic outcome measure did they finally commit to?
   ____________________
10. Who will handle the data analysis section of the presentation?
    ____________________

<details>
<summary>Answer key (check only after attempting)</summary>

6. Offshore wind
7. Insufficient/incomplete regional data for their area
8. Tom
9. Local energy price effects
10. Tom

</details>

## Self-audit after scoring

- Which section cost you more — the numbers/spelling section or the
  multi-speaker opinion-tracking section? That tells you whether your
  weakness is detail-capture or speaker-attribution, and they need
  different fixes (the first needs faster writing while listening; the
  second needs active "who thinks what" note-taking, e.g. a two-column
  scratch note per speaker).
- Did you get the *exact* word/spelling right, or just the right idea?
  IELTS Listening marks spelling strictly — "Castelano" is wrong.
- Replay the transcript (reading it this time) and find the exact phrase
  that signaled each answer. If you missed a question, check whether the
  distractor before the real answer caught you out — Listening
  deliberately plants a plausible wrong answer just before the right one.

Log your score out of 10 and the specific failure mode (detail-capture vs.
speaker-attribution vs. distractor trap) in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
