# IELTS Listening — Drill Set 1

**Important limitation, read first:** IELTS Listening is an audio test,
and this file is text. Reading the transcript is not a substitute for
listening practice — it removes the exact skill (processing speech once,
in real time, while writing) that this section tests. Do it properly:

1. Open the **Transcript** section in a text-to-speech reader (phone
   "Speak Screen"/"Select to Speak," a browser TTS extension, or have
   someone read it aloud to you) set to a natural speaking pace.
2. Cover or close the transcript. Only look at the **Questions**.
3. Play the audio **once, start to finish, no pausing, no rewinding** —
   exactly like the real test. Answer as you go.
4. Only after finishing all questions for a section, reveal the
   transcript to self-check.

Doing this by reading the transcript first will not tell you anything
true about your actual Listening band — don't shortcut it.

## Section 3 — Academic Discussion (10 minutes, 8 questions)

Context: two students, Priya and Tom, discuss a research project with
their tutor.

### Questions 1–4: Multiple choice (choose ONE letter)

1. What is the main topic of Priya and Tom's research project?
   (A) The effect of background noise on memory
   (B) The effect of sleep on memory consolidation
   (C) The effect of caffeine on reaction time

2. What problem did the students encounter with their first round of
   data collection?
   (A) Too few participants volunteered
   (B) Some participants didn't follow the sleep instructions
   (C) The equipment malfunctioned

3. What does the tutor suggest they do about this problem?
   (A) Discard the affected data entirely
   (B) Add a self-report compliance question
   (C) Repeat the entire study with new participants

4. What is Tom initially worried about regarding the tutor's suggestion?
   (A) It will take too much additional time
   (B) Participants might not answer honestly
   (C) It won't be approved by the ethics committee

### Questions 5–8: Who makes the following points — Priya (P), Tom (T), or the Tutor (Tu)?

5. Suggests that self-reported compliance data is still useful even if
   imperfect. ______
6. Raises a concern about participants not answering honestly. ______
7. Proposes checking published studies for how they handled a similar
   issue. ______
8. Agrees to revise the method and resubmit it by the end of the week.
   ______

### Transcript

> **Tutor:** So, how's the memory consolidation project coming along?
>
> **Priya:** It's going reasonably well, but we ran into a snag with the
> first batch of data.
>
> **Tutor:** Oh? What happened?
>
> **Tom:** We asked participants to sleep for a full eight hours before
> the recall test, but when we followed up, it turned out quite a few of
> them hadn't actually managed that — some had five or six hours, but
> they'd still shown up and done the test anyway.
>
> **Tutor:** Ah, that's a common issue with sleep studies that rely on
> self-regulation rather than a lab setting. Did you have any way of
> verifying how much they'd actually slept?
>
> **Priya:** Not really, we just assumed they'd follow the instructions.
>
> **Tutor:** Right, well, going forward, I'd suggest adding a short
> self-report question at the start of the test session — just "how many
> hours did you sleep last night?" It won't be perfectly accurate, but
> it lets you at least filter or control for the variation statistically,
> rather than just hoping everyone complied.
>
> **Tom:** I suppose my worry is that people might round up, or just say
> eight hours because that's what they think we want to hear, even if it
> wasn't true.
>
> **Tutor:** That's a fair concern, and it's a known limitation of
> self-report — but it's still considerably better than having no data on
> it at all, and you can note the limitation explicitly in your
> discussion section. Plenty of published sleep studies rely on exactly
> this method when lab-based verification isn't feasible.
>
> **Priya:** That's reassuring, actually — maybe we should look at a
> couple of those published studies to see exactly how they phrased the
> question and handled the analysis.
>
> **Tutor:** Good idea, I'd do that before you finalise the wording.
>
> **Tom:** Okay, that makes sense. We can revise the method section and
> get it back to you.
>
> **Tutor:** When do you think you can have that done by?
>
> **Tom:** I think we can manage it by the end of this week.
>
> **Tutor:** Perfect, send it over once it's ready.

### Answer key

1. (B)
2. (B)
3. (B)
4. (B)
5. Tu (Tutor)
6. T (Tom)
7. P (Priya)
8. T (Tom)

### Scoring

Out of 18 total: 17–18 = Band 9 · 15–16 = Band 8 · 13–14 = Band 7 ·
11–12 = Band 6. Below 11: identify whether you missed because you
**mis-heard a specific word** (vocabulary/accent issue — re-listen to
that exact sentence) or because you **lost the thread of who said what**
(structure-tracking issue — practice noting speaker initials as you
listen, not after). These require different fixes; log which one in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
