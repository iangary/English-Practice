# IELTS Listening — Practice Set 1 (transcript-based)

**Honest caveat first:** this file cannot play audio. True IELTS Listening
practice requires hearing the recording once, at natural speed, with
accents and background noise — get that from the official Cambridge IELTS
practice test audio or the IELTS.org sample tests, on a regular rotation
alongside this file. What this drill *does* train, which text-only
materials often skip: single-exposure note-taking and question-type
recognition, which is half the actual skill. Use it to supplement real
audio, not replace it.

**Protocol:** Have someone read each transcript aloud to you once at
natural speaking pace (or use a text-to-speech tool set to ~150 words/min
— do not use a voice you can pause/rewind mid-read), while you look only
at the question sheet, not the transcript. Write answers *during* the
single read-through, exactly as the real test requires. Only reveal the
transcript text afterward, to check against the answer key.

## Section 3 — Academic discussion (multiple choice + matching)

**Transcript (read once):**

> **Tutor:** So, you two looked at renewable energy storage for your
> group project. Priya, what did you find?
> **Priya:** I focused on lithium-ion batteries, since they're the most
> widely deployed. The main issue isn't the technology itself, it's
> supply — lithium and cobalt mining is concentrated in a handful of
> countries, which creates a bottleneck as demand grows.
> **Tutor:** And Marcus, you looked at an alternative?
> **Marcus:** Pumped hydro storage — pumping water uphill when there's
> excess power, then releasing it through turbines later. It's actually
> the most widely used storage method globally by capacity, but it needs
> specific geography, so it can't just be built anywhere.
> **Tutor:** Which do you both think scales better over the next decade?
> **Priya:** I'd still bet on batteries, honestly, because new chemistries
> — sodium-ion, for instance — are emerging that don't depend on the same
> scarce materials. I think the supply issue gets solved before 2030.
> **Marcus:** I'm less convinced. Even sodium-ion batteries degrade after
> a few thousand cycles, whereas pumped hydro plants run for fifty years
> or more with minimal degradation. For long-duration, seasonal storage,
> I don't think batteries catch up in a decade.
> **Tutor:** Interesting — so you're actually disagreeing on the
> *timeframe* more than the technology itself.

**Questions 6–8:** Choose the correct letter, A, B, or C.

6. What does Priya identify as the main limitation of lithium-ion
   batteries?
   A. Their cost
   B. Their geographic flexibility
   C. The concentration of raw material supply
7. What does Marcus say is required for pumped hydro storage?
   A. A large upfront government subsidy
   B. Particular geographic conditions
   C. Rare raw materials
8. What do Priya and Marcus ultimately disagree about?
   A. Whether renewable storage is worthwhile at all
   B. The time horizon over which batteries become competitive
   C. Whether pumped hydro is more widely used today

**Questions 9–10:** Match each statement with the correct speaker.

A. Priya   B. Marcus

9. Believes an emerging technology will remove today's main constraint.
10. Points to long operational lifespan as a decisive advantage.

## Section 4 — Monologue, note completion

**Transcript (read once):**

> "Today I want to talk briefly about the history of the printing press
> and why its impact is so often overstated in one direction and
> understated in another. Gutenberg's press, developed around 1440, is
> usually credited with enabling mass literacy almost immediately — but
> that's not quite accurate. For the first several decades, books
> remained expensive, and literacy rates in most of Europe rose only
> gradually, over roughly a century, not overnight. What the press *did*
> do immediately was standardize texts — before print, every hand-copied
> manuscript contained small variations and copying errors, but printed
> editions were, for the first time, identical across thousands of
> copies. This standardization had a huge effect on fields like science
> and law, where consistency of a shared reference text matters
> enormously. The underestimated impact, meanwhile, is the press's role
> in enabling the Reformation: Martin Luther's writings spread across
> Germany within weeks of printing, something simply impossible with
> hand-copied texts, and many historians now argue the Reformation's
> speed, not just its ideas, depended directly on this new technology."

**Questions 11–13:** Complete the notes. Write **NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS**
for each answer.

- Mass literacy rose gradually, over about a __(11)__, not immediately.
- The press's most immediate effect was __(12)__ of texts across copies.
- Luther's writings spread across Germany within weeks due to print,
  which is argued to have determined the __(13)__ of the Reformation.

### Scoring

12–13/13 = Band 8–9 pace · 9–11 = Band 7 · 7–8 = Band 6 · below 7: note
whether you missed the answer because you mis-heard/mis-read a specific
word, or because you lost the thread of the conversation — they need
different fixes (vocabulary/numbers drilling vs. structure-tracking
practice). Log the result in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`, and schedule a real
official-audio practice test within the same week — this file is a
