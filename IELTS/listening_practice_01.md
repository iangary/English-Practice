# IELTS Listening — Practice Set 1

**This repo is text-based, so there's no audio file to play.** That is not
an excuse to skip Listening — it's the single most neglected section by
self-studiers, and it shows up as a plateaued band score. Use one of these
three methods, in order of preference:

1. **Best:** Paste the script below into a text-to-speech tool or your
   phone's "read aloud" accessibility feature, set it to a natural
   (not slowed-down) speaking rate, and play it **once only**, exactly as
   the real test does.
2. **Good:** Have a study partner read it aloud at natural pace, once.
3. **Minimum viable (last resort only):** Read it yourself silently at a
   fixed pace (roughly 150 words/minute — use a stopwatch) exactly once,
   covering the questions while you "listen." This trains timing and
   note-taking discipline even though it can't train your ear — if this is
   your only option, prioritize real audio (podcasts, YouTube interviews,
   BBC/VOA) on your non-drill days to actually train listening
   comprehension, which this text method cannot do.

**Timer: do not pause, rewind, or re-read. You get one pass, exactly like
exam day. Transfer answers within 2 minutes after the audio ends, exactly
as the real test's transfer time works.**

## Section 1 — Form completion (everyday conversation)

> **Receptionist:** Good morning, Thompson Language Academy, how can I
> help?
> **Caller:** Hi, I'd like to enroll in an evening course. Can you tell me
> what's available?
> **Receptionist:** Sure. We've got Spanish, French, and Mandarin running
> this term, all evenings from six to eight.
> **Caller:** I'll go with Spanish. What do I need to do to sign up?
> **Receptionist:** I'll just need a few details. Can I get your full name?
> **Caller:** It's Daniel Petrovic — that's P-E-T-R-O-V-I-C.
> **Receptionist:** Great, and a contact number?
> **Caller:** 0781 445 920.
> **Receptionist:** And do you have any previous experience with Spanish?
> **Caller:** A little — I did it for two years in school, but that was
> over a decade ago, so I'd say I'm a false beginner, really.
> **Receptionist:** No problem, that actually fits our "Elementary Plus"
> group better than complete beginners. That class meets on Tuesdays and
> Thursdays, starting the 14th of October. The fee is 180 pounds for the
> ten-week term, and there's a one-off 15 pound materials charge for the
> course textbook.
> **Caller:** Great, I'll take it. Can I pay in installments?
> **Receptionist:** Yes, we allow two installments — half at enrollment,
> half by week five. Oh, and one more thing — the materials fee has to be
> paid in cash at reception, we can't take that part by card.

## Section 3 — Multiple choice + matching (academic discussion, two speakers)

> **Tutor:** So, Priya, how's the research going for your group project on
> urban beekeeping?
> **Priya:** It's going well, but my partner Tom and I disagree on the
> focus. I want to concentrate on the economic benefits — honey sales,
> pollination services for nearby allotments — but Tom thinks we should
> focus entirely on biodiversity impacts.
> **Tutor:** Those aren't mutually exclusive, you know. Have you
> considered structuring it so one is the main argument and the other is
> a supporting point?
> **Priya:** That's actually a good idea. I hadn't thought of subordinating
> one to the other rather than treating them as competing options.
> **Tutor:** Right. Now, in terms of sources — have you found enough
> primary data, or are you relying mostly on secondary literature?
> **Priya:** Mostly secondary so far. We did try to contact three local
> beekeeping associations for interviews, but only one got back to us.
> **Tutor:** That's fairly normal for a project this size — one solid
> primary interview plus good secondary sources is perfectly acceptable,
> as long as you're upfront in your methodology about that limitation
> rather than overstating how representative the interview is.
> **Priya:** Good, that's a relief. One more thing — our deadline is the
> 20th, but we were wondering if an extension is possible since Tom's been
> unwell.
> **Tutor:** For a documented illness, yes, you can apply for up to a
> five-day extension through the student portal — but you need supporting
> documentation, and it has to be submitted at least 48 hours before the
> original deadline, not after.

### Questions 8–10 — Multiple choice

**8.** What does the tutor suggest about Priya and Tom's disagreement?
(A) They should split into two separate projects
(B) One focus could support the other rather than compete with it
(C) They should drop the biodiversity angle entirely
(D) They should ask a different tutor to decide

**9.** How many beekeeping associations actually responded to the
students?
(A) None (B) One (C) Two (D) Three

**10.** What does the tutor say about relying mainly on secondary sources?
(A) It is unacceptable for this assignment
(B) It is fine only if the limitation is acknowledged in the methodology
(C) It will automatically lower their grade
(D) It is preferred over primary interviews

### Answer key

## Self-audit after each section

- Did you lose your place when a speaker corrected themselves or changed
  topic mid-sentence (as the receptionist and the tutor both do above)? IELTS
  Listening deliberately includes false starts and self-corrections — the
  *first* number/answer mentioned is often changed moments later, and the
  final, corrected version is what's scored.
- For form completion: did you respect the word/number limit exactly?
  Writing "zero eight one" instead of "081" when numbers are allowed, or
  adding an extra word past the stated limit, counts as wrong even if the
  content is right.
- For multiple choice: did you fall for an answer that was mentioned in
  the audio but then explicitly corrected or ruled out (like the "two
  options as competing" framing that the tutor explicitly reframes)?

Log your score out of 12 and the single biggest issue (mishearing a
number, losing the thread after a false start, missing a correction) in
`PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
