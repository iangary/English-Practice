# IELTS Listening — Practice Set 1

**No audio file is provided here — IELTS Listening format is simulated as
a cold read.** Protocol: read each transcript below **exactly once, at a
normal speaking pace, out loud if possible** (or have someone else read it
to you) while you fill in answers in real time, exactly as you would hear
it once on exam day. Do not re-read before answering. This trains the
actual skill tested — capturing information on a single pass — far better
than silent re-readable text would. If you have text-to-speech available,
pasting the transcript into it at natural speed is even closer to the
real test.

**Timer:** Allow yourself only the time it takes to read each transcript
once, plus the stated per-section time to finalize answers — no going
back to "listen" again.

## Section 1 — Everyday conversation (form completion)

**Context:** A customer is enquiring about a gym membership on the phone.

> **Receptionist:** Good morning, Riverside Fitness, how can I help?
> **Customer:** Hi, I'd like to ask about membership options. I'm
> interested in joining but I'm not sure which plan suits me.
> **Receptionist:** Sure. We have three main plans. The Basic plan is
> forty-five pounds a month and includes gym floor access only, no
> classes. The Standard plan is sixty-two pounds a month and adds group
> classes like yoga and spinning. The Premium plan is eighty-nine pounds
> a month and includes classes plus unlimited use of the pool and sauna.
> **Customer:** I think Standard sounds right for me. Do I need to pay a
> joining fee?
> **Receptionist:** Normally yes, it's twenty-five pounds, but we're
> currently waiving that if you sign up before the end of the month.
> **Customer:** Great, I'll do that. What do I need to bring on my first
> visit?
> **Receptionist:** Just a form of photo ID, and we'll take a quick photo
> for your membership card. Oh, and wear closed-toe shoes — we can't let
> you on the gym floor in sandals.
> **Customer:** Noted. And what are your opening hours?
> **Receptionist:** We're open six a.m. to ten p.m. on weekdays, and eight
> a.m. to six p.m. on weekends.
> **Customer:** Perfect. Can I book my induction session for this
> Thursday?
> **Receptionist:** Let me check... yes, we have a slot at four-thirty
> p.m. with our trainer, Marcus. Does that work?
> **Customer:** That works. Thanks very much.

## Section 3 — Academic discussion (multiple choice + short answer)

**Context:** Two university students, Priya and Tom, discuss a group
project on renewable energy with their supervisor.

> **Supervisor:** So, tell me where you've got to with the wind energy
> case study.
> **Priya:** We've finished the literature review, but we're having
> trouble deciding which case study site to focus on. I wanted to look at
> an offshore wind farm, but Tom thinks onshore would be easier to find
> data for.
> **Tom:** Right, the thing is, offshore data tends to be held by private
> energy companies and a lot of it isn't publicly available. Onshore
> farms, especially the ones run with government involvement, publish
> much more detailed performance data.
> **Supervisor:** That's a fair practical concern. But Priya, why did you
> want offshore specifically?
> **Priya:** Offshore wind farms generally have higher and more
> consistent wind speeds, so the efficiency data is more interesting —
> there's less noise from turbulence that you'd get on land near
> buildings or hills.
> **Supervisor:** Both valid points. Here's a suggestion: could you use
> an onshore site for your primary data analysis, since the access
> problem is real, but include a short comparative section using published
> aggregate offshore statistics — not site-specific data — to address the
> efficiency angle Priya's interested in?
> **Tom:** That could work. We'd still get the deeper analysis from
> onshore data but keep the offshore comparison for context.
> **Priya:** I'm happy with that as a compromise.
> **Supervisor:** Good. One more thing — your deadline is the 14th, not
> the 21st as you may have seen on the old syllabus; that got corrected
> last week.
> **Tom:** Good to know, thanks — we'd have missed that.

**Questions 7–10:**

### Scoring
9–10/10 = Band 8+ pace · 7–8 = Band 7 · 5–6 = Band 6 · below 5: your
issue is very likely **not vocabulary** — it's losing your place after a
distractor (a plausible-sounding wrong detail mentioned before the real
answer, e.g., the "21st" deadline mentioned and then corrected). Re-read
the transcript afterward and mark every place a detail was stated and
then changed or qualified — that's where real IELTS Listening loses
people, not obscure words.

### Why single-pass reading matters here
If you got questions wrong that you could clearly answer on a second
read, that gap **is the score** — IELTS Listening never allows a second
pass, so the gap between your single-pass and multi-pass performance is
exactly the number of band points you're leaving on the table. Drill this
set again in a week without looking at the transcript first, to see if
the gap shrinks.
