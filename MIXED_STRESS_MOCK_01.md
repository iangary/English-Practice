# Mixed Stress Mock 1 — Cross-exam, no break, single sitting

This is the Weeks 5–6 "combine skills under fatigue" drill from
`STUDY_PLAN.md`, built out as an actual timed session. Do not attempt
this until you've completed at least Set 1 of the relevant materials
for each section below — this mock exists to test whether skills hold
up under fatigue and context-switching, not to teach them for the first
time.

**Total time: 67 minutes, one sitting, phone away, no pausing between
sections.** The whole point is that section 3 happens while you're
already tired from sections 1 and 2 — that fatigue is realistic test-day
conditions, not an unfair condition to remove.

## Section 1 — GRE Reading Comprehension (8 minutes)

Use `GRE/reading_comprehension_set2.md` (see below) if you haven't done
it yet this week; otherwise use any not-yet-reviewed passage from
`GRE/reading_comprehension_practice.md`. Strict 8-minute timer including
answering all questions. Do not check answers yet — move straight to
Section 2 the moment the timer ends, even mid-sentence if needed.

## Section 2 — GRE Quant, 6 questions (12 minutes)

Pick any 6 unreviewed questions from `GRE/quant_drills.md` or
`GRE/quant_drills_set2.md`. 2 minutes/question, 12 minutes total. Again:
stop the instant the timer ends and move on, scored or not.

## Section 3 — IELTS Writing Task 2, same theme as Section 1's passage (40 minutes)

This is the actual point of the mock. Pick (or write) an IELTS Task 2
prompt on a theme related to whatever Section 1's passage was about
(economics passage → write on an economics-adjacent prompt; a science
passage → a technology/science-adjacent prompt). Full 40-minute
protocol from `IELTS/writing_task2.md`, done immediately after Sections
1–2 with no rest in between.

**What to actually watch for while grading this one:** compare this
essay's quality against a Task 2 essay you wrote fresh, not fatigued.
A specific, honest question to answer in your log: did your thesis stay
just as sharp, or did you fall back on vaguer, safer phrasing because
you were tired? That drop — if it's there — is exactly what a real exam
day with multiple sections back-to-back will expose, and it's better to
find it here than on test day.

## Honest comparison to run once you have 2+ runs of this mock logged

Compare your IELTS Task 2 band on this mock against your average
*fresh* Task 2 band from `writing_task2.md`/`writing_task2_set2.md`. A
drop of more than half a band under fatigue means your Task 2 process
relies too heavily on being well-rested — which the real exam will not
guarantee if IELTS isn't your only test that week. Fix: run this mock
more often, specifically to build fatigue-resistance into the process
itself, not just to re-confirm the gap exists.
