## Section 1 — GRE Verbal, cold (11 minutes)

### 1a. New vocabulary, first exposure (4 min)

but don't expect to. Read each once, then immediately cover the
definitions and see how many you can reconstruct from the example

### 1b. Text Completion, no prep (4 min — two sets, ~2 min each)

State the logical signal (continue vs. reverse) before you look at
choices. Write your answer before checking — no changing it after.

**1 (two blanks).** Although colleagues described him as (i)______ in
meetings, rarely raising his voice even when challenged directly, his
written memos were often strikingly (ii)______, attacking proposals in
language far sharper than anything he said aloud.

Blank (i): (A) phlegmatic (B) garrulous (C) sycophantic
Blank (ii): (A) perfunctory (B) vitriolic (C) sanguine

**2 (three blanks).** The negotiator's reputation for (i)______ meant
both sides trusted almost nothing she said at the table; years of
(ii)______ answers to direct questions had taught them that a clear
"yes" from her was, if anything, a signal to look for the (iii)______
condition buried in the fine print.

Blank (i): (A) candor (B) equivocation (C) perspicacity
Blank (ii): (A) cogent (B) evasive (C) ebullient
Blank (iii): (A) obvious (B) hidden (C) favorable

<details><summary>Answer key (don't peek before answering)</summary>

1. (i) A — phlegmatic; (ii) B — vitriolic. Signal: "Although" = contrast
between spoken calm and written sharpness.
2. (i) B — equivocation; (ii) B — evasive; (iii) B — hidden. The whole
sentence is one consistent idea (distrust of her directness), not a
contrast — every blank should reinforce "you can't take her words at
face value."

</details>

### 1c. Quant speed round (3 min, no calculator beyond what the real
test allows — i.e., the on-screen basic calculator only, if you're
simulating it)

1. If 3x − 7 = 2x + 5, what is x?
2. A store marks up an item 25% then offers a 20% discount on the
   marked-up price. Is the final price higher, lower, or equal to the
   original price? By what percent?
3. What is the probability of rolling a sum of 8 with two standard
   six-sided dice?
4. If the ratio of boys to girls in a class is 3:5 and there are 32
   students total, how many girls are there?
5. Quantitative Comparison: Column A: the area of a circle with radius 4.
   Column B: the area of a square with side 7. Which is greater?

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. x = 12
2. Lower, by 5% (1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00 → actually equal — check your work:
   1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00, so it's **equal**. If you answered "lower," you
   made exactly the sign error this drill exists to catch.)
3. 5/36
4. 20 girls (3:5 → 8 parts = 32 students → 4 per part → 5×4 = 20)
5. Column A = 16π ≈ 50.3. Column B = 49. **Column A is greater.**

</details>

## Section 2 — IELTS Writing Task 2 (40 minutes, hard stop)

This prompt is deliberately more abstract than the Set 1 prompts in
`IELTS/writing_task2.md` — abstract, future-facing prompts are where
Band 6 writers stall because they can't find concrete examples fast.
That's exactly the muscle a baseline needs to test.

> Some people believe that artificial intelligence will soon be capable
> of performing most jobs currently done by humans, making many forms of
> traditional higher education unnecessary. To what extent do you agree
> or disagree?

Self-grade against the rubric in `IELTS/writing_task2.md` (Task
Response, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range &
Accuracy). Note specifically: did you commit to a clear position on
"to what extent," or did you hedge into "it depends" without ever
landing anywhere? Hedging without a stated position caps Task Response
at Band 6 regardless of how balanced your reasoning sounds.

## Section 3 — TOEFL Integrated Writing (20 minutes, 150–225 words)

Read once (3 min), "listen" to the lecture once, no re-reading, write
for the rest.

**Reading passage:**

> Vertical farming — growing crops in stacked, indoor, climate-controlled
> layers rather than in open fields — has been proposed as a solution to
> urban food shortages. Advocates point to three advantages. First,
> vertical farms can be built inside cities, drastically cutting the
> transportation distance between harvest and consumer. Second, because
> conditions are fully controlled, crops can be grown year-round
> regardless of outside weather or season. Third, vertical farms use a
> fraction of the land area of traditional farming for the same crop
> yield, since growing space is stacked vertically rather than spread
> horizontally.

**Lecture transcript (read once):**

> "The pitch sounds compelling, but the economics tell a different
> story once you look past the surface. First, yes, transportation
> distance shrinks, but the energy cost of replacing free sunlight with
> artificial grow lights around the clock is enormous — in most studies,
> the carbon and cost savings from shorter transport are wiped out
> several times over by electricity use. Second, 'year-round regardless
> of season' is true, but it ignores that vertical farms have so far
> proven commercially viable only for a narrow set of crops — leafy
> greens and herbs mostly — because grains and other staples simply
> don't yield enough value per square foot of expensive indoor space to
> justify the setup. Third, the land-use savings are real, but they
> don't account for the specialized construction, cooling, and lighting
> infrastructure required, which makes the upfront capital cost per unit
> of food produced far higher than it looks when you only compare land
> area."

**Your task:** Summarize the lecture's points and explain how they cast
doubt on specific claims in the reading passage.
