# 10-Week Intensive Plan

Assume 90–120 minutes/day, 6 days/week. If you have less time, cut volume,
never cut the timer and never cut the weekly mock. Rest day is day 7, and
even then you owe 20 minutes of vocab.

## Week 0 — Baseline (do this before anything else)

Take one full timed mock of each exam's weakest-for-you section this week
(you don't need all three exams' full mocks — just enough to know where you
stand). Log every score, with date, in `PROGRESS_LOG.md`. No studying
"around" this — if you don't know your baseline, every later number is
meaningless.

## Weeks 1–2 — Foundations, no shortcuts

- **Daily:** 30 GRE vocab words from `GRE/verbal_vocab.md` (new + review),
  drilled both directions (word→meaning, meaning→word). Once Set 1 is
  fully mastered (both directions, two different days), move on to
  `GRE/verbal_vocab_set2.md` — don't stack an unlearned new set on top
  of an unfinished one.
- **Mon/Fri:** One IELTS Writing Task 2 essay, 40 minutes, no outline
  prepared in advance. Self-grade against `IELTS/writing_task2.md`'s rubric.
- **Wed:** One IELTS Writing Task 1 report, 20 minutes, from
  `IELTS/writing_task1.md` — rotate chart types (line, bar, pie, table,
  process, map) so you're not only ever practicing the easiest one.
- **Tue/Thu:** One GRE Text Completion set + one Reading Comprehension
  passage, both timed, from `GRE/text_completion_practice.md` and
  `GRE/reading_comprehension_practice.md`.
- **Sat:** TOEFL Independent + Integrated Writing, back to back, full time
  pressure (30 min + 20 min), from `TOEFL/writing_tasks.md`.
- **Sun (light day, still counts):** One IELTS or TOEFL listening set,
  alternating weekly, from `IELTS/listening_practice.md` or
  `TOEFL/listening_tasks.md` — use the "listen/read once" protocol in
  the file, no re-reading the transcript before answering.
- **Weekly mock:** One full IELTS Academic Reading passage set, strictly
  timed to 20 minutes including transfer of answers.

## Weeks 3–4 — Speed and precision

- Cut your essay planning time by 5 minutes from week 1–2's pace. If your
  score drops, you were relying on planning time as a crutch — fix the
  underlying structure instead of restoring the time.
- Add GRE Quant drills (`GRE/quant_drills.md`) 4x/week, strictly timed
  (1:45/question average, no calculator use beyond what the real test
  allows).
- Add IELTS/TOEFL Speaking: record yourself answering 3 cue
  cards/prompts per session, play it back, transcribe your own filler
  words (really) and count them.
- Bump listening to 2x/week (both `IELTS/listening_practice.md` and
  `TOEFL/listening_tasks.md` once each) — listening is the section most
  people quietly under-train because it "feels" passive.
- **Weekly mock:** Full TOEFL Reading section (3 passages, 54 minutes).

## Weeks 5–6 — Stress-test weaknesses

- Identify your two lowest-scoring drill types from `PROGRESS_LOG.md`.
  Double their frequency this fortnight. Everything else drops to
  maintenance (half frequency).
- Start combining skills: do a GRE Reading passage, then immediately an
  IELTS Writing Task 2 on a related theme — context-switching under
  fatigue is what the real test day actually demands.
- **Weekly mock, Week 5:** Full GRE Verbal section (2 sections, 41 min total).
- **Weekly mock, Week 6:** Full IELTS Writing (Task 1 + Task 2, 60 min).

## Weeks 7–8 — Full-length mocks, every week

- Alternate full official-length practice tests: one complete IELTS mock,
  one complete TOEFL mock, one complete GRE mock (General Test timing) —
  spread across the two weeks, each under real conditions (single sitting,
  timed breaks only where the real exam allows them).
- Daily vocab review continues — do not drop it even during mock weeks.
  This is exactly when people quietly let vocab slide and then plateau.
- After each mock: a brutally honest post-mortem in `PROGRESS_LOG.md` —
  not just the score, but *where* time was lost and *which* question types
  you got wrong for the same reason twice.

## Weeks 9–10 — Peak and taper

- Week 9: one more full mock per exam you're actually sitting, plus
  targeted drilling only on your remaining weak points.
- Week 10: taper volume by ~40%, keep intensity (timed, scored) the same.
  Last 2 days before any real exam: light review only, no new material,
  sleep on schedule. Cramming the night before a 3-hour exam is a net
  negative — your brain needs the recovery more than the extra pass.

## Standing rule for all 10 weeks

Every Sunday, before anything else: review `PROGRESS_LOG.md` for the week,
write one sentence on what specifically goes wrong when you miss a point
(not "I need to review more" — e.g. "I mis-time Task 2 intros, averaging
9 minutes when I should cap at 5"). Vague diagnoses produce vague fixes.
